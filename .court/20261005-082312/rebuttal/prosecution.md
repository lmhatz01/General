# Prosecution rebuttal

1. **Partly survives.** The shared engineering core is real, but "every route is the same" is false. The file says the double degree "may reduce WAM, time for Monash Formula SAE, and depth of technical engineering skill." Waiting is not free: the double degree and the longer timeline are choices made now, and they carry costs and HECS-HELP debt. The record also gives no evidence that the choice gets easier at university.

2. **Does not survive as evidence.** A list of skills Lukas wants to build, plus one elective he looked up, shows interest, not tested fit. The record has no work experience, no Formula SAE time, no results. "Consistent" is the defense's word. The file shows only a Year 12 student's stated interest. This also leaves the charge standing, since the case asks about a career, not a degree.

3. **Survives, but is weak.** Maths Methods and Physics fit engineering. That says nothing about motorsport versus finance. Economics and Product Design are consistent with either, so they cannot settle the question.

4. **Turns against the defense.** Naming a trade-off is not resolving it. The record lists three options (three units per semester, HECS-HELP, a minor) and never says which Lukas would choose, or what WAM he needs for either career. The file's claim that Formula SAE is what "motorsport employers look for" appears in the defense, not in the record.

5. **Concession cuts deeper than stated.** Salary, location and willingness to move overseas are missing. Those are the inputs the career question turns on, so the question cannot be answered without them.

**The point for the jury:** point 1, whether the choice can safely wait. If the double degree measurably costs WAM and Formula SAE time, "keeping both open" weakens the motorsport path he calls primary.
