## Charges that stuck
- The missing facts on salary, location and willingness to move overseas leave the career question open (11 jurors).
- The double degree might cut his WAM and Formula SAE time, which his main motorsport goal depends on (6 jurors).
- The double degree means extra years and HECS-HELP debt (4 jurors).

## What the defense got right
- Every route starts with the same Monash Mechanical Engineering degree, so the motorsport-or-finance choice can wait and the missing facts only make advice conditional.
- His interest is specific and researched: named skills, and electives like MMA3101 already looked up.

## Conditions for acquittal
1. Say plainly whether you would move overseas for a motorsport job. About six jurors named this as the thing that could flip their vote.
2. Find out from Monash whether Commerce can be added or dropped after first year, and what a three-units-per-semester load would do to your WAM and Formula SAE time. Write down the answer.
3. Restate the question as "Mechanical Engineering alone, or with Commerce (Finance), given I want F1 engineering?" The jurors say this is the real decision.

## The sentence
The court finds the plan sound enough to proceed. Start Mechanical Engineering and keep Commerce as an option, since the choice does not have to be made yet. This week, settle the three items above. Then ask the Monash course advisers the double-degree question directly. Do not commit to the double degree until you have those answers.
