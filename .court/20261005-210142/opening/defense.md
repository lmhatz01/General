# Defense opening

The record gives no outcome data, so I defend the claim on its logic and on how Lukas framed it. The claim is a modest one: the degree is "versatile for many industries". It does not promise a guaranteed entry into any of them.

1. **The claim matches his primary goal.** The file says: "He is interested in motorsport/F1 engineering (mechanical engineering) as the primary goal." Mechanical Engineering at Monash is the direct route to that goal, so the claim holds for motorsport on the case's own terms. The obvious objection is that the claim is too general. It is general only because Lukas has two goals, and engineering serves the main one.

2. **The degree stays useful if the plan changes.** The file says finance is "secondary" and asks "whether engineering alone provides a pathway into finance later." The claim answers yes: finance and consulting are named as reachable. The objection is that there is no evidence for this. That is true, and I concede it. But a Year 12 student can reasonably go ahead with a broad degree that keeps options open and does not close any off. That is what "versatile" means.

3. **The decision does not depend on the claim being right everywhere.** The options listed are "Engineering (Mechanical) alone", the double degree, and "Engineering with finance electives". At least two of these are engineering-first. If the claim is only partly true, he can add commerce subjects or electives later or alongside. Because the claim leaves the finance route open and does not rely on it, the case can go ahead.

4. **Technical depth and Formula SAE time are protected by the engineering-first path.** He wants to "develop CAD, manufacturing, vehicle dynamics" and be "heavily involved in Monash Formula SAE." His own worry is that a double degree might "limit extracurricular/Formula SAE time" and "reduce technical depth". The claim supports the single-degree or electives route, which avoids both risks and still keeps finance reachable. The objection is that a Commerce component might help a finance move more. The record does not say that it would.

**Conceded:** nothing in the file shows that Monash specifically beats other schools, or that engineers actually move into finance or consulting. The words "top school" are asserted, not shown.
