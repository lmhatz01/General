# Prosecution opening

**1. The hedge is unproven, and the plan now depends on it entirely.** With switching ruled out, the single degree is irreversible. Its finance fallback rests on a section the review labels "judgement, NOT verified against sources." The review also says F1 is "low probability from any pathway." If the main goal misses, the fallback is what Lukas is left with. Investment banking is rated "Hard without Commerce signalling." Cost: a locked-in degree whose only safety net was never tested.

**2. The A+ plan overloads the years where the record says WAM is at risk.** It puts "3-4 commerce/finance units in Year 1-2 free slots." The record gives Year 1 breadth, a Year 1 elective and "2 in Year 2": about four slots. Those four are the same years as the foundation-maths risk ("Maths is the WAM risk in first year"). On top of that come FSAE at "15-20+ hours a week" that "tends to expand to fill time," plus a finance society role, a modelling project and internships. McLaren requires "minimum 2:1 or equivalent." Cost: a lower WAM, which is the one thing both F1 employers ask for.

**3. Those free slots are spent on the wrong skills.** The record says Monash Motorsport "has moved from combustion to autonomous and electric vehicles," and that the relevant skills are "controls, electronics, data and simulation." Using the early slots on commerce leaves none for those skills. Technical electives are what the single degree offers over the double, yet they get spent on the backup career. Cost: the single's main advantage is wasted, and Lukas arrives at FSAE without the skills it uses.

**4. The comparison has too few options and too little verification.** Only E3001 and E3005 are compared, and no degree built around electronics or controls is considered. Unverified items include "FSAE hours count toward the 420 CPD hours," "current Monash Motorsport first-year joining rules," CSP effects, and MMA3101 availability. The course maps are "pending finalisation – subject to change." The salary loss of "well over $150k" is labelled "rough, unsourced." Cost: the verdict rests on numbers and rules that have not been checked.

**5. The review's advice to start single and "keep the double only if those checks come back in its favour" no longer works.** That advice assumed Lukas could switch later, and the retrial facts remove that option. Cost: the recommendation as written cannot be followed.
