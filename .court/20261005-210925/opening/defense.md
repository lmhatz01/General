# Defense opening

The claim is modest: an engineering degree from a top school is *versatile*. It holds up, and Lukas should go ahead on the plan as he described it.

1. **The claim fits his actual plan.** The file says his "primary goal: motorsport/F1 engineering, specifically mechanical engineering," with finance/business as a "secondary pathway." A degree that serves the main goal and keeps the second open is the claim's point. The objection is that finance and consulting may need a commerce credential. But he asks whether "engineering alone provides a pathway into finance later," and the record has no evidence that it can't. He is also weighing "engineering with finance electives," which is a cheap hedge.

2. **The degree is built for his main goal.** He names "Monash Formula SAE, CAD, vehicle dynamics, manufacturing and practical experience." These are core mechanical-engineering activities, and the team is at the university he is considering. The objection is that a top school's name does little for F1. It is the hands-on work that counts, and Mechanical alone leaves the most time for it.

3. **His worry about the double degree is a reason to pick a path, not a flaw in the claim.** He fears a double degree could hurt his "WAM," his "time to Monash Formula SAE," and his "deep technical skills." The claim does not say he must take the double degree. Versatility means he can choose Mechanical alone and still keep finance open. That makes the claim stronger.

4. **Choosing late is itself versatility.** He is a Year 12 student. A broad engineering degree lets him keep both goals open without committing to either yet.

**Concessions.** The file has no data on graduate outcomes, such as how many Monash engineers enter finance or consulting, or on how recruiters treat engineering-only applicants. It does not show that "top school" matters more than the course or experience. I cannot defend those points from the record. The wording "from motorsports to even finance" is also loose, since motorsport roles are far more competitive than the claim suggests.
