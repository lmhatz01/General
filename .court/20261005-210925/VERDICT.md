# The verdict: GUILTY, 10 to 2

**The case:** Claim: "An engineering degree from a top school like Monash University is versatile for many industries, from motorsports to even finance and consulting."

**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## How the jury voted

| Juror | Vote | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Accountant | GUILTY | As an accountant I see a decision that costs years of tuition and time (single versus double degree) being supported by a claim with no graduate-outcome, salary or hiring figure behind it, so it cannot be relied on as stated. |
| The First-Time Customer | GUILTY | As someone hearing this for the first time, I would not act on a reassurance that gives me no evidence for finance or consulting and doesn't help me choose between the three degree options I'm actually weighing. |
| The Competitor | GUILTY | As a rival who would have to beat this offer, I see a prestige slogan with no outcomes data behind "even finance and consulting", and I would happily compete against a Commerce double degree or a hands-on Formula SAE record while this claim sits unproven. |

## Charges that stuck
- No evidence that engineering-only graduates actually enter finance or consulting (no outcomes, recruiter or alumni data): 10 jurors.
- It never says which of Lukas's three options (Mechanical alone, double degree, electives) to take: 5 jurors.
- It reads as a generic prestige line: 3 jurors.

## What the defense got right
- The claim never tells Lukas to take the double degree. Mechanical alone, with finance electives as a cheap hedge, fits his main goal and keeps finance open.
- Lacking proof is not the same as being wrong. The record shows the claim is unproven, not that engineering fails to lead into finance.

## Conditions for acquittal
1. Find real Monash graduate-outcome figures or named alumni showing mechanical engineers, without Commerce, entering finance, consulting and motorsport roles.
2. Check what finance and consulting graduate programs require of engineering-only applicants (Commerce, WAM cut-offs, electives), and what adding Commerce costs in years and fees.
3. Reword the claim modestly: engineering keeps finance open, a Commerce component or high WAM may be needed, and Mechanical suits the motorsport-first goal.

## The sentence
Lukas, the claim is not thrown out, but it cannot be your basis for enrolling as it stands. This week, gather the outcome data, ask Monash and a few employers what engineering-only applicants need, and rewrite the claim as a premise you test, not a promise. Meanwhile, Mechanical with finance electives costs little.
