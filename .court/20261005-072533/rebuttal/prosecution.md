PROSECUTION REBUTTAL

1. Goals match. Partly survives. The defense concedes Mech alone is F1-optimal and calls electives or a minor "a real alternative" while admitting the record has no evidence the full degree signals more. A fallback is bought at the cost of the top-ranked goal, and the defense offers no evidence the price is worth it.

2. FSAE plan. Does not survive. Joining in year 2 answers only when he starts. It does not answer the time cost once he is in, which is the load of a full second degree on top of FSAE. The plan is his stated intention, not a tested one.

3. Option C as mitigation. Weak. Six years at 3 units delays income and adds HECS accrual and opportunity cost. The defense dismisses this because fees are "not the main concern", but that covers upfront fees only. Option C also does not add elective slots, so it protects WAM and time, not technical depth.

4. Candour and informed choice. Does not survive as a defense of the plan. Naming risks is not mitigating them. The defense admits the key facts (MMA3101 access, additional-unit rules, commencement-year rules, maximum duration) are unverified. The decision is stated as already made ("I chose") on unchecked foundations.

Concession. The defense's own words admit that if CFD depth is blocked, the technical case weakens. That is the fatal dependency, and it is unresolved.

The point for the jury: whether a decision framed as chosen can stand when its central technical premise (access to CFD and similar depth inside the double degree) is unverified, and when the cheaper alternative, Mech plus finance electives or a minor, is conceded by the defense to be live.
