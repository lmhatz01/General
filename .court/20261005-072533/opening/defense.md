DEFENSE: the double degree (Option B/C) should go ahead as described.

1. The choice matches his actual goals, not just F1. The case says "He does NOT want to optimise purely for F1" and wants "finance/business/consulting options kept open if F1 does not work out" plus "long-term earning potential". Mech alone is the F1-optimal path, but he explicitly ranks fallbacks and earnings. The double degree is the only option that builds a formal finance credential, which he himself concedes engineering alone lacks: it "does not automatically make him competitive for elite finance (IB, PE, VC)". Objection: electives or a minor could do this. Those are a real alternative (his own), but the record has no evidence they carry equal signalling weight.

2. He has already planned around the FSAE risk. "He understands he can join in second year", and plans year 1 for CAD, Python/MATLAB and practical work before "heavy FSAE involvement from year 2." This meets the objection of "less FSAE time" head on.

3. Option C is a deliberate mitigation, not a naive load. Spreading over "about 6 years at roughly 3 units per semester" is designed "to leave time for FSAE, internships, projects and a stronger WAM". That targets the WAM, internship and burnout risks he lists. The objection of delayed income and HECS accrual is softened by his statement that upfront fees "are not the main concern" (CSP).

4. He is well informed and candid about the costs. He names the risks (lower WAM, restricted electives, delayed income) and flags unverified items, including MMA3101 availability and commencement-year rules ("do not assume the 2026 handbook applies"). A decision made with its downsides in view, and with checks still to run, is a sound one.

Concession: the elective restriction is real by his own account, and fewer units per semester "does not create extra elective slots". CFD access is unverified, and the record gives no Monash rule to confirm or refute it. If MMA3101 or similar depth is blocked, the technical-depth case for B/C weakens. That is a check to do before committing, not a reason to reject the plan.

Missing from the record: any verified Monash handbook rules (maximum duration, reduced load, additional units, HECS limits), and any data on F1 hiring or finance-entry outcomes by path.
