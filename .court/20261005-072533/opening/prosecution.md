# Prosecution opening

1. **The choice rests on unverified rules.** The file says he "has not verified whether it is available in the double degree, whether it needs to be an additional unit, its prerequisites, or whether rules will change by his commencement year." Reduced load, maximum duration, additional units and HECS-HELP are also still open questions. Yet the case opens "I chose." Cost: if the structure blocks CFD or caps duration under six years, Option C fails and the decision is redone.

2. **It trades the top goal for a fallback.** F1/motorsport is ranked first, with "strong engineering technical depth." The file concedes "engineering technical elective space is much more restricted" and that Student A "may be stronger for F1." Cost: he rejects the best path for his main goal to insure against a goal he says he will not optimise for.

3. **The 6-year spread does not fix the real problem.** "Taking fewer units per semester does not create extra elective slots." Option C adds no technical depth but adds delayed income, "more HECS accrual and opportunity cost," and longer burnout exposure. Cost: two extra years for time Option A could give without them.

4. **A cheaper option meets the finance goal.** He lists "Mech Eng + selected finance electives/minor" and admits elite finance needs "internships, networking, financial modelling, finance clubs" anyway, mostly outside the degree. The file shows no evidence the full Commerce degree beats a minor for IB, PE or consulting. Cost: a second degree paid for in time and WAM with no shown gain.

5. **The FSAE plan is untested.** "He understands he can join in second year" is his belief, with no evidence of selection requirements or time demands. Cost: if the team expects more than a double load allows, the F1 pathway weakens further.
