# Defense opening

The single Mechanical degree with a self-built finance signal should go ahead.

1. **The goal is served by engineering, not commerce.** McLaren asks for "an engineering, maths or science degree with minimum 2:1"; Mercedes lists "hands-on vehicle experience such as Formula Student"; "Neither mentions commerce." Objection: finance is a hedge. But the hedge is cheap: "Most of the finance signal obtainable with 3-4 well-chosen units, a financial modelling project and a finance society role."

2. **It protects the time FSAE and grades need.** The double is "5 years full-time" with Commerce core "several contributing little to F1 or serious finance," and design, thermofluids, FEA and the final-year project all arrive a year later. Spread out it becomes "7 years (not ~6)" and "50+ hours a week for seven years." Objection: a slower double is gentler. It is longer and no lighter overall.

3. **It leaves room to learn.** The single has "about 8 flexible slots"; the double has "engineering minors not available" and "no technical elective slots." Those slots can hold finance units (option A+) or simulation and controls work matching Monash Motorsport's focus on "controls, electronics, data and simulation."

4. **It is reversible; the double is not.** The review recommends "starting in the single degree and keeping the double only if those checks come back in its favour." Committing to the double risks years and money: "well over $150k gross" of delayed salary (rough, unsourced), plus Band 4 debt per unit.

5. **It fits the maths risk.** With no Specialist Maths, "Maths is the WAM risk in first year." A lighter, purely engineering load protects the grade F1 employers screen on.

**Concessions.** F1 is "low probability from any pathway," and the visa hurdle applies to every option. Investment banking is harder without Commerce. The record is silent on transfer rules, CSP effects and FSAE joining rules. The single still wins: the door it narrows is "fixable later via master's or CFA after 2-3 years of work."
