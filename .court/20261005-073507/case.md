Should I (Lukas, Year 12 VCE student, Victoria, Australia) take the single Monash Mechanical Engineering degree (E3001) rather than the Engineering + Commerce (Finance) double (E3005), given my main goal is F1/motorsport engineering and Monash Formula SAE (FSAE)?

Student: VCE subjects are Mathematical Methods, Physics, Economics, Product Design and Technologies, English (no Specialist Maths). Main goal: motorsport/F1 mechanical engineering and Monash Formula SAE. Secondary: finance/business as a hedge/second pathway. I had been considering the double, possibly spread over ~3 units a semester to protect WAM and FSAE time, and using HECS-HELP. I also like designing an ideal naturally aspirated race engine as a hobby.

Facts from a research review (prepared 5 Oct 2026, based on Monash 2027 course maps updated 3 Aug 2026, marked "pending finalisation – subject to change"):
- E3001 Mechanical: 4 years; about 8 flexible slots (first-year breadth, Year 1 elective, 2 in Year 2, then engineering minor or mechanical technical elective slots in Years 3-4).
- E3005 Mechanical + Commerce: 5 years full-time; engineering minors not available; Mechanical map shows no technical elective slots. Same engineering core stretched across 5 years with Commerce core units (marketing, management, commercial law, stats) interleaved, several contributing little to F1 or serious finance.
- Timing in single vs double: Design 1 (MMA2001) Year 2 Sem 1 vs Year 3 Sem 1; Thermofluids 1 and Modelling and control Year 2 Sem 2 vs Year 3 Sem 2; Mechanical design 2 Year 3 vs Year 4; FEA (MMA4001) and final-year project Year 4 vs Year 5.
- Maths: no Specialist Maths, so almost certainly on foundation path; Foundation mathematics (ENG1090) required without Specialist Maths study score above 30, prerequisite for Engineering Mathematics and Numerical Analysis. If Physics score under 25, also PHS1001. Maths is the WAM risk in first year.
- MMA3101 (CFD) exists in the 2026 handbook; prerequisites, elective status and availability for the commencement year NOT verified. No slot in the double's Mechanical map; could plausibly fit a Year 3-4 technical elective slot in the single degree.
- Load: standard 24 credit points a semester; part-time is under 36 credit points a year. Three units a semester is exactly 36, so not classed part-time; effect on CSP status unconfirmed. Time-limit figures found were decades old.
- The double is 40 units: at 3 units a semester that is 14 semesters = 7 years (not ~6). The single at 3 a semester is 32 units, ~5.5 years. The double costs ~25% more calendar time at any given weekly load.
- Each unit is ~12 hours a week; 3 units = 36 hours study. FSAE in build phases plausibly 15-20+ hours a week (planning assumption, unverified). Slower pace still means 50+ hours a week for seven years.
- F1 hiring evidence: McLaren graduate programme asks for an engineering, maths or science degree with minimum 2:1 or equivalent. Mercedes lists hands-on vehicle experience such as Formula Student and notes UK roles are subject to Home Office visa rules. Neither mentions commerce. What gets hired: engineering grade, FSAE ownership, CAD; visa is a separate hurdle.
- Monash Motorsport has moved from combustion to autonomous and electric vehicles and designs its own inverters and motors. Relevant skills are controls, electronics, data and simulation, not NA engine design. One older team interview says technical students are only taken from second year (current rule unconfirmed); if so, the double means arriving in Year 2 without a mechanical design unit.
- Finance careers (judgement, NOT verified against sources): easy from engineering alone: management consulting, operations/strategy in industrials, project management, energy/mining/infrastructure advisory. Moderate: project/infrastructure finance, corporate development (engineering + a few finance units + finance club + internship is competitive). Hard without Commerce signalling: investment banking (early cycles, preference for finance-credentialled candidates). PE/VC mostly post-experience. Fixable later via master's or CFA after 2-3 years of work. Most of the finance signal obtainable with 3-4 well-chosen units, a financial modelling project and a finance society role.
- Cost: HECS-HELP contributions per unit, so stretching the double doesn't multiply them; Commerce is Band 4 (higher than engineering at $9,880 per full-time year for 2027), so the double adds more debt per unit. Larger cost is 2-3 extra years of delayed graduate salary, roughly estimated well over $150k gross (rough, unsourced).
- Technical electives matter less than the double's delayed core and lost time; FEA, numerical methods and machine learning (MMA3001) are core in the Mechanical map anyway. A single CFD unit isn't the gate to F1 aero; an FSAE aero/simulation project is stronger proof. Aerospace specialisation puts Aerodynamics 2 (MAE3401) in core but loses some mechanical design units.
- Options in the review: A single with managed load; B double standard pace (weakest: heaviest load, delayed design and thermo, no electives); C double spread (~7 years, sequencing risk, only if finance a genuine co-career); A+ (recommended): single degree plus 3-4 commerce/finance units in Year 1-2 free slots, summer units if needed, and finance experience outside the degree (finance society, modelling project, internships).
- Review's own caveats: F1 is low probability from any pathway (small UK-based graduate intakes, visa sponsorship constraint); FSAE tends to expand to fill time. Unconfirmed items: whether transfer between E3001 and E3005 after Year 1 is possible and at what cost; MMA3101 availability; whether FSAE hours count toward the 420 CPD hours Monash requires; current Monash Motorsport first-year joining rules; how a 3-unit load affects CSP status and time limits.
- The review recommends starting in the single degree and keeping the double only if those checks come back in its favour.
