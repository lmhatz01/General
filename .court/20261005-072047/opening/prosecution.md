# Prosecution opening

1. **The money is unaccounted for.** The case says "no ... current income, savings, ... or whether the $10000 is savings or borrowed." A Year 12 student is, on the record, a school student. If the $10000 is borrowed, a loan plus interest falls on someone with no stated income. If it is savings, it may be nearly all they have. Cost: debt or an emptied safety net before study or work has started.

2. **The price is only the entry fee.** The case states "about $10000" and nothing else. Registration, compulsory third-party insurance, comprehensive insurance, servicing and fuel are absent from the record. Young and new drivers often face high insurance premiums, but the file gives no figures, so how big this cost is cannot be judged. Cost: a purchase that looks affordable and is not.

3. **There is no evidence of need.** The file says nothing on "whether they currently have access to a car." Without this, buying may replace a free family car, public transport or a licence not yet held. The file does not say the student has a licence, and "Year 12 student" does not settle it. Cost: $10000 spent on something not needed.

4. **The asset is unjudgeable.** The file gives "no make, model, age, kilometres, seller type." A used car at this price could be sound or a lemon. Seller type (dealer or private) changes the buyer's legal protection, and the record does not say which applies. Cost: a repair bill or a car that fails soon after purchase.

5. **The timing is wrong as stated.** The person is mid-Year 12, with exams and the next step (university, TAFE, work, a gap year) unknown. The record gives no plans or location. Cost: a large, hard-to-reverse commitment made before the facts that should drive it are known; a used car also depreciates, so selling later loses money.

The case as written cannot support a yes. It asks for approval of a $10000 spend while omitting every fact that decides it.
