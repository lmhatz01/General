# Defense: buying a $10000 used car should go ahead

1. **Used is the sensible way to buy at this budget.** The case asks about "a $10000 used car", not a new one. A used car has already taken its steepest depreciation, so a Year 12 student gets more car per dollar. The objection is that used cars carry hidden faults. That risk is real but manageable with a pre-purchase inspection and a roadworthy check, and it is smaller than the cost of a new car.

2. **The amount is realistic for a first car.** The file says "spending about $10000" and that the person is in "Victoria, Australia (so the dollars are most likely AUD)". In AUD, $10000 is a normal budget for a reliable, older small car. The objection is that it is a lot of money for a student. But the record does not say it is a stretch, and a figure that size is not obviously reckless.

3. **A car can serve a Year 12 student's next steps.** Turning 18 or finishing school often brings work, TAFE or university, and travel that public transport covers poorly. The record is silent on whether the student has "access to a car" now. If they do not, a car has clear practical value. The objection is that they may not need one yet, and that is a fair point, but it is a reason to check, not to say no.

4. **The question is built to flex.** The brief asks to "say what would change the answer". The idea can be defended as conditional: yes if the money is the student's own savings, the car is a sound, well-documented model, and running costs are covered. That is a stronger position than a flat no.

**Conceded:** The record gives no income, savings, or source of the $10000. If it is borrowed, or it drains all their savings, I cannot defend it. Registration, insurance (steep for young drivers) and repairs are also missing, and they could push the true cost well above $10000.
