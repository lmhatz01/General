**The case:** Should I buy a $10000 used car?

**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## Charges that stuck
1. Nobody said whether the $10000 is the student's own savings or borrowed, and the defense admitted it cannot defend the purchase if it is borrowed or drains savings (all 11 guilty jurors).
2. Registration, young-driver insurance and repairs are on top of the price, and no income or budget is stated to pay them (10 jurors).
3. There is no evidence of real need or legal readiness: licence, age to sign contracts, or no other way to get around (6 jurors).

## What the defense got right
1. Buying used at this price is a normal, sensible first-car approach, and the amount is not obviously reckless for AUD.
2. The missing facts are things the student can check before paying (source of money, inspection, roadworthy, seller type), so this is a "yes, if" case rather than a flat no. The one not-guilty juror leaned on this.

## Conditions for acquittal
1. State that the $10000 is the student's own spare savings, not borrowed, and does not empty their savings. This appears in nearly every juror's flip line.
2. Set aside and write down a budget for a year of registration, insurance and servicing or repairs, with income or savings to cover it. Get a real insurance quote this week.
3. Confirm need and eligibility: holds a licence, is 18 or able to sign legally, has no free car available, and plans to buy from a licensed dealer or with a roadworthy certificate and inspection.

## The sentence
The court finds the plan fails as described, not that a car is a bad idea. Do not hand over any money yet. Work out whose money it is, get an insurance quote, and confirm you hold a licence and truly need the car. If those checks come out well, come back with the facts and the answer is likely yes.
