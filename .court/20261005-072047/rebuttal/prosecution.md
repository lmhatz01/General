# Prosecution rebuttal

1. **Does not survive.** "Used is better than new" answers a question nobody asked. The charge is whether to spend $10000 at all, and used-versus-new says nothing about whether the student can afford it. Inspection and roadworthy costs are extra spending the defense adds to the bill.

2. **Mostly fails.** "Normal budget" is a claim about cars, not about this student. The record gives no income or savings, so the defense cannot say the sum is not a stretch. The defense's own words, "the record does not say", cut against it: silence is not evidence of affordability.

3. **Weak.** The defense admits the record is silent on whether the student already has car access, so the value of a car is an assumption. "Check first" concedes that the case as stated is not ready to proceed.

4. **Survives, but it helps the prosecution.** A conditional yes (own savings, sound model, running costs covered) means the unconditional case fails. Nothing in the record meets any of those conditions. The charge is that it should not go ahead "the way it is described", and the description meets none of them.

**Concession by the defense:** It cannot defend the purchase if the money is borrowed or drains savings, and it admits registration, insurance and repairs could push the true cost well above $10000. The record is silent on all of these.

**Point for the jury:** Point 4. Do the conditions the defense needs appear in the case as described? They do not, so the case fails as stated.
