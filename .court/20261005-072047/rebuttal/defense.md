# Defense rebuttal

1. **Money unaccounted for: partly survives.** The gap is in the file, not the idea. The case asks us to judge the general idea and say what would change the answer, so "depends on savings versus borrowed" is the answer.

2. **Running costs: survives only as a condition.** The record gives no figures, as the prosecution admits. An unquantified cost cannot sink the case. It sets a budget rule: price plus a year of running costs.

3. **No evidence of need: weak.** The record is silent, and silence cuts both ways. The prosecution assumes a free family car exists; the file does not say so.

4. **Asset unjudgeable: does not survive.** The case is about the general idea, not a specific car. Inspection and seller type can be checked before paying.

5. **Timing: partly survives.** The record gives no plans, so the harm is speculation. Nothing says the purchase must happen now.

The prosecution's closing line proves too much: any "should I buy X?" with no details fails that test.

**The point for the jury: point 1.** It is the only one that could make the purchase harmful whatever car is chosen. If the money is borrowed against no income, the answer is no. If it is the student's own spare savings, the rest become conditions to check.
