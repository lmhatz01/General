EVIDENCE FOUND (from web search result summaries; the full pages could not be opened because the network blocked them, so figures are as reported in snippets and not independently checked):
1. Deakin University research (reported by Engineers Australia and trade press): of recent Australian engineering bachelor graduates aged 20-24, 46% worked in a professional engineering role; across all ages, 32%. More than 14% of all engineering bachelor graduates worked in IT and technology roles outside engineering. Reported fields outside engineering include marketing, construction, finance, science, education and health. Most were in highly skilled, well-paid jobs. Note: this shows many engineering graduates work outside engineering, but the finance share was not given in what was found, and "outside engineering" is mostly not finance.
2. Engineers Australia / Create Digital reporting: about 95% of engineering graduates in full-time work three years after graduating; entry median salary about $75,000; engineering graduates earn about $26,000 more than other graduates five years in; median income of engineering graduates about $106,550. (Source figures as reported, year not confirmed.)
3. A UK figure reported in search results: 28% of UK engineering graduates enter finance and business, the same rate as all other graduates. This suggests engineering graduates do not enter finance at a higher rate than others, and it is a UK figure, not Australian.
4. Anecdotal career stories (UK/Canada/US alumni and forum posts) of mechanical engineers moving into investment banking; guidance sites say banks are used to hiring technical graduates if they show motivation and prepare for interviews. No percentage for mechanical engineers into investment banking was found.
5. Motorsport side: Adrian Newey has said that, because of the F1 budget cap, F1 is "no longer the best-paid industry" and loses people to tech companies and WEC teams. UK motorsport graduate starting salaries reported at about 25,000-30,000 pounds; UK motorsport engineers about 27,000-60,000 pounds, average about 37,430 (2024). Examples of former F1 engineers moving to environmental science or to an MBA and automotive strategy.
6. Australian graduate consulting employers (for example Deloitte, BCG, Accenture) recruit engineering graduates for strategy and operations roles.

Gaps in the evidence: no Australian figure for the share of engineering-only graduates who end up in finance; no data comparing engineering-only with engineering+Commerce graduates into finance; mostly snippets, not full sources.
