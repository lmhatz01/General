# Defense opening

The plan, as Lukas wrote it, is sound: lead with motorsport mechanical engineering at Monash, keep finance as an option, and decide the rest later.

1. **Engineering-first is the lower-regret path, and the evidence supports his argument.** Deakin research found only "46% worked in a professional engineering role" at ages 20-24, and "more than 14%" of all engineering graduates work in IT outside engineering. Reported destinations include "marketing, construction, finance, science, education and health", mostly "highly skilled, well-paid jobs". The objection is that finance is not specifically shown. True, but the claim he needs is that the degree keeps doors open, and this shows it does. Consulting employers "recruit engineering graduates", and banks are "used to hiring technical graduates".

2. **The downside is cushioned.** About "95% of engineering graduates in full-time work three years after graduating" and a reported "$26,000 more than other graduates five years in". If motorsport pay disappoints (Newey: F1 is "no longer the best-paid industry"), he still holds a strongly paid degree.

3. **His motivation and preparation are real.** Primary interest is F1 mechanical engineering. He is already doing "engine-design work", has looked up "Monash electives such as MMA3101", and plans Formula SAE. Employers value this build experience, and a double degree could dilute it. He names that risk himself: it "may cost WAM, Formula SAE time and technical depth".

4. **Staying flexible is cheap.** "Engineering with finance electives or a minor" is an option. The unknowns, "whether Commerce can be added or dropped later", are checkable facts, not flaws in the plan. The new fact "willing to move overseas" widens his motorsport options.

**Conceded:** The evidence does not show engineering-only graduates reach finance as readily as double-degree holders. The UK figure (28%, "the same rate as all other graduates") suggests no special advantage. There is no Australian finance share. Low junior motorsport pay (UK £25,000-30,000) and whether he would accept it overseas are unaddressed. The evidence is also snippets only, "not independently checked".

These are gaps to fill, not reasons to stop. The plan can proceed while he checks Monash's Commerce rules and WAM load.
