# Defense rebuttal

**1. Partly survives.** The 28% UK figure shows no finance advantage, but also no disadvantage: engineers enter finance at the same rate as everyone else. That supports "keeps it open," which is all Lukas claimed. The record adds 95% full-time employment, a $26,000 earnings premium, and consulting firms recruiting engineers. Nothing shows he must drop Commerce on this premise.

**2. Survives as a caveat, not as a reason to stop.** The evidence is thin, and the record says so. But the plan does not depend on any one figure. Engineering is the strong base in every option, and the options differ only at the margin.

**3. Survives, and it is the strongest point.** But it is a gap to close, not a flaw in the plan. Whether Commerce can be added or dropped later is a question Monash can answer. The plan is to be completed after it is checked, not abandoned.

**4. Mostly fails.** "Willing to move overseas" directly addresses entry. The pay gap is real, but the record's figures are UK, and its examples of ex-F1 engineers moving on show the engineering degree has fallback value. The record does not say how he would get an F1 role from Victoria. That is a missing fact, not a proven barrier.

**5. Fails.** An ATAR gates every plan, motorsport or finance. The case is about direction, not about whether he gets in. "Aiming for strong results" is a goal, and the record gives no sign he will miss it.

**The point for the jury: 3.** If the Commerce rule is checked, the plan can go ahead. If it is not, the charge holds.
