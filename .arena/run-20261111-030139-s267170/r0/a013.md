## Assumptions

- "Strong results" means a high ATAR, but no number is assumed. The ranking below holds across a range of ATARs; the "what would change it" section covers the extremes.
- Primary goal is mechanical engineering with a motorsport lean. Finance is a fallback or complement.

- He is an Australian citizen or permanent resident, so he is eligible for a Commonwealth supported place (CSP) with HECS-HELP.

## The plain-speaking part: where the plan is weak

1. **The double degree is not what gets you into motorsport.** Race teams hire on technical evidence: Formula SAE results, CAD and manufacturing portfolios, simulation and data work, and strong engineering fundamentals. A Commerce degree adds almost nothing to that case. It adds one year (typically five years versus four, which is the check to run) and diverts time and WAM.
2. **"Finance as a fallback" is cheaper than it looks.** Engineering graduates from strong universities routinely move into finance, consulting and analytics roles, because quantitative skill is the scarce part. A Commerce degree helps, but it is not the only door. A cheap hedge (a handful of finance electives, a minor-style sequence, the student finance or investment club, a later graduate diploma, or CFA study) keeps the door open without a fifth year.
3. **Formula SAE competes with grades for the same hours.** Teams reward the people who give them 15 to 25 hours a week during build season. A student carrying a heavy double-degree load, or spreading it at three units per semester, may have the calendar slack but not necessarily the same energy and WAM. Spreading to three units extends the degree (more years of living costs and HECS, and a later start to full-time pay) and does not by itself buy time inside the team's build season.
4. **The F1 route is narrow and mostly overseas.** Formula 1 teams are mostly UK-based, with work-rights hurdles and a pool dominated by top-grade Masters and PhD graduates. Australia's motorsport industry (Supercars and related teams, suppliers, automotive and aerospace engineering) is the realistic landing zone, and a very strong Formula SAE record travels well in both directions. Treat F1 as a stretch goal with a practical plan B, not as a plan you can optimise a university choice around alone.
5. **Missing Specialist Mathematics.** Methods is the standard prerequisite for engineering, but the first-year maths in a heavy-maths course assumes comfortable algebra and calculus. Plan to use the bridging or support resources the university offers, and confirm in the VTAC Guide whether any target course lists Specialist as a prerequisite or recommended study. This is a check item, not a disqualifier.
6. **Interest in motorsport is not skill.** I separate three things below: direct skill development (CAD, vehicle dynamics, manufacturing), transferable practical intuition (building things that break, working to deadlines in a team) and mere interest (following F1). Only the first two count on a CV.

## Ranked recommendation

Ranking criteria, in order: (1) depth and rigour of mechanical engineering, (2) a strong Formula SAE team with real build and competition culture, (3) industry links and placements, (4) flexibility to add finance, (5) cost and duration.

| Rank | Option | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monash, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical specialisation, single degree, with finance electives or a minor and Monash Motorsport** | Strong engineering school, a well-known and successful Formula SAE team on the same campus, and the least lost time. Keeps the option of adding Commerce open. |
| 2 | **Monash, Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance) double degree** | Same engineering and team access, but longer and heavier. Worth it only if Lukas decides finance is a real second career, not an insurance policy. |
| 3 | **Swinburne, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical** | Known for industry-based learning and placements, plus a hands-on Formula SAE team. A very good option if practical experience is the priority and the ATAR is a constraint. |
| 4 | **University of Melbourne, Bachelor of Science (or relevant undergraduate pathway) into Master of Engineering (Mechanical)** | Prestigious, strong research and Melbourne's own Formula SAE team, but the 3+2 structure means five years and the engineering content comes later. Strong if he wants a research or postgraduate path. |
| 5 | **RMIT, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical** | Practical, industry-connected and with an active racing team. Close to Swinburne in logic. |
| 6 | **Deakin, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical** | Includes work-integrated learning, and has a motorsport team. Geelong campus is a commute or relocation consideration. |
| 7 | **UNSW Sydney, mechanical engineering** | Strong brand and the Redback Racing team, but interstate cost and relocation, and no clear advantage over Monash for his goals. |
| 8 | **University of Queensland, Sydney, Adelaide and similar** | Credible schools with Formula SAE teams (for example UQ Racing and the University of Sydney's team). Choose them only for a lifestyle reason or a scholarship. Note that Adelaide's arrangements have been changing, so verify. |

Overseas undergraduate study (UK motorsport engineering courses, for example) is not realistically justified for a first degree: cost, visa and loss of the Australian Formula SAE and HECS-HELP pathway. It becomes sensible as a postgraduate step after a strong Australian degree.

### Why Monash single degree is first

- **Technical depth per hour.** All of the degree's time goes to engineering and the team. Direct skill development comes from first-year design and modelling units, then mechanics, dynamics, thermodynamics and fluid mechanics, materials, manufacturing, control and the final-year design project.
- **Formula SAE.** Being on campus with the team makes it practical to join in first year, which is the best single predictor of a motorsport engineering CV. Check the team's current recruitment timing on its own site.
- **Keeps options open.** A finance sequence of electives or a minor gives vocabulary and credibility. Whether he can add Commerce later by transfer is something to ask Monash directly rather than assume.
- **Cost.** Four years instead of five means a year of earlier full-time income and less HECS-HELP debt. Check current indexation and repayment thresholds on the study assist or Australian Government site rather than relying on memory.

### Why the double degree is second, not first

It is not a bad option. The disadvantages are concrete: one extra year, a higher load, harder WAM maintenance and less time for the team. It wins only if finance becomes a genuine second goal. If the choice is "Formula SAE leadership role with a strong WAM" versus "Commerce degree", the first is better for the primary goal.

## Subject-level advice (Monash)

I cannot verify current Monash unit codes, and I cannot identify MMA3101, so treat the following as **topics to look for in the Handbook**, not confirmed codes. Search the Monash Handbook for your intended specialisation and read each unit's outcomes.

| Topic to find in the Handbook | How it helps a motorsport pathway |
|---|---|
| Engineering mathematics (multivariable calculus, differential equations, linear algebra) | The basis of vehicle dynamics, vibration and control models. |
| Mechanics and dynamics (rigid body, vibration) | Suspension kinematics, weight transfer, ride and handling behaviour. |
| Engineering design and CAD units | Parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing and design for manufacture, which are exactly what a Formula SAE team needs. |
| Solid mechanics and FEA | Chassis and component stiffness, stress and basic FEA. |
| Materials and composites | Carbon fibre monocoque and aluminium or steel selection. |
| Thermodynamics, fluid mechanics and aerodynamics | Powertrain cooling and aerodynamic loads. |
| Control and mechatronics | Data acquisition, sensors, traction and stability control. |
| Numerical methods, programming and optimisation | Simulation, lap-time and design-space optimisation. |
| Manufacturing | Machining, process choice and design for manufacture. |
| Finance electives or a Commerce minor (only after the above) | Keeps finance open without costing technical depth. |

Whether MMA3101 fits depends on what it is. Look up its title, prerequisites and learning outcomes in the Handbook, then judge it against the list above. If it is not mechanics, dynamics, control, materials, manufacturing, CAD, numerical methods or design, it should not displace a core engineering unit.

## Entry requirements: what to check

For each course in your preference list, confirm on the VTAC Guide for 2027 entry and the university page:
- Prerequisites (Methods and Physics are the usual engineering expectations; check whether Specialist is required or recommended).
- Selection rank or ATAR range for last year (treat as a guide only; it changes yearly).
- Adjustment factor schemes (for example Monash's and Melbourne's schemes, and SEAS-type equity or subject-bonus programs) and whether you qualify.
- Whether the course has additional requirements (some programs use portfolios, interviews or scholarships).
- Course length, honours status and whether professional accreditation is by Engineers Australia.

## What would change the ranking

- **A very high ATAR and strong interest in research or postgraduate study:** raise Melbourne.
- **A lower ATAR than hoped:** raise Swinburne, RMIT and Deakin; they are still legitimate routes into engineering.
- **Genuine enthusiasm for finance as a career (not just a hedge):** raise the double degree to first.
- **A scholarship or a team leadership offer elsewhere:** move that option up.
- **Monash confirming an easy later switch into Commerce:** strengthens the single degree further.

## Next steps (in order)

1. **Now to results (mid-December):** Check the 2027 VTAC Guide and the course pages for dates. Rank your preferences in the order you truly want them; VTAC treats the order as preference, so do not game it.
2. **Before results:** Open the Monash Handbook for the mechanical specialisation and the double degree, and write a one-page map of the units. Email or attend an information session to ask about the single-to-double transfer, minors, and how many units team members typically carry.
3. **Now:** Learn CAD on your own (a free or student licence of a parametric CAD tool, such as Fusion or SolidWorks). Build two small designs with drawings and tolerances. This is direct skill development.
4. **After ATAR release:** Use the change-of-preference window if it opens; the deadline is on VTAC.
5. **Before orientation:** Follow Monash Motorsport and other teams' recruitment calls and be ready to apply in first year.
6. **Ongoing:** Keep the WAM high in first year; it matters for honours, scholarships and placements.

## Bottom line

Put the Monash mechanical engineering single degree first, join Monash Motorsport in first year, and use finance electives or a minor as the hedge. Take the double degree only if finance becomes a real goal, not an insurance policy. Verify every number in this document against official sources before you commit.
