# Best-suited university options for Lukas (VTAC 2027 entry)

## The short answer

Do the Monash Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical specialisation, as a single degree, and build finance around it with commerce electives or a minor. Make Formula SAE (Monash Motorsport) your main extracurricular from first year. Keep the Engineering/Commerce double as a close second. It becomes the better choice only if one of the conditions in "What would change the ranking" is true.

The reason is simple. Your primary goal is motorsport engineering. In that field, what gets you hired is a strong technical record: a good WAM, a real role on a Formula SAE car, and demonstrable CAD, manufacturing and data skills. The double degree costs you time and attention, which are the two things you need most for that. It buys finance optionality, but a strong engineering degree already gives you most of that optionality.

## Assumptions

- Your subjects are Methods, Physics, Economics, Product Design and Technologies (PDT) and English, with no Specialist Maths. You are aiming for a high ATAR, but the target is not stated, so I have not invented a number.
- "Motorsport/F1 engineering" means a career in vehicle design, performance, simulation or data. It does not mean being a driver or being close to the sport in some other way.
- You are happy to study in Melbourne. Moving interstate is treated as a cost, not a plus.

## Reality checks on the plan

1. **F1 is a very small job market.** There are only a handful of F1 teams, mostly in the UK, and they hire graduates in small numbers. Realistic routes are:
   - an Australian degree, then Formula SAE, then local motorsport or high-performance engineering (Supercars teams, suppliers, automotive R&D);
   - a UK or European master's, then entry to the motorsport industry.

   An Australian engineering degree is a legitimate start for either route. Your university choice matters less than your portfolio.
2. **Missing Specialist Maths is a manageable gap, not a disaster.** First-year engineering maths is taught from scratch, but it moves fast. Spend the summer before semester 1 on Specialist-level topics (vectors, complex numbers, basic differential equations and integration techniques).
3. **"Interest in motorsport" is not a skill.** It does not count on a résumé. Fluency in CAD, a car you helped build, and data you analysed do.
4. **The double degree is not automatically superior.** It feels safe because it keeps two doors open. For motorsport specifically, you would be paying an extra year and a heavier load to improve your backup, not your primary goal.

## What actually builds a motorsport profile

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Direct engineering skill** | Dynamics, vibrations, control, thermo-fluids, solid mechanics and materials, numerical methods, CAD/CAE, manufacturing, data analysis (MATLAB/Python) |
| **Transferable practical intuition** | Machining and composites on the Formula SAE car, wiring and data logging, test days, debugging, deadlines, teamwork |
| **Mere interest** | Watching races, following F1 news (fine for motivation, worthless for hiring) |

The ranking below favours options that maximise the first two.

## Ranked options

### 1. Monash, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical, with finance electives or a commerce minor (recommended)

- **Why it ranks first:**
  - Monash Motorsport is on campus, and its recruitment feeds from first-year students.
  - It is the best combination of a strong team, a strong course and a Melbourne base.
  - The four-year length leaves room for a heavy Formula SAE commitment and a high WAM.
  - A single degree makes your first-year load manageable while you learn CAD and join the team.
- **Where finance fits:** use free electives or a minor in commerce or finance. Check the Handbook for what is allowed and how many units are available. This gives you the introductory finance vocabulary without the double degree's overhead.
- **Subjects to prioritise** (verify codes and availability in the Handbook; the codes below are from memory and may have changed):
  - Engineering mathematics (first-year ENG1005 or similar): this is the foundation for dynamics, control and simulation.
  - Mechanics and dynamics units (e.g. ENG1014, MEC2401): these are the core of suspension, vehicle dynamics and kinematics.
  - Mechanics of solids and materials: these underpin FEA, composites and structural design.
  - Design and manufacturing units: parametric CAD, drawings, tolerancing, design for manufacture and assembly.
  - Control and numerical methods: these are used in vehicle control, simulation and optimisation.
  - Thermo-fluids: this is the base for aerodynamics and powertrain.
  - Final-year or thesis project: pick something motorsport-related, such as suspension, aero, composites or data.
- **Weakness:** finance exposure is limited to electives, so if you decide at 21 that you want a finance career, you will be competing against commerce graduates. Plan for that with a graduate path (see below).

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours) / Commerce (Finance), double degree

- **Why it is close behind:**
  - Same Formula SAE access as option 1.
  - It gives you a formal finance qualification and a stronger finance fallback.
  - It is a recognised combination for engineering-adjacent finance, consulting and commercial roles.
- **Costs:**
  - It is roughly an extra year at standard load (five years versus four), plus the extra HECS-HELP debt (check current fees).
  - At standard load it is a heavy semester, and a heavy semester usually lowers your WAM and your Formula SAE time.
  - Stretching it to about three units a semester frees time for the team but makes it longer still, so the delay to earning increases and the opportunity cost grows.
  - It dilutes technical depth, because commerce units replace engineering electives.
- **Honest assessment:** the double is a good choice if finance is a genuine second career goal. It is a poor choice if it is only insurance. Insurance is better bought more cheaply, with a minor or a later master's.

### 3. University of Melbourne, Bachelor's degree then Master of Engineering (Mechanical)

- **Strengths:** top academic reputation, strong research and mathematics, a high-quality master's, and a good brand for graduate employers overseas.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - The pathway is longer and structured differently, as a three-year bachelor's followed by a two-year master's. Check that the undergraduate major you choose leads into the mechanical master's.
  - Selection ranks are generally higher.
  - The master's years are expensive in HECS terms.
  - The team culture and hands-on emphasis are, to my understanding, less central than at Monash. Verify this.
- **Choose it if** you want the strongest academic foundation and are comfortable with a longer path.

### 4. RMIT, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical

- **Strengths:** a practical, hands-on reputation, strong industry links, and an RMIT-based Formula SAE team (check its current name and status).
- **Weakness:** I could not verify its current standing relative to Monash. It is a good fallback if Monash is out of reach on selection rank.

### 5. Swinburne, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical

- **Strengths:** well known for industry-based learning and placements (verify the current structure), and for practical, project-based work.
- **Weakness:** an industry placement may add time. A good choice if you value paid industry experience early.

### 6. UNSW, Mechanical Engineering (Sydney)

- **Strengths:** high-quality course, strong research, and a Formula SAE team.
- **Weakness:** moving to Sydney adds cost and separates you from Victorian motorsport networks. It does not outrank Monash on the factors that matter to you.

### 7. Deakin, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical

- **Strengths:** a Geelong campus, a team, and solid accreditation.
- **Weakness:** a smaller motorsport network than Monash or RMIT, as far as I know.

### Also considered

- **Sydney, UQ and Adelaide:** good courses with active teams (Adelaide's structure changed with the 2026 merger into Adelaide University, so check it). I ranked them below the Melbourne options because interstate moves add cost without a clear motorsport advantage.
- **Overseas:** realistic only at postgraduate level. Examples are UK motorsport or vehicle-dynamics master's courses at places such as Cranfield or Oxford Brookes (verify current offerings, fees and visa rules). Do not plan an overseas bachelor's, since the cost and visa barriers are high and the payoff is not clear.

## Can engineering alone lead into finance later?

Yes, with a caveat. Engineers move into finance (quantitative roles, consulting, infrastructure and industrial finance) fairly often, especially with a strong WAM and a master's or graduate program. The usual routes are:
- graduate programs at banks, consultancies and industrials that accept any degree;
- a Master of Finance or MBA later;
- the CFA program (verify current requirements).

What you give up is easy entry to purely commerce-focused graduate roles at the start. If you want that door open, take a commerce minor.

## What would change the ranking

- **Move the double to first** if you find over the next year that you actually want a finance career as much as an engineering one, or if Monash confirms a flexible pathway that lets you start the double and drop to single without penalty.
- **Move RMIT or Swinburne up** if your ATAR is below the Monash cut-off, or if you value structured industry placement over a research-heavy course.
- **Move Melbourne up** if your ATAR is very high and you value a master's-level academic path over team involvement.
- **Drop the double further** if your first-semester WAM suffers or if the team requires heavy time commitment.

## Cost and HECS-HELP

Both degrees are Commonwealth-supported places, and HECS-HELP lets you defer the student contribution. I have not verified current amounts or the repayment threshold. Two points hold regardless: the double costs one extra year of contribution and one year of foregone full-time salary, and stretching the double over a longer period increases both. Check the Monash fees page and the StudyAssist website for the current figures.
