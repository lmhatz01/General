## 4. Why Monash single-degree engineering is first

**Technical depth.** The single degree leaves room for the electives and projects you listed: CAD, vehicle dynamics, manufacturing and so on. A mechanical engineering degree is accredited by Engineers Australia [Recall]. Accreditation is what lets you work as a professional engineer and be recognised overseas, so check it on the course page.

**Formula SAE.** Monash Motorsport is the university's Formula SAE team [Recall]. It is one of the best-known student teams in Australia, but I did not verify its recent results. For motorsport hiring, a year or more of real team work, such as designing a component, machining it, testing it and defending it in a design review, is worth more than a double degree. It is the closest thing you can get to the job while still a student. Treat it as part of the degree choice, not an optional extra.

**Workload.** Your concern that a double degree hurts WAM and team commitment is reasonable. Engineering alone is already demanding, and the team is a heavy time commitment. A single degree gives you the most room to do both well.

**Finance later.** The assumption that engineering alone closes the door on finance is mostly wrong [Judgement]. Engineering graduates are recruited into consulting, quantitative roles and some finance roles, because the maths and modelling training is valued. The more honest gap is credentials and finance vocabulary, which you can fill with finance electives, a minor-type sequence, the CFA path, or a postgraduate degree such as a master's in finance later, often with employer support. I could not verify which elective or minor options exist, so check them in the Handbook.

**Cost.** A shorter degree costs less in tuition and means earlier full-time pay. Check the current Commonwealth Supported Place fees and HECS-HELP rules and loan limits on the government study assist site [Recall: HECS-HELP is an income-contingent loan, repaid through the tax system once you earn above a threshold].

## 5. The double-degree question, answered directly

**The double degree is not automatically superior, and for your stated primary goal it is not the best default.**

| Factor | Single (Mech Eng) | Double (Eng + Commerce Finance) |
|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Highest; room for extra engineering electives | Same engineering core, but fewer free electives |
| WAM | Easier to keep high | Harder; two degrees compete for the same hours |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best | Tight unless you extend the duration |
| Duration | About four years [Recall] | About five years at full load [Recall]; longer at three units a semester |
| Cost and delay to earning | Lowest | Extra year or more of fees and lost income |
| Finance flexibility | Moderate, via electives or postgraduate study | Highest, with a recognised commerce credential |
| Employability | Strong for engineering | Strong, and broader, but not necessarily stronger for motorsport |

**The three-unit plan.** Stretching the double degree to three units a semester frees time for the team, but it has costs. The degree takes noticeably longer, which means more years of fees and later full-time earnings. It may change your eligibility for some things, such as scholarships, part-time status for concessions, or visa rules if they ever apply. And a lighter load does not guarantee you will actually use the time for the team. I could not verify the rules, so check Monash's course-completion limits and your HECS-HELP position.

**What would make the double degree the right choice (it moves to #1 if):**
1. Your interest in finance is real and not just a fallback, meaning you would take finance even if you had no motorsport option.
2. You find after first-year engineering that you can hold a strong WAM and still commit to the team.
3. You are comfortable with the extra duration and cost.

**What would push you to single degree even more strongly:** a first-semester result that is weaker than you expected, or a heavy team role such as a lead position on the build.

**A practical way to keep both options open [Judgement, and a check is needed].** Start in the double degree or in engineering and check whether you can move between them after the first year or two without losing credit. If transfers are allowed, this lets you decide with real marks and real team experience, not guesses. Confirm this with Monash before relying on it.

## 6. How to judge individual subjects (without trusting me on codes)

Because I cannot confirm codes, here is how to check any subject, including MMA3101, in the Handbook. Ask: what is taught, what is assessed, and what does it let you do on a race car?

| Skill area | What to look for in the subject | Why it matters for motorsport |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics and numerical methods | Differential equations, linear algebra, numerical solutions | The basis for modelling suspension, tyre and powertrain behaviour |
| Dynamics and vibration | Rigid-body dynamics, vibration | Vehicle dynamics, ride and damper tuning |
| Control and data | Control systems, signals, data analysis | Data acquisition, traction and stability systems |
| Materials and solid mechanics | Stress, fatigue, composites | Safe, light structures and parts |
| CAD and design | Parametric modelling, drawing, tolerancing, design for manufacture | The daily tool for a race engineer or designer |
| Manufacturing | Machining, processes | Understanding what can be built, and at what cost |
| Fluids and thermodynamics | Fluid mechanics, heat transfer, engines | Aerodynamics and powertrain |
| Optimisation and computation | Programming, FEA, CFD, optimisation | Faster design iteration |

Skills are not all equal. **Direct engineering skill** is built by the maths, mechanics, design and manufacturing subjects plus team projects. **Transferable practical intuition** is built by workshop time, building and testing, and data logging. **Mere interest in motorsport** (watching races, knowing the teams) does not count for hiring on its own. Judge each subject by whether it gives the first or second.

- **Course-specific extras.** Some courses use a scholarship, a portfolio or an interview. Check whether any of the ones above do.
- **Cost.** Check whether each course is a Commonwealth Supported Place and what it costs per year.
