## The honest bottom line

Your instinct to question the double degree is right. For F1 and motorsport, what gets you interviews is **demonstrable technical work**: a Formula SAE role with real ownership, CAD portfolio pieces, manufacturing and testing experience, and strong engineering grades. A Commerce (Finance) degree adds almost nothing to that profile. It costs a year, plus about a quarter of your study time that you would otherwise put into the car, the team and your WAM.

Your fallback, finance, does not require a Commerce degree. Engineers move into finance (quant roles, consulting, engineering-heavy banking and corporate roles) regularly. The reverse move, from finance into engineering, is nearly impossible. So the asymmetry favours engineering first.

Where your plan is weak:

1. **"Flexibility" is being paid for with a year and a workload.** A double degree is only flexible if you would actually use the finance half. Be honest about whether that is a real second goal or anxiety insurance.
2. **Spreading to three units a semester is a trade, not a fix.** It protects your WAM and your team time, but it adds roughly a year of fees and delays your first graduate salary. HECS-HELP defers the cost; it does not remove it.
3. **Interest in motorsport is not a skill.** Watching F1 is not evidence. Teams hire on projects.
4. **Preference order matters less than you think.** You can almost always add Commerce later (see "Next steps"), but you cannot easily undo a double that has dragged down your engineering results.

## Ranked recommendation

### 1. Monash BE (Honours), Mechanical, single degree, with Monash Motorsport
- **Why first:** you are already looking here, and it has a long-running, well-known Formula SAE team (Monash Motorsport) that is one of the main reasons to choose Monash. It is in Melbourne, so you stay local. Mechanical is a direct entry, not a two-step.
- **How to get finance anyway:** use free electives or a minor if the handbook allows it. Check whether a finance, economics or business minor is available to engineering students, and which units count.
- **Key risk:** most of the value comes from joining and *staying* in the team. Check how the team recruits (usually first-year intake and onboarding) and apply in your first weeks.
- **What to check:** current selection rank, prerequisites (Methods, Physics), whether first-year engineering is common across streams so you can switch into or out of the double, and the exact rules for adding Commerce after year 1.

### 2. Monash BE (Honours) + Commerce (Finance), extended load
- **When it beats #1:** only if you genuinely want a finance career as a real option, not a safety net, and you are willing to go to about 5.5 to 6 years or accept a heavy load.
- **Cost:** extra year of fees, and a real risk to WAM and team involvement. If you take it, treat Monash Motorsport as non-negotiable and cut commerce load before cutting team time.
- **Verdict:** a defensible second choice, not the default. Decide at the end of first year with real results, not now.

### 3. University of Melbourne: Bachelor of Science (or Design) then Master of Engineering (Mechanical)
- **Strengths:** strong research and academic reputation, and a breadth-first structure that lets you keep options open. The 3+2 structure gives you an early exit with a bachelor's degree.
- **Weaknesses:** the pathway is longer and more abstract early on, hands-on engineering arrives later, and your motorsport team involvement starts in a less structured way. Verify the major and prerequisites you would need in the Bachelor to enter the Master's, because a wrong choice here can lock you out.
- **Verdict:** best option if you value breadth and a research brand more than early hands-on practice.

### 4. Swinburne: Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, with industry-based learning
- **Strengths:** known for a paid industry placement model. For employability and real industry exposure this can beat a purely academic path. It has a Formula SAE team (Swinburne Racing, verify).
- **Weaknesses:** lower academic brand than Monash or Melbourne in some employer eyes, and the placement is not guaranteed to be motorsport.
- **Verdict:** serious contender if you prefer work experience early and want to graduate with a paid year on your CV.

### 5. RMIT: Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical
- **Strengths:** practical, workshop-oriented culture and a long-standing Formula SAE team (RMIT Redback Racing, verify). City location and strong industry links.
- **Weaknesses:** same caveat as Swinburne on brand perception; verify the course structure and any placement requirement.
- **Verdict:** strong choice if hands-on skill is your priority.

### 6. Interstate options: UNSW, Sydney, UQ, Adelaide (and Deakin in Victoria)
- **UNSW, Sydney, UQ:** strong engineering schools with established Formula SAE teams (UNSW Racing, USyd and UQ Racing, verify names). They suit you only if you are willing to move. Moving costs rent and travel, and you lose the Melbourne motorsport industry cluster, so I would not rank them above the Victorian options unless a specific team or course convinced you.
- **Adelaide:** has a motorsport team and decent engineering; same relocation cost logic.
- **Deakin:** has a motorsport team (verify); a reasonable fallback in Victoria, and often more attainable on selection rank.
- **Overseas (UK, e.g. Oxford Brookes, Imperial):** not realistically justified at undergraduate level, because of fees (international rates, no HECS-HELP) and visa and recognition complexity. Revisit for a postgraduate degree after you have a strong WAM and team record, if F1 teams in the UK remain your target.

## Subjects and skills: what to look for (and why)

I cannot give verified codes, so here is what to search for in each handbook and the reason each matters:

- **Engineering mathematics and numerical methods:** the basis for simulation, optimisation and data analysis. Lap-time simulation and suspension kinematics are numerical problems.
- **Dynamics and mechanics of machines:** directly underpins vehicle dynamics, suspension geometry and load transfer.
- **Control systems:** traction control, engine management and active systems.
- **Materials and manufacturing:** composites, machining and design for manufacture. This is what turns a CAD model into a car that survives.
- **Engineering design and CAD units:** parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing and basic FEA. Pair them with team work to build a portfolio.
- **Thermodynamics, fluid mechanics:** the foundation for aerodynamics and powertrain.
- **Data and programming units:** data acquisition and analysis, which is where much modern race engineering lives.

How to read it: coursework gives you **direct engineering skill** in fundamentals. Formula SAE gives you **practical intuition** (manufacturing, testing, failing and fixing). Following F1 gives you **interest only**. Employers weigh the first two.

## What would change the ranking

- If your **final ATAR or selection rank misses Monash Engineering**, Swinburne or RMIT move up, and Melbourne's 3+2 may still be reachable.
- If you discover you **really want finance**, the double (#2) moves to the top.
- If you want a **paid placement** more than a big brand, Swinburne moves above Melbourne.
- If you are **offered a scholarship or a spot in an interstate team**, reassess the interstate options.
- If Monash does not allow adding Commerce after year one, the "decide later" strategy weakens and you should decide earlier.
