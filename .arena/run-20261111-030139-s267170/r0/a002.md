## 5. The double-degree question, answered directly

**The assumption in your plan:** that a Commerce (Finance) degree adds a meaningful finance pathway that engineering alone cannot provide. I think this is weaker than it looks. Engineers enter finance regularly (quantitative roles, consulting, engineering-adjacent finance), and employers in those roles often value the quantitative training more than the commerce credential. I have not verified placement data for this, so treat it as a reasoned view.

**Direct comparison on your own criteria:**

| Criterion | Engineering alone | Double degree |
|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Highest. All time goes to engineering. | Lower. Time is split. |
| WAM/GPA | Easier to keep high. | Harder, with more subjects and more variety. |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best. | Worst at a normal load. A slower load helps but extends the length of the degree. |
| Duration | 4 years | 5 years at normal pace, longer if spread out |
| Cost / HECS-HELP debt | Lower. | One extra year of fees and debt. Check current fees and indexation. |
| Finance flexibility | Via electives and later study. | Highest. |
| Opportunity cost | Lower. | An extra year of delayed full-time salary. |
| Employability for motorsport | Strong if the team and project record are strong. | Same, but only if you still get the team time. |

**The decisive point for your stated goal.** Motorsport employers hire on technical profile: a strong Formula SAE record, CAD fluency, manufacturing and data work. A commerce degree does not help there, and a thin team record hurts. So for a primary goal of motorsport, the single degree wins unless you are confident you will keep the team commitment under the double load.

**When the double degree wins.** If your motorsport goal weakens, or you want the finance option badly enough to pay for it, the double degree is reasonable. It also helps that, as I understand it, you can usually change course early on. Verify with Monash whether you can transfer between the single and double degree after first year, and how credit works. This would let you start with the double degree and drop commerce, or the reverse.

**The 3-units-per-semester idea.** Slowing the load frees weekly hours, but it lengthens the degree by roughly a year or more, adds cost, and delays your graduation into the job market. If team time is the real constraint, the single degree achieves the same thing without the extra years.

**Distinguishing three things:**
- *Direct skill development:* CAD, FEA, dynamics, machining and manufacture, built by doing in the team and in units.
- *Transferable intuition:* the feel for how a car behaves, which comes from time at the track and in the workshop.
- *Mere interest:* watching F1 and following the sport. It motivates you but employers do not count it.
