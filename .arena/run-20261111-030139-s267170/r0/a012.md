## The first problem to deal with: no Specialist Mathematics

Your subjects are Methods, Physics, Economics, Product Design and Technologies, and English. You are not doing Specialist Mathematics. This is an assumption in your plan that needs to be said plainly.

- First-year engineering maths (calculus, linear algebra, differential equations) builds on content that Specialist covers: complex numbers, vectors, and harder calculus. You can succeed without it, but the first semester is steeper.
- Many Victorian courses do not require Specialist, but they recommend it or offer bridging. `[CHECK]` each course's wording on prerequisites and bridging units.
- Practical consequence: before the start of 2027, work through a first-year university maths preparation course (Monash and others publish bridging material) and revise vectors and complex numbers.
- This weakness also affects the double-degree question, because a hard first semester combined with Commerce units makes it harder to protect your WAM.

Your strength is Physics plus Product Design and Technologies, which is genuinely useful for CAD and design-for-manufacture thinking. Economics helps with the finance side.

## Ranked options with reasoning

### 1. Monash, Engineering (Honours) Mechanical, single degree, plus finance electives

Why it is first:

- **Technical depth.** All of the course load goes to engineering. Over four years you can take the mechanics, dynamics, materials, manufacturing, control and design units that matter for motorsport without competing demands.
- **Formula SAE.** Monash Motorsport is one of the most established teams in Australasia. Realistic involvement means 10 to 20 hours a week in peak build season. A double degree on a standard load makes this hard. A single degree makes it sustainable.
- **WAM.** A high engineering WAM matters for graduate programs, scholarships and Honours-level opportunities. One degree makes it easier to protect.
- **Duration and cost.** Four years instead of five. You start earning a year sooner, and you carry less HECS-HELP debt (one fewer year of contribution, though check the current rate).
- **Finance flexibility.** Engineering plus a small commerce or finance minor/elective set keeps the finance door open, and finance employers hire engineers. `[CHECK]` the Monash Handbook for which commerce units can count as electives or a minor in your course.

Honest weakness: you get less formal finance education than a double degree. For an engineering-to-finance move, that is usually fixable later with a short postgraduate course or the CFA route, and many quantitative finance roles care more about maths and computation than a commerce major.

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance)

This frees Formula SAE time but extends the course to about six or more years and increases total HECS-HELP debt and the delay in earning. `[CHECK]` that part-time or reduced load has no effect on your eligibility or on HECS-HELP.

## What the finance pathway actually requires

You asked whether engineering alone gives a pathway into finance later. Yes, with limits:

- Engineering is a respected quantitative degree. Banks, consulting firms and asset managers hire engineers, especially for quantitative and analytical roles.
- Your Economics subject and a few finance units show interest and basic literacy.
- What engineering alone will not give you is a formal finance qualification. Competitive graduate finance programs often want a high WAM and evidence of finance interest (clubs, internships, a finance minor), not necessarily a commerce degree.
- If you later want the credential, a postgraduate course is available.

That is why the single-degree route with finance electives protects both goals at a lower cost.

## Subjects and skills (what to prioritise and why)

I could not verify Monash unit codes, so use the Handbook for the exact code. In general, favour these types of units, and here is how each helps a motorsport career:

- **Engineering mathematics and numerical methods:** the basis for simulation, optimisation and data analysis. Without it you cannot understand what FEA or lap-time simulation is doing.
- **Dynamics and mechanics:** vehicle dynamics (load transfer, suspension geometry, tyre behaviour) is applied rigid-body dynamics. This is the direct engineering skill.
- **Control systems and signals:** needed for traction control, active systems and data acquisition.
- **Materials and manufacturing:** composites, machining and design for manufacture are what a Formula SAE car actually needs, and what teams hire on.
- **CAD and engineering design:** parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings and tolerancing are best learned by doing real design projects, not only in class. Formula SAE is the best classroom for this.
- **Data analysis and programming:** Python or MATLAB for data acquisition and analysis. Teams value this highly and it also transfers to finance.
- **Thermodynamics and fluids:** powertrain and aerodynamics.

**MMA3101:** you mentioned this code. I cannot confirm what it is or whether it is a minor or elective. Open the Handbook entry and check the unit title, prerequisites, offering and whether it counts toward your course before you plan around it. Do not choose a course because of one subject code.

## Skill development: three different things

- **Direct engineering skill:** CAD, FEA, vehicle dynamics, manufacturing, composites. These come from coursework plus Formula SAE.
- **Transferable practical intuition:** machining, tolerancing and real test-and-fix experience. These come from workshops and building a car, not lectures.
- **Interest in motorsport:** watching F1 or following the sport helps motivation but is not a qualification. Employers see Formula SAE role, results and your portfolio.

## Overseas

Overseas undergraduate study (for example in the UK) is not realistically justified. Costs are high, you would not receive Commonwealth support, and Australian degrees with accreditation and Formula SAE are sufficient to enter the industry. An overseas Master's (for example in motorsport engineering) after you finish is a reasonable later option if you want to enter European motorsport. Decide that in your third or fourth year.
