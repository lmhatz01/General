## Question 1: double degree or single degree?

**My answer is that the single degree is better for your stated primary goal. The double degree is acceptable only if you pass a test you can check.** I am not assuming the double is better, and I'm not assuming it's worse.

### Why a motorsport hiring manager cares less about Commerce than you may think

Motorsport and F1 hiring is driven by:
- demonstrated hands-on engineering, usually via Formula SAE;
- technical depth in dynamics, materials, manufacturing, CAD and data analysis;
- strong fundamentals shown in your marks.

A Commerce degree adds almost nothing to that profile. It is also not needed for the finance fallback, as shown below.

### Does engineering alone give a pathway into finance? Yes, mostly.

- Engineering graduates, particularly with strong quantitative skills (Methods, mechanics, numerical methods, optimisation), do get into banking, consulting and quantitative roles. Many graduate programs accept any degree. **[Check this per employer, since some roles require specific coursework.]**
- You can add finance later: a postgraduate finance or MBA-style course, or professional study such as the CFA, which is open to people from any discipline. **[Check current CFA eligibility rules.]**
- The reverse direction (Commerce graduate into F1 engineering) does not work at all.

So the finance fallback is cheaper to preserve than the engineering primary goal is to rebuild. That asymmetry is the central reason to protect the engineering profile.

### Comparison on your own criteria

| Criterion | Single (Eng Hons) | Double (Eng Hons + Commerce) | Single plus finance electives or minor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Highest | Lowest, because the same time is shared with commerce units | High, with a small trade-off |
| WAM | Easiest to protect | Harder. Heavier load, two disciplines, and you get less time per unit | Moderate |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best | Worst, unless the load is stretched | Good |
| Duration | About 4 years full-time **[Check]** | About 5 years full-time **[Check]** | About 4 years |
| Cost (HECS-HELP) | Lowest | Highest. Extra year or more of fees, plus a delayed first salary | Close to single |
| Finance flexibility | Moderate (later study) | Highest and credentialed | Moderate to good |
| Employability in engineering | Strong | Neutral to strong. Not stronger for motorsport | Strong |
| Opportunity cost | Lowest | Highest | Low |

### The stretched double (three units a semester)

The idea of spreading the double degree at three units per semester is the weakest part of your plan if it is meant to protect Formula SAE time.
- At 75% load, a five-year degree takes about 6.7 years (5 x 4/3). That is roughly a year and a half to two years extra on top of the standard length. **[Arithmetic only; check Monash's maximum completion time and your eligibility to study part-load.]**
- It also changes things that depend on full-time status, such as some scholarships and government payments, and the timing of your HECS-HELP repayments and your entry into the workforce. **[Unknown. Check the rules that apply to you.]**
- Formula SAE is not a once-a-week commitment. A competing team member commonly puts in a very large number of hours per week in the build season. **[Check with the team.]** Cutting unit load helps only if the freed hours actually go into the team and into projects, rather than into more paid work or leisure.
- It also lengthens the time before you can apply for graduate roles.

If you want finance credentials without the cost, a **finance minor or electives inside the single degree** is likely the better compromise. **[Unknown whether Monash allows a Commerce minor inside the engineering course. This is the most important thing to check.]**

### Decision rules (what would change my mind)

Choose the **double** if all of these hold after you have checked them:
1. Your ATAR clears the double's entry rank comfortably (not marginally).
2. You can complete the Monash Motorsport commitment you intend to make in the same period, confirmed by speaking to current members who are also doing the double.
3. You have a specific finance career in mind, rather than "flexibility".
4. You are comfortable with the extra duration and cost.

Choose the **single** if any of these hold:
- you are unsure about finance as an actual career;
- you want the maximum WAM for a competitive technical graduate program or postgraduate application;
- you want maximum team time.

You can also **start in the single and decide on the double later.** Check whether Monash lets you transfer into the double in the first year, and how it treats units already completed. **[Unknown. Verify.]** Moving down from a double to a single is generally easier than moving up, but confirm the current rule before using this as a strategy in your preferences.

## Question 2: the ranked options

### 1. Monash, Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, single degree

Why it ranks first:
- **Team.** Monash Motorsport is one of the best-known Formula SAE teams in Australia and has a long record at the national event. **[Check current results, and whether the team currently builds combustion, electric or both.]** For your stated goals, joining and contributing to a team like this is worth more than almost any single subject.
- **Size and links.** Large engineering school with industry connections **[Check current industry placement and internship options]**.
- **Fit with your plan.** It is the same university you are already considering, so you lose nothing in momentum.
- **Entry.** Methods and Physics are the expected prerequisite area for mechanical engineering, and Specialist Mathematics is commonly recommended rather than required. **[Check exact prerequisites and the grade required for each.]** Not having Specialist will hurt most in first-year mathematics, so plan for it (see the next steps).
- **Cost and time.** Lowest total of the Monash options.

Weakness: it is a big cohort, so your WAM is compared with many strong students, and it will not give you formal commerce credentials.

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours) / Commerce (Finance), double degree

Rank 2 because it is a good degree, but it carries the highest cost and the highest risk to your primary goal. It makes sense only if you meet the decision rules above. If you list it, you should have a clear plan for the extra year and for protecting your team time.

### 3. University of Melbourne, BSc then Master of Engineering (Mechanical)

- Melbourne uses a three-year undergraduate degree followed by a two-year professional master's (so five years in total). **[Check the current structure, and which undergraduate majors lead to the Mechanical master's.]**
- Melbourne University Racing is a well-established Formula SAE team **[Check current status]**.
- Strengths: strong research reputation, breadth, and a master's-level qualification.
- Weaknesses for you: it delays the specialist engineering until the master's, requires a place in the master's, and is likely to be at least as hard to enter as Monash. **[Unknown. Check entry rank and how the MEng is entered.]**
- It is a serious alternative rather than a backup. It is rank 3 only because the single Monash route gets you into engineering sooner at lower cost.

### 4. Swinburne or RMIT, Mechanical Engineering (Honours)

- Both are known for applied, industry-linked programs, and Swinburne in particular for a paid industry placement year as part of some courses. **[Check whether it is compulsory, optional and paid, and for which course.]**
- Both have Formula SAE teams. **[Check current names, activity and results.]**
- These suit you if you value long, real placement experience over a brand name, which is a reasonable stance for motorsport.
- Entry ranks are generally lower than Monash or Melbourne. **[Unknown.]**

### 5. UNSW or UQ (interstate)

- Strong engineering schools with active Formula SAE teams. UNSW's team is generally called Redback Racing. **[Check.]**
- The cost, living away from home and loss of your Melbourne network count against them unless you have a specific reason.

### 6. Deakin and others

Deakin, and universities such as Adelaide, UWA, Sydney and UTS, all have engineering courses and student racing teams **[Check each]**. I rank them lower here only because I cannot give them detailed assessment without verified information, and they are not clearly better than the options above for you.

## Where your plan is weak

1. **"F1 engineering" is a very narrow target.** F1 teams are concentrated in the UK and Europe, and entry from Australia usually requires a strong portfolio, relevant experience and the right to work there. **[Unknown. Check visa rules.]** Have a realistic range: Australian motorsport and Supercars, automotive and engineering design, high-performance manufacturing and defence/aerospace all use the same skills.
2. **A double degree is not a substitute for Formula SAE.** It can in fact take time away from it.
3. **High WAM versus the team is a real trade-off.** Plan the balance before you start, because it is harder to fix later.
4. **No Specialist Maths** means you should expect first-year maths to take extra effort.
5. **I do not know your ATAR or your marks yet.** Everything above is conditional on that.
