## The core question: single or double degree?

**What the double degree actually costs.** A double degree keeps the full engineering program and adds commerce units on top. It does not hollow out the engineering content. So technical depth per se is not the problem. The real costs are:

- **One extra year** (about 5 years instead of 4) **[CHECK exact length on Monash's course page]**, so one more year of fees, HECS-HELP debt and delayed full-time pay.
- **Workload per semester.** Commerce units run in parallel with engineering labs and design projects. This is when a WAM slips.
- **Formula SAE time.** The team's heaviest workload (design freeze, manufacture, testing, competition prep) is exactly when assessments cluster. The double degree competes directly with this.
- **Opportunity cost.** A fifth year on finance units is a year not spent on internships, a thesis or team leadership.

**What it gives you.** Finance and business credentials, a clearer path into banking, consulting or commercial roles, and a signal to employers that you chose business deliberately. For a motorsport employer these are marginal. For a finance employer they matter more.

**Does engineering alone close the finance door?** No. Engineering graduates move into consulting, finance, quant-type and operations roles, and you can add a postgraduate qualification later (a graduate certificate or diploma in finance, or a Master of Finance or business degree, when you know you want it) **[CHECK admission rules and any credit]**. The reverse is not true: a commerce graduate cannot easily move into motorsport engineering. Keeping engineering as the core keeps both options open; adding Commerce first keeps one option cheaper but slightly less open.

**Verdict.** If motorsport is the primary goal, as you say, the single degree wins on the criteria you listed: it protects WAM, gives more time for Formula SAE, and gets you into the workforce a year sooner. The double degree is justified only if you honestly expect to pursue finance seriously, not as vague insurance. "Fallback" is usually cheaper to buy later than to buy now.

**Spreading the double over a longer period (three units a semester).** This is the weakest option. It keeps the double degree's cost and extends the debt period, and it frees time you could instead get by doing the single degree at full load. It may also affect Youth Allowance, Austudy or other support, which often require a minimum study load **[CHECK with Centrelink]**, and Commonwealth-supported place time limits **[CHECK]**. A lighter load can make sense in a hard semester. As a plan for the whole degree, it is not better than the single degree.

**What would change this answer:** you find you enjoy finance units in first-year electives; you discover a specific finance role you want; or your WAM stays high even with the extra load. Test it cheaply: do the single degree with one or two finance electives in year one and watch what happens.

## Ranked options

### 1. Monash single degree (Mechanical Engineering, Honours) and Monash Motorsport

- **Why first:** large engineering faculty, and **Monash Motorsport** is one of the better-known Formula SAE teams in Australia **[CHECK current team page and results]**. It is a student-run team, which means real design, manufacture and test work on a car. It sits at Clayton, within reach of home.
- **Technical fit:** the standard mechanical program covers mathematics, mechanics, dynamics, thermofluids, materials, design and control. Two codes I am fairly confident about: **ENG1005** (engineering mathematics) and **ENG1060** (computing for engineers) **[CHECK both]**. I have not verified other codes, so I do not list them.
- **Your subject MMA3101:** I cannot confirm what it is. Look it up in the Monash Handbook and check its prerequisites, whether your course lets you take it, and which area it sits in, before building a plan around it.
- **Finance later:** look in the Handbook for minors and elective space, including whether any commerce-faculty units are available to engineering students **[CHECK]**.
- **Entry:** a selection rank (ATAR plus adjustments) must be met. VCE prerequisites are likely Methods and possibly Physics **[CHECK on the Monash course page and VTAC guide]**. Specialist is probably not required, but you did not take it, so expect first-year mathematics to be faster than your VCE preparation.
- **Weakness:** the team is competitive. Joining takes effort, and the time commitment is large.

### 2. Monash Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (finance), standard pace

Everything in 1, plus Commerce. See the discussion above. Choose this over 1 if finance is a genuine second goal. Check whether the double degree allows a specific finance major, the total length, whether you can transfer into or out of it after first year, and whether the combined course has a different entry rank **[CHECK all]**.

### 3. University of Melbourne: Bachelor of Science then Master of Engineering (Mechanical)

- **Why third:** strong research, strong brand, and a student motorsport team (Melbourne Uni Motorsport) **[CHECK current name and activity]**. The Master's is the accredited engineering qualification.
- **Structure:** a 3-year bachelor's plus a 2-year master's (about 5 years) **[CHECK]**. You enter the bachelor's now and must meet progression requirements for the master's **[CHECK]**.
- **Concerns:** it is theory-heavy compared with RMIT or Swinburne. The longer path costs a year more than Monash single. The competitive progression requirement puts pressure on WAM, the very thing you worry about. Its breadth also helps finance, if you want to add that.
- **Prerequisites:** Methods is expected, and Physics is likely useful. Specialist may be recommended **[CHECK]**.

### 4. Swinburne: Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), with industry-based learning

- **Why here:** Swinburne's strength is paid industry experience, often a placement year **[CHECK structure and whether it is compulsory, optional or competitive]**. That is the most valuable item for a CV outside the team. It also has a student racing team, Swinburne Racing **[CHECK]**.
- **Trade-off:** the entry rank is generally lower than Monash or Melbourne **[CHECK]**, and the brand is less strong. A placement year extends the degree.
- **Best if:** your ATAR comes in below your Monash target, or you value practical experience over brand.

### 5. RMIT: Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical

- **Why here:** hands-on focus and Redback Racing, the student Formula SAE team **[CHECK]**. It has a city campus and industry connections **[CHECK]**.
- **Trade-off:** similar to Swinburne, a less prestigious research reputation. Check how its mathematics depth compares with Monash.

### 6. Others (only with a reason)

- **UNSW, University of Queensland, University of Sydney, Adelaide:** each has a Formula SAE team and a strong mechanical program, and UNSW Racing and UQ Racing have strong reputations **[CHECK]**. For a Victorian student the extra cost of moving (rent, travel) is large, and you would use a different application system (UAC, QTAC and so on, not VTAC) **[CHECK]**. A move is worth it only if you want to leave Melbourne or a specific program or team clearly suits you better. Adelaide's university structure is changing in 2026, so check which entity and course names apply **[CHECK]**.
- **Deakin:** has a mechanical engineering degree and composites research capability at Waurn Ponds (Geelong) **[CHECK]**, and a motorsport club **[CHECK name and activity]**. The location is the obstacle if you live in Melbourne.
- **Overseas:** not justified. International fees are very high, you would lose HECS-HELP access, and a good Australian degree plus a strong Formula SAE record is a recognised route. Revisit it only for a postgraduate degree or an exchange after you have a strong WAM.

## Cost and HECS-HELP

Most domestic places are Commonwealth-supported, and HECS-HELP lets you defer the student contribution as a loan. The contribution amounts depend on the units you take, the HECS-HELP rules (including indexation and any recent changes to debt or repayment) change, and a double degree has more total units over more years. I have not verified any figure **[CHECK on each university's fees page and the StudyAssist site]**. Fewer units each year lowers your yearly bill but not the total, and a longer degree adds a year of lost full-time income.
