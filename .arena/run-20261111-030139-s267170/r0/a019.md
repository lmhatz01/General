## The system, not just the ranking
Your decision has four coupled parts. Changing one moves the others.

- **Time is the binding constraint.** A year of Monash Motorsport work is roughly a part-time job in build season. A double degree consumes extra units in the same hours. Spreading it over about three units per semester frees hours, but it lengthens the degree and delays your first full-time salary. Each extra year costs fees (partly HECS-HELP debt) plus a year of foregone graduate pay.
- **WAM and technical depth trade off with workload.** Graduate-program shortlists (including motorsport-adjacent and Tier 1 supplier programs) often screen on WAM, but a strong team project is the differentiator for motorsport specifically. A double degree can lower WAM through load. A single degree plus team time raises hands-on skill but may also lower WAM if you overcommit to the team. Your safest profile is high-credit/distinction WAM plus 2 to 3 seasons on the team.
- **Finance can be added later; technical foundation cannot be cheaply added later.** Engineering graduates enter finance via graduate programs (quantitative roles, engineering consulting, corporate finance, commodities) or via a later master's (Master of Finance, MBA, CFA). The reverse, entering motorsport engineering from commerce, is very hard. So the asymmetry favours engineering first.
- **Selection into F1 is brutal regardless of degree.** Very few Australians reach F1 teams directly. Realistic motorsport outcomes include Supercars teams, Formula 2/3 and other categories, automotive OEMs and Tier 1s (e.g. in Victoria and overseas), and defence or aerospace. Plan the degree so the fallback outcomes are good too. This reduces the case for finance as insurance: an engineering degree plus team experience is already a strong fallback.

## Ranked options and justification

### 1. Monash Mechanical Engineering (single), with a finance add-on
- **Why:** Monash Motorsport is the central draw, and it is open to students from any Monash degree, so you do not need the double to join it. Monash has a large mechanical cohort, labs and workshops, and strong industry links. It is in Melbourne, so there is no relocation cost. A single degree gives the most hours for team work, CAD, manufacturing practice and WAM.
- **Finance without the double degree:** look in the Handbook for a Commerce or Finance minor or elective block available to engineering students, and for Monash's Engineering "Industry-based learning" options. Check the exact structure; I have not verified what is offered for 2027.
- **Choose it if:** motorsport is genuinely first and you want maximum technical depth and team time.
- **Risk:** you may feel you are giving up optionality. That is mostly illusory because of the asymmetry above. Many students also transfer into the double after year 1 if their WAM and interests suggest it. Confirm Monash's transfer rules.

### 2. Monash Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance)
- **Why it is second, not first:** the course is longer (typically around five years or more full time; check the exact length) and carries heavier load. Your own worry is correct: it risks WAM, team capacity and technical depth. The extra credential helps most if you are really drawn to finance careers (e.g. engineering-plus-finance in energy, infrastructure, quant or consulting).
- **Spreading it out:** three units per semester is feasible, and it is a legitimate way to protect WAM and team time. The cost is a longer degree and later earnings. Check how Monash treats part-time load for HECS-HELP and for the maximum completion time.
- **Choose it if:** after first-year exposure you still want a serious finance track, or you value a distinctive profile for engineering-management roles.
- **Plain statement:** for a student whose stated primary goal is motorsport engineering, the double is not automatically better. It is better only if finance is a real second goal.

### 3. UNSW Mechanical Engineering (Honours)
Strong engineering reputation and Redback Racing. Sydney means relocation and living costs. It also offers combined degrees. Rank it above others because of overall engineering strength, not because it beats Monash on motorsport access for you.

### 4. UQ Mechanical Engineering (Honours)
UQ Racing is a major Formula SAE program and a strong brand in the category. The cost is interstate relocation. Choose if you prefer a larger, more team-focused culture and are willing to move.

### 5. University of Melbourne (Bachelor of Science, Engineering Systems, then Master of Engineering, Mechanical)
Strong academic brand and a Melbourne base, with a Formula SAE team. It is a 3+2 structure (about five years total), which delays technical specialisation. Entry is typically highly competitive, so check the current selection rank. It ranks below Monash for this student because of the structure and later specialisation, not because of quality.

### 6. RMIT Mechanical Engineering (Honours)
Known for practical, industry-linked teaching and an active racing team. Good if you want a hands-on emphasis and a lower selection-rank barrier. Check its team and workshop access.

### 7. Swinburne Mechanical Engineering (Honours)
Industry-placement orientation (Swinburne has traditionally offered a professional placement model). This is worth checking if you want paid industry experience built in. Verify the current course structure and any team.

### 8. Deakin Mechanical Engineering (Honours)
A reasonable fallback with a motorsport-linked team. It ranks last mainly because of campus location, and the other options are stronger for your goals.

## Subjects to look for (by function, not code)
Find the Monash Handbook units for each of these and confirm what they cover:
- **Engineering mathematics and computing** (first-year maths, numerical methods, scripting in Python or MATLAB): the base for simulation and data analysis.
- **Mechanics, dynamics and vibrations:** vehicle dynamics, suspension kinematics and ride, and the physics behind lap-time modelling.
- **Solid mechanics and materials:** stress, fatigue, composites and chassis/component design, plus basic FEA.
- **Thermofluids and aerodynamics (fluid mechanics, CFD):** the basis for aero and engine cooling.
- **Machine design and CAD (parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing, design for manufacture):** the most visible skill team leaders ask for.
- **Control systems and instrumentation:** data acquisition, sensors, and traction/stability control.
- **Manufacturing and workshop practice:** machining, welding, composites. Much of this is learned in the team, not in class.
- **Optimisation and data analysis:** setup optimisation and telemetry analysis.
- **Powertrain and internal combustion or electrical machines:** choose according to the team's current direction (Monash Motorsport has run electric in recent seasons, so check).

**Skill type:** CAD, FEA, dynamics, controls and numerical methods are *direct* engineering skills. Machining, composites and workshop habits are largely *transferable practical intuition*, built best by the team. "Interest in motorsport" alone is not a skill and does not carry weight with employers.

## Entry and prerequisites (confirm all)
- Prerequisites: VCE Mathematical Methods and Physics (check the exact unit and study score requirements for each course). Specialist Mathematics is generally not required but can help. You are not taking it, and you cannot add it now, so confirm no course lists it as a hard requirement.
- Check each university's VTAC listing for any selection rank adjustments or access schemes you may qualify for (for example, Monash and others run adjustment-factor or equity schemes). Do not assume you qualify.
- Interstate courses use their own prerequisites and use ATAR-based selection through UAC or QTAC (UQ via QTAC, UNSW via UAC) with VTAC applicants handled separately; check how a Victorian student applies.

## Cost and HECS-HELP
- Commonwealth-supported places generally let eligible domestic students defer fees through HECS-HELP. Engineering and commerce student contributions differ by discipline. Check current amounts on each university's fee page, because I cannot verify 2027 rates.
- A longer degree means more fee-years in total, and more years of living costs. Interstate adds relocation.

## What would change the ranking
- You find, after a first-year trial, that finance interests you more than motorsport: move Monash double up to first.
- You are offered a place in a team or program outside Victoria that you really want (UQ Racing, Redback): move UQ or UNSW up.
- Your ATAR is lower than expected: RMIT, Swinburne, Deakin move up, and Monash single degree becomes a fallback to the transfer-in route (check rules).
- Monash Motorsport changes its membership or capacity: re-check.
- You want a research or academic path (for example, F1 aerodynamics or simulation): Melbourne's 3+2, or a later overseas master's, moves up.

## Weaknesses in your plan (plainly)
- The assumption that a double degree is better for flexibility is weak. It buys finance credentials at a high cost in time and technical focus.
- Finance can be reached later via graduate roles or a master's. Engineering alone is a legitimate pathway into finance.
- The biggest lever for motorsport employability is not the degree. It is team seasons, a documented project portfolio and a strong WAM. Joining the team early matters more than extra commerce units.
- Entry into F1 itself is highly uncertain. Plan for a good outcome across the wider motorsport and automotive industry.
