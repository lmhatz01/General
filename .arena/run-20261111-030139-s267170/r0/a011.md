# Best-suited university options for Lukas (VTAC 2027 entry)

## Bottom line

1. **Monash Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical specialisation, as a single degree, with finance added through electives and later study.** This is the best fit for a motorsport-first goal.
2. **Monash Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance) double degree.** Rank this second, as a conscious choice rather than a default. It is worth it only if finance is a real second career and you accept 5+ years. It is a poor choice if its main effect is to squeeze out Monash Motorsport.
3. **Swinburne Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, with the industry placement.** This is the strongest realistic alternative to Monash, and you can switch to it if the Monash offer does not come through.
4. **Melbourne: Bachelor of Science (mechanical-systems major), then Master of Engineering (Mechanical).** It has high prestige but is a longer, less hands-on route (3+2).
5. **RMIT Mechanical Engineering (Honours)** with Redback Racing.
6. **UNSW Mechanical Engineering (Honours)** or its Co-op program, with UNSW Racing, if you are willing to move to Sydney.
7. **UQ, Sydney, Adelaide or UWA** (strong teams, but you would be moving interstate for little gain over Monash), then **Deakin** (a sound fallback with its own motorsport team).
8. **Overseas:** not as an undergraduate. Consider a UK MSc or exchange later (see section 5).

**Double degree versus single degree, answered directly.** For F1/motorsport as the primary goal, the single engineering degree plus Monash Motorsport is better. Finance is reachable later from an engineering degree, whereas a thin technical profile is hard to repair. The double degree is not wrong, but it costs time, money, WAM risk and Formula SAE hours, and in return it buys credentials you can mostly get another way.

## 1. What matters, and the evidence behind it

The question is which environment builds a technical profile that F1, Supercars or advanced engineering employers recognise. Weighted roughly by importance, the factors are:

1. **Hands-on, team-based engineering output (Formula SAE).** Employers hire from FSAE teams because it is real design, manufacture, testing and data work. It is the best single signal and it is only partly a university choice, because every serious university in Australia has a team.
2. **Strength of the engineering fundamentals** (mechanics, dynamics, control, materials, numerical methods) and your grades in them.
3. **Industry placement and links**, and the quality of the networks around the course.
4. **Time and capacity.** Hours are the scarce resource, and the double degree consumes them.
5. **Flexibility** into finance, consulting or other fields.
6. **Cost, duration and location.**

Three honest points about your plan:

- **Interest in motorsport is not evidence.** Interest, race-watching and sim-racing count for little with employers unless they become projects and results. Direct skill development comes from design work, FSAE and coursework. Transferable practical intuition comes from machining, fabrication and driving or testing. Interest alone is a motivator and nothing more.
- **The institution matters less than what you do there.** Monash, Swinburne, RMIT and UNSW all produce mechanical engineers who reach motorsport. The difference is usually team involvement, project quality, placements and persistence. Do not pay a large premium (a longer degree, a move interstate) for a brand name.
- **"A double degree keeps every door open" is an assumption.** It keeps finance doors open at a cost. Section 3 shows the costs.

### Monash, Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance), double degree [Check all]

- **Duration:** typically five years full-time, which is one extra year over single [Check].
- **ATAR:** typically higher than single engineering, since two faculties set the bar [Unverified].

## 3. Single versus double degree: the explicit analysis

| Factor | Single (Eng Hons) | Double (Eng + Commerce) |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical depth** | Full elective space for advanced mechanical, vehicle and control units | Core retained, but fewer technical electives |
| **WAM/GPA** | One curriculum to focus on | Two curricula; commerce can lift or dilute WAM depending on your strengths. Your Economics background helps. |
| **Workload** | Heavy but manageable | Heavier at a normal load |
| **Formula SAE capacity** | Best | Squeezed unless you extend the degree |
| **Employability (motorsport)** | Strongest relative to hours | Marginal gain: finance adds little to an F1 application |
| **Employability (finance)** | Still possible (see below) | Strongest |
| **Internships** | More room for engineering placement | Must split time between engineering and finance placements |
| **Duration** | 4 years | 5 years at full load |
| **Cost** | Lowest | Extra year plus a higher commerce band |
| **Flexibility** | Good | Best |
| **Opportunity cost** | Low | An extra year of earnings foregone plus higher debt |
| **Graduate outcomes** | Engineering plus the option of finance later | Both, but you may be a generalist in each |

### The three-units-per-semester plan

This is the part of your plan I would challenge most. If a full-time load is four 6-credit-point units a semester [Check], then:

- Single, 32 units at three per semester is about 11 semesters, or roughly 5.3 years.
- Double, about 40 units at three per semester is about 13 to 14 semesters, or roughly 6.7 years.

You would pay extra years of living costs, and a part-time load can affect Youth Allowance eligibility and some scholarships [Check]. Slower pacing also delays your graduate start. It is a legitimate way to protect FSAE and WAM, but it is a costly one. It is worth it only if you will really use the freed time on engineering work. Compare it with the simpler option of the single degree at full load. **Confirm the actual unit counts and the part-time rules with Monash.**

### Can engineering alone lead to finance?

Yes, with caveats.

- Engineers are routinely hired into finance, consulting and quantitative roles because of their analytical training. Quant and risk roles value mathematics and programming, which your engineering degree supplies.
- Standard banking graduate roles do not need a commerce degree, but some value one. A later Master of Finance, a CFA or a business postgraduate is the usual bridge, and it costs less time than carrying a second degree from day one.
- The risk is that finance recruiting is early and competitive. If you want investment banking specifically, the double degree helps. If you want engineering-based finance (quant, energy, infrastructure), single engineering suffices.
- **Cheap hedge:** take finance electives or a minor if available. Join a finance or investment club. Do an internship in finance during a summer break. This gives you evidence of interest without a second degree.

### Verdict

**Do single engineering and add finance by electives and later study, unless you decide finance is a serious co-career.** The double degree is not irrational. It is simply the answer to a different goal (a flexible engineering-and-finance career) than the one you ranked first (motorsport engineering).

## 4. Costs, HECS-HELP and entry mechanics [Check all]

- **HECS-HELP:** as a domestic student you will probably have a Commonwealth Supported Place. The loan covers your student contribution, and repayment depends on income after you graduate, so there is no upfront cost. Student-contribution amounts differ by field, with commerce higher than engineering, and are indexed each year. Rules changed in 2025 (a one-off debt reduction and a new repayment structure) [Unverified]. Check studyassist.gov.au for the current figures.
- **Total cost difference:** the double is more expensive mainly because of the extra year and the higher commerce band, not because of any upfront fee.
- **Living costs:** include a longer degree in your budget. Staying in Melbourne avoids relocation costs.
- **Selection rank:** your ATAR plus any adjustment factors. Check the Monash and Swinburne pages, and the VTAC Guide, for how the rank is built and for prerequisites such as study-score minimums.
- **Specialist Maths:** I do not believe it is required for these courses, but I could not verify that. If a course recommends it, ask how first-year maths is taught for students without it.

## 5. Overseas, honestly

An undergraduate degree overseas (the UK is where most F1 teams are based) is not realistically justified for you. International fees are very high, you lose HECS-HELP, and a UK or US degree is not a prerequisite for F1 anyway. Teams recruit through Formula Student/FSAE results, placements and graduate roles.

A more realistic route is to finish an Australian degree with strong FSAE and a good WAM, then consider a UK postgraduate motorsport engineering master's or a graduate role. UK work-visa rules for graduates change often, so check them when you get there [Unverified]. An exchange semester is a cheaper way to test the international route.

## 6. What would change the ranking

- **Monash single drops to second or third:** if you do not get an offer, Swinburne or RMIT become first. If you find you want to live in another state, UNSW Co-op or UQ rise.
- **The double rises to first:** if you decide finance, such as investment banking, is your genuine first career, or if Monash confirms an easy path to reduce the double load without losing Monash Motorsport time.
- **Swinburne rises above Monash double:** if its placement structure fits and you value paid industry experience over brand weight.
- **Melbourne rises:** if you prefer a research or postgraduate path (for example, a PhD in vehicle dynamics or aerodynamics).
- **Your ATAR:** a lower-than-hoped result shifts the order toward Swinburne and RMIT, not toward a weaker plan.
- **Team culture:** if a team visit shows another university's team is more welcoming or better resourced, weight that heavily.

**January to first semester (2027)**
6. Accept the offer, enrol, and tell Monash about any scholarships or access schemes you applied for.

**Ongoing:** keep a portfolio (CAD, drawings, test data, team reports). Aim for an engineering internship by third year and for a finance exposure (a club or short internship) if you want to keep that option.
