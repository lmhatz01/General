## 1. Single degree or double degree?

The double degree is not automatically better here. I would rank the single degree first for a motorsport-first student, for these reasons.

**Technical depth.**
- Formula 1 and top-tier motorsport hiring is driven by engineering ability. That means projects, a strong grade profile in mechanics, and demonstrable hands-on design and build work.
- Commerce units add nothing a race team will screen for. They displace elective and project time instead.

**WAM and workload.**
- A double degree means two sets of assessment cultures. Engineering is problem-set and lab heavy. Finance is quantitative but exam and assignment based.
- A high WAM matters most for honours cut-offs, graduate programs and scholarships. It is easier to protect in a single degree.

**Formula SAE capacity.**
- This is the most important point. A student team is a very large time commitment, often 15 to 25 hours a week in build season, and it is where much of the real skill development happens.
- Spreading the double degree at about three units per semester frees time, but it stretches the degree to roughly 6 to 7 years. You would also be paying HELP debt for longer and entering full-time work later.
- The other way to read it is that three units per semester is just a part-time double degree. The Commerce half then competes with the team for the same hours.

**Employability and finance flexibility.**
- Engineering alone does keep a finance pathway open. Quantitative, consulting and finance-adjacent graduate programs routinely hire engineers. Postgraduate options (a Master of Finance, a Master of Business or an MBA after some work experience) are also open.
- The reverse is not true: finance study alone does not open an F1 engineering door.
- The cost is real. You give up the Commerce credential and its recruiting-pipeline signal for finance-specific graduate programs as a first-year-out graduate.

**Opportunity cost.**
- Roughly an extra year of full-time study, or the equivalent hours, to gain a credential you have rated secondary.
- If your fallback is a "plan B" rather than a real second career, a few finance electives and a later postgraduate degree achieve most of the value for much less time.

**HECS-HELP.**
- A longer degree means a larger HELP debt, and in the commerce half the student contribution band has been higher than in engineering. I could not confirm current band amounts.
- Check the current figures on the Study Assist website and the Monash fees page.
- Do not treat HECS-HELP as free money. It is debt that is repaid through the tax system once income passes the threshold.

**The case for the double.** It has option value. Many students decide they do not love engineering, or that finance pays better, or that they want the quant-trading route. The double degree hedges that. And I would not rule it out if your ATAR comfortably clears it.

## 2. The honest weaknesses in your plan

1. **No Specialist Mathematics.**
   - Methods plus Physics meets the usual prerequisites. First-year engineering maths is designed for that.
   - But Specialist helps with vectors, complex numbers and mechanics, and you will feel the gap in first-year calculus and linear algebra if you are not diligent.
   - Mitigation: do the university's bridging or foundation maths material over the summer break, and use first-semester maths as a place to be strong, not just to pass.
2. **Motorsport interest is not the same as skill.**
   - It helps motivation and networking, but it earns no credit with employers.
   - What counts is the direct skill development (CAD, FEA, manufacturing, data acquisition), then transferable practical intuition (building, breaking and fixing things), and only then interest.
3. **"F1 engineering" is extremely competitive.**
   - Very few Australian graduates enter F1 directly. Realistic routes are the following, and a degree plan should serve them all:
     - Formula SAE, then a UK motorsport company, or a master's or placement there.
     - Australian motorsport (Supercars teams and suppliers).
     - Adjacent high-performance engineering (automotive, aerospace, defence).
   - Choose a degree that is good for all of these, not one that only works if F1 happens.
4. **The ranking is partly about team quality, which I could not verify.** Formula SAE team results change from year to year.

## 3. Decision test for the double degree

Enrol in the double only if all of these are true. Otherwise take the single degree.

- You are confident you want a finance career, not just a safety net.
- Your ATAR clears the double comfortably, since its cut-off is typically higher.
- You are prepared to carry a reduced load and graduate later.
- You will still commit to Formula SAE in first or second year.

VTAC lets you list both Monash courses. Putting the double first can work as an option-preserving move, because dropping Commerce later is usually easier than adding it. I could not verify Monash's current transfer rules and grade requirements. Confirm them before you rely on this. If you do this, set a firm decision point at the end of first year based on WAM and team workload.

## 4. Ranked options in detail

### 1. Monash, Engineering (Honours), Mechanical (single degree)
- **Why it ranks first:** the largest engineering school in the country, strong industry links in Melbourne, and Monash Motorsport, which is a long-established team with both combustion and electric history. It is close to home, and it is the university you already know.
- **Skills fit:** the Mechanical specialisation covers mechanics, dynamics, thermodynamics, materials, design and manufacturing. Add numerical methods and control as electives.
- **Finance:** take business or economics electives where the course structure allows, and keep Economics as a strength. Postgraduate finance later if you want it.
- **Check:** current course page, entry prerequisites, the final-year project options, and the industry-based learning or internship options.

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours) and Commerce (Finance)
- Same engineering strengths, plus Finance, at the costs set out in section 1. It ranks second because it dilutes the thing you said matters most, not because it is a bad course.

### 3. University of Melbourne, BSc (Engineering Systems) then Master of Engineering (Mechanical)
- **Strengths:** top academic brand, Melbourne University Motorsport, and strong access to finance and consulting recruiters. The master's is where the specialised mechanical study sits.
- **Costs:** 5 years in total, the 3+2 structure puts the engineering depth late, and the master's entry is conditional on grades. Check the current prerequisites, including whether Specialist is recommended.
- **Rank:** it beats the others on brand and finance access, and trails Monash on hands-on mechanical focus and the directness of the path.

### 4. Swinburne, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours)
- **Strength:** the industry-placement model gives paid, structured work experience during the degree. That is a direct, tangible edge for employability.
- **Check:** the current Mechanical offering, the placement structure, and team activity (Swinburne has a racing team).

### 5. RMIT, Engineering (Honours), Mechanical
- Practical orientation, strong industry connections, Redback Racing. Check the work-integrated learning requirements and team status.

### 6. Deakin
- Deakin Motorsport exists, and Deakin has a strong applied-engineering reputation. The Geelong campus affects the commute and the lifestyle. Check the course and the campus.

### 7. Interstate
- UQ (UQ Racing), UNSW (Redback Racing) and Adelaide (Adelaide University Motorsport) have strong teams. Moving costs money and takes you away from the Victorian industry network. Only consider it if the team or the course gives a clear advantage.

## 5. What would change the ranking

- **ATAR lower than hoped:** shift weight to Swinburne, RMIT and Deakin, and keep Monash single as a first preference if it is still reachable.
- **ATAR very high and you want the brand:** move Melbourne up to second or first.
- **You decide finance is the real first choice:** the double, or Melbourne, moves up.
- **A team you are keen on has a bad season or loses its facilities:** re-check before you commit.
- **A scholarship or an industry-sponsored place appears:** it can outweigh the ranking.

## 6. Subjects and skills to look for (by topic, not code)

When you read the Monash handbook, look for these units and write down the codes yourself.

- **Mathematics and numerical methods:** these give you the modelling toolkit used for simulation and optimisation, which race teams use for lap-time and setup analysis.
- **Dynamics and vibration:** the direct basis of vehicle dynamics, suspension kinematics and damping.
- **Control:** powertrain and active-systems calibration, and data-driven tuning.
- **Materials and solid mechanics:** structural design, FEA and composites, which are central to chassis and aero parts.
- **Manufacturing and design:** DFM/DFA, tolerancing, machining and CAD. This is the area where Formula SAE does most of the teaching.
- **Fluid mechanics and thermodynamics:** the aerodynamics and powertrain foundations.
- **Data analysis and programming:** data acquisition and post-processing.

For MMA3101: I could not confirm what it is. Open its handbook entry, read the description, and ask yourself which of the topics above it feeds. If it is not on that list, it is probably not a priority.
