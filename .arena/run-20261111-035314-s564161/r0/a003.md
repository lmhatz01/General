## Assumptions

- You want a strong ATAR but I do not assume a number. Rankings below hold across "high 80s to high 90s"; the one place ATAR matters for the ranking (Melbourne, UNSW, Sydney) is flagged.
- Staying in Melbourne is the default (cost, Formula 1 industry in Victoria is thin, but Victoria has the strongest motorsport engineering cluster in Australia: V8 Supercars/Triple Eight-type suppliers aside, Monash Motorsport, RMIT, Swinburne and local composites/automotive suppliers).
- "F1 engineering" is realistically a UK/European move after a degree. Australian universities place graduates there mainly via Formula SAE results, a strong honours transcript and a Master's/internships abroad; it is not a degree-selection problem alone.

## The attacks on your plan (answered up front)

1. **"Double degree = better" is an assumption.** It is not. The Commerce half adds roughly a year (5 vs 4 years) and about 25 percent of your credit points to units that F1 teams and motorsport suppliers will never read. For the primary goal it is pure opportunity cost; its value is as a hedge.
2. **Spreading it over about three units a semester is the weakest idea in the plan.** At 3 of the usual 4 units, a 5-year course takes about 6.5-7 years. That is two extra years of fees/HECS-HELP debt (check: Commonwealth Supported place time limits and the indexation of HECS-HELP), two fewer years of earnings, and you would graduate a full cohort behind peers who did Formula SAE at the same pace. It is better to keep a full load and drop the commerce component than to dilute everything.
3. **Formula SAE is the real credential, not the degree title.** Team-leadership hours, a car that ran at the Australasian event, and ability to machine, laminate and debug will outweigh the extra degree. A double degree makes the 15-20 hours a week in the workshop harder, exactly when exams collide.
4. **"Engineering alone can't lead into finance" is false.** Engineers move into finance (quant/analyst, graduate programs at banks, consulting, the CFA path, a later MBA/Master of Finance). A finance Master's later costs less in lost technical years than a concurrent degree, and by then you will know whether you want it.
5. **Interest in motorsport is not a skill.** Watching F1 and knowing the sport is interest. Parametric CAD, FEA sanity-checking, machining and a documented design is direct skill. Driving or karting gives transferable intuition only. Rank courses on direct skill.

## Ranking

### 1. Monash, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical Engineering specialisation, with finance as electives/an extra minor-type sequence if the structure allows (4 years)

Why first:
- **Monash Motorsport is on your doorstep and is among the strongest teams in Australia.** It runs both an IC and an EV car, has an international record (Formula Student Germany presence is in its listed entries) and a long industry-sponsor list. This is the single biggest lever for your stated goal.
- **Four years at a full load** keeps WAM high, which matters for honours classification, graduate programs, and any later Master's application abroad.
- **Technical depth.** Core mechanical content to use deliberately (codes recall; verify): first-year common ENG1005 (engineering mathematics), ENG1060 (computing for engineers, programming and numerical methods), ENG1014 (mechanics), ENG1011/ENG1013 (design); then the mechanical core of solid mechanics, dynamics, thermodynamics/fluid mechanics, materials, control and design of machine elements; then the final-year project. Choose electives toward: vibrations/dynamics and control (vehicle dynamics, suspension kinematics), computational methods/FEA (stress and aero), materials (composites), manufacturing, and CFD/aerodynamics if offered. Pair them with real Formula SAE work: team subsystems apply these almost one-to-one (suspension geometry = kinematics; data acquisition = signals/control/numerical methods; aero = fluids/CFD).
- **Finance stays open.** Use up to your elective allowance on introductory finance/economics (your VCE Economics helps), and check the Monash rules on switching into the double degree after first year (check: whether and when this is allowed, and whether first-year results are needed). That preserves the option cheaply.
- Prerequisites (check on the Monash page and VTAC): Units 3/4 English, Mathematical Methods and typically Physics or Chemistry. You are covered; Specialist Maths is not what I recall being required, but it helps; you do not have it, so confirm it is not a hidden requirement.

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours) / Commerce, Finance major (5 years, full load)

Pick this only if you will sincerely commit to finance as a co-equal outcome, or you want the hedge badly enough to pay the extra year. If you take it:
- Do **not** stretch the load to three units. Take the standard load, and use the long summer terms (check availability) for engineering placement/Industry-based learning rather than for Commerce.
- Cap your Formula SAE commitment realistically: sub-team member in years 1-2, lead in a later year only if WAM allows.
- Expect a more fragmented technical profile. Counter it with elective choices in the engineering half exactly as in option 1.

Why second, not first: the extra year and extra fees buy a finance credential you can acquire later with a Master of Finance (or a short CFA route) at a point when you know you want it; the same year spent on a motorsport team or an overseas Master's is worth more to the primary goal. It ranks above the other universities because it keeps the Monash Motorsport advantage and the hedge.

### 3. RMIT, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical (4 years) (check exact course name and code on RMIT and VTAC)

Strengths: very practical, strong industry links (aerospace/automotive in Victoria), a long-running Formula SAE team, and a reputation for hands-on labs and work-integrated learning. Weak points for you: smaller research depth than Monash/Melbourne and, in my recollection, fewer elite-team alumni at F1 level; verify the team's recent competition results on its site. Best choice if your ATAR misses Monash or you want a stronger hands-on feel.

### 4. Swinburne, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, with the Professional Placement (about 5 years including a work year; check)

Swinburne's built-in industry year is the real draw; a paid year at an automotive or manufacturing employer is concrete evidence of capability. It has a Formula SAE team (Swinburne Racing; verify). Technical prestige is lower than Monash.

### 5. University of Melbourne (Bachelor of Science/Engineering Systems major then Master of Engineering (Mechanical); 3+2 = 5 years)

Excellent academic reputation and strong mathematics/modelling, but the 3+2 structure, higher selection rank and a team that I cannot confirm is as strong as Monash's make it a worse fit. Worth it only if you value the Melbourne brand or a research track more than Formula SAE.

### 6. Deakin (Mechanical Engineering Honours; check) and other Victorian options

Deakin has a motorsport/racing team and Geelong-based manufacturing links (advanced materials/Carbon Nexus is a real strength for composites; verify current offerings). A reasonable safety option.

### 7. Interstate: UQ, UNSW, Sydney, Adelaide

- **UQ** (UQ Racing is consistently among the top Formula SAE teams in Australia), **UNSW** (Redback Racing; check which university runs it), **Sydney** (Sydney Uni Formula SAE) and **Adelaide** (Adelaide University Motorsport; note Adelaide has changed institutional structure recently, check) all offer strong mechanical engineering. They rank below Monash because you would pay relocation costs for no clear technical gain; Monash's team and course already sit at the top tier. Consider UQ only if you want the best Formula SAE team reputation and are willing to move.

### Overseas

Not justified for undergraduate entry: cost, visas and no HECS-HELP. The realistic overseas play is a **Master's or graduate job in the UK/Europe after an Australian honours degree** with Formula SAE experience (and e.g. a placement). Treat that as year 4-5 planning, not a VTAC decision.

## Single vs double: the answer

**Do Monash Engineering (Honours) alone, with finance electives, unless you can honestly say you would be satisfied with a finance career.** The primary goal is motorsport engineering; the double degree adds a year, cost and a lower WAM risk, in exchange for a hedge you can buy later. The part-time spread (3 units a semester) is worse than either choice. If you do decide on the double, take it at full load.

## What would change the ranking

- Monash's published course structure turns out not to allow finance electives or minor study, or switching to the double later: raises option 2.
- You discover finance interests you cannot imagine giving up: option 2 first.
- You miss the Monash entry or its engineering selection: RMIT then Swinburne first.
- You want a research/PhD track: Melbourne rises.
- Monash Motorsport's recruitment is restricted for first-years (check): treat that as a reason to apply to its recruitment window as early as possible, not as a ranking change.
