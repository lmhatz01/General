# Best-suited university options for Lukas (2027 entry)

## 1. The short answer

1. **Monash, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours) in Mechanical Engineering, single degree, with finance-flavoured electives.** Join Monash Motorsport from first year.
2. **Monash, Engineering (Mechanical) + Commerce (Finance) double degree, taken at a normal load.** This is a close second, not a loser. Take it only if you accept the conditions in section 5.
3. **Swinburne, Mechanical Engineering (Honours) with its industry-placement option.** This is the best Victorian alternative if you want paid, structured industry experience built in.
4. **University of Melbourne, Bachelor of Science (or Design) followed by Master of Engineering (Mechanical).** It is a strong brand with a strong racing team. Choose it if prestige and the finance door matter more than a direct four-year engineering degree.
5. **UQ, UNSW, Sydney and RMIT** as interstate or local fallbacks, in that order of "only if the location suits".
6. **Deakin and Adelaide** are credible but lower priority for your stated goals.

**The plan's weak point.** The double degree is not automatically better, and your own list of concerns is right. For F1 and motorsport, hiring teams look at what you built and how well you understand the engineering. A Finance major is invisible to them. The same goes for most of the things people assume matter, such as a second degree or an extra year.

## 3. What matters most for the motorsport goal

I separate three kinds of value, because they are not equal.

| Kind | What it is | Examples | Weight for F1 / motorsport |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct engineering skill | Things you can demonstrate and be tested on | Dynamics, materials, CAD, FEA, controls, manufacturing, Formula SAE design work | Highest |
| Transferable practical intuition | Skills that carry over but are not motorsport-specific | Workshop time, data analysis, teamwork, documentation, project management | High |
| Interest in motorsport | Enthusiasm, following the sport | Watching F1, knowing the teams | Low. It gets you the interest, not the job. |

Finance sits mostly in the second group. Formula SAE sits mostly in the first. That is why the ranking leans toward protecting time for the team.

## 4. Option-by-option comparison

### Monash (Clayton) – Mechanical Engineering (Honours), 4 years
- **Team:** Monash Motorsport, one of the largest and most established Formula SAE teams in Australia. It is on campus and well integrated with the faculty.
- **Why it ranks first:** it keeps the Formula SAE door open, gives a full four-year engineering degree, and lets you add finance through electives and a minor-type study.
- **Prerequisites (verify):** VCE Mathematical Methods, and Physics is normally expected. Specialist Maths is usually not required, but check the current course page.
- **Course structure (verify):** a common first-year engineering core in mathematics, computing, design and physics, then mechanical-specific units. I recall unit codes such as ENG1005 (engineering mathematics) and ENG1060 (computing), but treat these as recalled, not verified.
- **MMA3101:** I could not verify what this subject is or whether it is relevant. Open the Monash Handbook entry for it and check its content, prerequisites and the semester it runs.

### Monash – Engineering (Mechanical) + Commerce (Finance), 5 years
- **Duration:** typically one year longer than the engineering degree alone. If you drop to 3 units a semester as you plan, expect more than five years in total, which delays your first full-time salary.
- **Benefit:** a formal Finance major, recognised by finance employers, plus a Commerce network.
- **Cost:** a heavier timetable and more time away from the team and workshop. This is the central trade-off.

### Swinburne – Mechanical Engineering (Honours)
- **Strength:** a well-known industry-based learning model with a work placement year. Verify the current structure and whether the placement is paid.
- **Team:** Swinburne has a Formula SAE team. I have not verified how large or competitive it is currently.

### University of Melbourne – Bachelor of Science/Design, then Master of Engineering (Mechanical)
- **Structure:** Melbourne uses a "3 + 2" model. The Master is the professional engineering qualification, so the full route is about five years.
- **Team:** Melbourne University Racing (MUR) is a strong Formula SAE team.

### UQ, UNSW, Sydney

## 5. The central question: double degree or single degree?

**My answer: do the single Mechanical degree first, add finance through electives, and treat the double degree as the second choice.**

| Factor | Engineering only (with finance electives) | Engineering + Commerce |
|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Highest | Same core units, but less spare capacity to go deeper |
| WAM / GPA | Easier to protect | Harder, because workload is spread across two disciplines |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best | Tighter. Possible, but you give up sleep or grades |
| Duration | About 4 years | About 5 years or more |
| Cost / HECS-HELP | Lower total debt | One extra year of fees and delayed earnings |
| Finance flexibility | Good (electives, later Master's, graduate programmes) | Best, with a formal major |
| Employability, motorsport | Strongest | Neutral. The Commerce part does not help here. |
| Employability, finance | Reasonable. Many finance firms hire engineers for quantitative roles. | Strongest |
| Opportunity cost | Low | A full extra year, plus reduced team involvement |

**Reasoning.**
- **Your primary goal is motorsport.** Employers hiring for that look at your Formula SAE role, your design and manufacturing evidence and your engineering results. Finance study does not compete on any of those.
- **Engineering alone does give a pathway into finance later.** Engineers with strong mathematics and modelling skills are hired into quantitative analysis, consulting and some banking graduate programmes. You can also add a Master of Finance or similar later, or pursue professional finance credentials such as the CFA. I have not verified current graduate programme intake rules, so check them.
- **The assumption to challenge.** You may be assuming the double degree keeps both doors fully open. It keeps the finance door more open but narrows the motorsport door in practice, because time is finite.

**When the double degree is the right call** (this is what would change my ranking):
- You decide finance is as likely a career as motorsport. Then option 2 should move to first.
- You are confident you can keep a strong WAM and a real Formula SAE role at the same time. Test this in first year before committing.
- You can transfer into the double degree later at your university. Check whether Monash allows adding or removing Commerce after first year, and the deadline. I could not verify this.

**A practical route that keeps both options open:** enrol in the single degree, perform well in first year, and ask the faculty in writing about adding Commerce later. Also ask whether commencing students can enrol in the double and drop Commerce if workload is too high. Whichever direction is easier at Monash should guide your VTAC order.

**On three units per semester:** a reduced load stretches the degree and increases total fees and time before full-time work. It protects marks and team time, but it delays graduation. If you do this, plan it deliberately (for example, a lighter load only in the semesters you lead a sub-team) rather than by default.

## 6. Which subjects to choose, and why each one helps

Use the Monash Handbook (or the equivalent for any other university) to find the actual unit codes. I name the topic and its motorsport payoff. Search the handbook for these topics, and do not trust any code I have not verified.

| Topic | How it contributes to motorsport |
|---|---|
| Engineering mathematics and numerical methods | The language of every simulation. Needed to understand lap-time models and solver behaviour. |
| Dynamics and vibrations | Suspension, tyre response, ride and handling. The core of vehicle dynamics. |
| Mechanics of materials and solid mechanics | Sizing wishbones, uprights, chassis tubes and composite layups so they are light but safe. |
| Control systems | Traction control, engine management, active systems, and data-driven tuning. |
| Thermodynamics and fluid mechanics | Powertrain, cooling and aerodynamics. |
| Engineering design and CAD | Parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing, design for manufacture and assembly. This is the daily work in a team. |
| Manufacturing and materials | Machining, composites, welding, and knowing what can actually be made. |
| Computational modelling and FEA | Checking designs before building. Learn the limits of the software, not only the buttons. |
| Optimisation and data analysis | Choosing trade-offs and interpreting data acquisition logs. |
| Programming (MATLAB / Python) | Needed for analysis, logging and scripting in almost every motorsport role. |

**Finance electives that cost the least technical time:** one or two units in economics, financial accounting or introductory finance. You already study Economics in VCE, so you will start with an advantage.

## 7. Most important thing: Formula SAE

- Join the team in your **first year**, even in a small role. Early involvement builds responsibility that later turns into sub-team leadership.
- Pick a sub-team that develops your target skills: suspension, chassis, aerodynamics, powertrain, or data acquisition.
- Document what you do: CAD files, drawings, test results and design reports. This is your portfolio.
- If you choose a university for its team, check how many first-years the team accepts and how it selects.

## 11. What would change this ranking

- **Finance becomes the main goal:** the double degree or Melbourne moves up.
- **A university offers a paid motorsport placement or sponsorship:** it moves up regardless of brand.
- **Monash does not allow flexible movement between the double and single degree:** you must choose at the start, so weigh your confidence in motorsport as the main goal.

- **You want an overseas career in F1:** a strong Australian engineering degree plus Formula SAE is still the base. A UK Master's later is the realistic overseas step.
