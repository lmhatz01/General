## The question you asked: double degree or single degree?

I will argue both sides at full strength, then give the synthesis.

**Strongest case for the double degree.** Finance is your explicit fallback. A Commerce (Finance) major gives you a recognised credential, not just a few electives, and it keeps doors open to banking, consulting and engineering-management roles. Motorsport is a tiny, brutally competitive market; a person who is "an engineer who also understands money" has options if F1 does not happen, and the budget cap era in F1 makes cost/finance literacy more relevant to teams than it used to be. At Monash you are already in the right faculty ecosystem, and one extra year is a modest cost against a 40-year career.

**Strongest case against it.** F1 and top-tier motorsport teams hire on demonstrated technical depth: strong grades in core engineering, a portfolio, and above all Formula SAE leadership and real build experience. The double degree attacks all three: it adds a full year of commerce content that does not count towards that profile, it dilutes your engineering WAM through a heavier, more varied load, and it eats the hours Monash Motorsport needs, which are large and concentrated. The finance fallback is also weaker than it looks: finance employers care about the university brand, WAM and internships more than about having the Commerce parchment, and engineers with strong quantitative skills move into finance routinely without it (graduate quant, consulting, analyst and engineering-finance roles). So the double degree buys flexibility you can often get cheaper, and costs you the thing your primary goal rewards.

**Synthesis.** The two arguments agree on one fact: the binding constraint is your time and attention, not your options. The double degree is a good product for someone with no strong primary goal. You have one. So:

- **Default to single-degree Engineering (Honours).** Protect WAM and Formula SAE hours. Take finance-flavoured study as electives (economics, accounting/finance units where your course allows) or a minor-type sequence. You already study Economics in VCE, so you start with a base.
- **Keep the double-degree door open, because switching is usually possible.** Confirm with Monash whether you can add Commerce after first year or two (check: transfer rules, credit, and whether the extra units are still Commonwealth-supported). If so, you do not need to decide this in November; you decide after seeing your first-year results and how much time Monash Motorsport actually takes.
- **Choose the double only if** (a) you are genuinely 50/50 between engineering and finance careers, or (b) you accept that you will be on a 5- to 6-year timeline with a slower, lighter engineering load and a likely lower engineering-only WAM, and you will still make the team's schedule work.
- **The "three units per semester" plan** (stretching the double to roughly 7 years or more at part-load) is the weakest version. It lowers weekly pressure but delays graduation, delays your first graduate salary, adds interest-free but indexed HELP debt, and does not fix the core problem: more total study years in front of employers who compare you with peers who graduated at 22 with two years of Formula SAE. I would only do it for a documented reason such as health or heavy team leadership, and I would then rather reduce load in the single degree.

Does engineering alone give a pathway into finance later? Yes, with caveats. Graduate programs in banking, consulting and quantitative roles regularly take engineering graduates, particularly those with high WAM, strong maths/modelling skills and an internship. A postgraduate Master of Finance or similar later is a standard route if you want the credential after a few years of work. What engineering alone does not give is automatic access to finance recruitment; you must apply deliberately and build that profile through electives, an internship and networking. Your plan is weak on one assumption: that finance needs to be "insured" up front. It mostly does not.

## What matters for F1 and motorsport (and what does not)

Separating three things so you do not confuse them:

- **Direct engineering skill development:** mechanics, dynamics, control, materials, CAD/CAE, manufacturing, numerical methods, data analysis, design projects, Formula SAE build and test.
- **Transferable practical intuition:** machining, composites layup, wiring, fault-finding, test-day discipline, working to a deadline with a team. Mostly gained in Formula SAE and workshop time, not lectures.
- **Mere interest in motorsport:** watching races, knowing the sport. It helps motivation and networking, and it earns you nothing in a selection process by itself.

Teams and suppliers hire on the first two. Honest note: F1 itself is extremely hard to enter directly from Australia; most Australians who get there go through UK-based teams after a Masters or strong Formula SAE and often a UK internship, or through Supercars, Motorsport Australia categories, tier-one suppliers, or adjacent industries (aerospace, defence, automotive R&D, EVs). The realistic strategy is a high-quality engineering profile with Formula SAE, then target the broad "high-performance engineering" market, with F1 as the stretch outcome.

## Subject-level advice for Monash mechanical (all codes: check in the current handbook)

I cannot confirm specific codes from here, including MMA3101, so I describe categories and what each does for you; verify codes in the Monash handbook and the Mechanical specialisation's course map.

| Subject area | Why it matters for motorsport |
|---|---|
| Engineering mathematics and numerical methods | Differential equations and linear algebra underpin vehicle dynamics, vibration and control; numerical methods are how simulation tools actually work. |
| Computing/programming for engineers (MATLAB/Python) | Data acquisition analysis, lap-time simulation, optimisation scripts. Probably the highest-return skill for a race engineer role. |
| Statics, dynamics and mechanics of solids | Suspension loads, chassis stiffness, component sizing. |
| Fluid mechanics and thermodynamics | Aero fundamentals, cooling, engine and powertrain cycles. |
| Materials | Composites, metals, fatigue; decides what you can make light and strong. |
| Engineering design and CAD units | Parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings, GD&T/tolerancing, design for manufacture. Formula SAE is where you get these at depth. |
| Manufacturing/mechatronics/control | Machining processes and sensor/control systems; control is central to modern powertrain and vehicle systems. |
| Optimisation and data analysis electives | Setup optimisation, telemetry, test-data interpretation. |
| Vehicle dynamics / powertrain / aero electives (usually later-year) | The direct motorsport content; check availability each year. |
| Finance/economics electives (if allowed) | Cost modelling, budgeting, business cases; relevant to cost-capped racing and a finance fallback. |

Rule of thumb for choosing electives: pick the subject that makes you better at modelling and measuring a physical system, over one that is merely "motorsport-themed" and light on mathematics.

## Entry, prerequisites and cost: what to check

- **Prerequisites:** engineering at these universities typically requires VCE Mathematical Methods and often Physics (check each course page; do not assume). Specialist Maths is usually recommended or gives a strong head start, not required, at most Victorian courses; confirm per course. If Specialist is recommended, plan for bridging content in first-year maths and use any university bridging resources over summer.
- **Selection rank and adjustments:** your selection rank is ATAR plus any adjustment factors (for example, subject bonuses, equity or regional schemes). Rules and eligibility change yearly; verify on VTAC and each university's pages. Do not rely on last year's cut-off as a guarantee.
- **Duration:** single degree 4 years (full-time, Honours); double degree 5 years; Melbourne route 3 + 2. Part-time stretches extend this.
- **Cost:** Commonwealth Supported Places are charged a student contribution that varies by discipline and year; engineering and commerce bands differ. HECS-HELP defers payment and is indexed. Check the current-year student contribution amounts and your eligibility on the university and studyassist.gov.au pages. A longer degree means more years of contribution and a later start to full earnings.
- **Accreditation:** confirm that the specific degree is Engineers Australia accredited for the specialisation you choose.
- **Formula SAE team:** confirm recruitment timing, whether first-years can join, expected weekly hours, and whether the team requires prior skills.
