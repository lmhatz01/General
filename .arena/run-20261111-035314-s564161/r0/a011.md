## 2. The question you asked: double or single?

**Plain answer: for a motorsport-first goal, the single Engineering degree wins. The double degree is a reasonable choice only if you are prepared for the trade-offs below.**

| Factor | Engineering alone | Engineering + Commerce (Finance) |
|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Highest; room for 6-8 technical electives (dynamics, control, numerical methods, composites) | Fewer electives; core engineering is kept for accreditation, but your freedom is eaten by the Commerce half |
| WAM/GPA | Easier to protect in the engineering units that employers see | Two sets of assessment cultures, and Commerce marks are typically easier to keep high but do not help with motorsport |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best. Team work is 10-20 h/week in build season | Poorer at full load; at 3 units a semester it is workable but stretches the degree |
| Duration | 4 years full-time **[Check]** | About 5.5-6 years full-time; longer again at 3 units a semester |
| Cost/HECS-HELP | Fewer total units, so lower total debt | More units, so higher debt and 1-2 extra years of delayed full-time salary |
| Employability | Engineering accreditation is the thing motorsport employers look at | Helpful for finance, consulting, and commercial or business roles; adds little for race-engineering jobs |
| Finance flexibility | Good: many engineers move into finance via a graduate program or a Master of Finance | Best, with direct credentials |
| Opportunity cost | Low | Highest: an extra 1.5-2 years plus fewer Formula SAE hours |

**Weak points in the plan, stated directly:**

1. **"Double degree gives flexibility" is only partly true.** It gives flexibility toward finance. It gives *less* flexibility toward motorsport, which is your stated primary goal.
2. **Formula SAE time is the real constraint.** F1 and top-level motorsport teams hire on demonstrated engineering work, such as a well-documented suspension or aero project, not on a second credential. A degree that leaves you at 3 units a semester helps your time only if you really use the freed hours for the team.
3. **Engineering alone can lead into finance.** Engineers regularly move into banking, consulting and quant roles after an engineering degree. The finance fallback is real without a Commerce degree. It is not as clean, but it is real. You can add a Graduate Certificate or Masters later, which also lets you see whether you actually like finance first.
4. **Economics at school is not evidence that you will like finance.** Test it with a first-year Commerce unit or two as breadth before committing 2 extra years.
5. **Finance electives in a single degree may be capped.** Check Monash's rules on breadth, minors and unit counts so that you know exactly how much finance you can fit without the double **[Check]**.

**A hybrid that protects you:** begin in the single degree, use your breadth slots on finance or accounting units, and decide by the end of first year whether to transfer into the double. Whether a transfer is allowed, and with what entry rank, is a Monash-specific rule **[Check with Monash admissions]**. Do not assume it works.

## 3. Why Monash is first

- **Monash Motorsport** (the Formula SAE team) is one of the best-known in Australia and has a long record in the local competition **[Confident that it exists and is well regarded; Check current format and recruitment]**. It is on campus at Clayton, where the engineering courses are, which removes the travel barrier.
- Mechanical Engineering at Monash is accredited by Engineers Australia **[Confident; Check the current accreditation status]**, which is what matters for graduate recruitment and later registration.
- It is the only Victorian option that combines a large, well-resourced team with a flexible double-degree escape route, so you can keep both options open.
- Staying in Melbourne avoids relocation costs, and you already know the Monash path.

## 5. Entry, prerequisites and cost

- **Prerequisites [Check]:** Monash and most Victorian engineering courses require Units 3/4 English plus Mathematical Methods, and usually Physics or Chemistry. You have the core ones. Specialist Mathematics is usually recommended, not required. **Not having it is your biggest first-year risk**, so plan to do bridging or preparation (see section 8).
- **ATAR/selection rank:** I will not state numbers. Look at the "lowest selection rank to receive an offer" for the 2026 intake on each course page and on VTAC. A double degree normally has a higher cut-off than the single degree **[Check]**, so your ATAR could decide the question for you.
- **Adjustments and schemes:** Monash and others have rank-boost schemes, equity and regional schemes, and Subject Bonus schemes (for example, extra points for strong Methods or Physics results) **[Check each. Do not assume you qualify]**.
- **Cost:** Domestic bachelor places are normally Commonwealth Supported Places, with the student contribution deferred through HECS-HELP **[Check the current band for engineering and for commerce; also the current indexation and any recent debt-reduction changes]**. A longer double degree means more units and more total debt.

## 6. Edge cases and what would change the ranking

| Situation | Effect |
|---|---|
| ATAR is below the double-degree cut-off but above the single-degree cut-off | The question is settled: single degree. Add finance breadth. |
| ATAR is below both Monash cut-offs | Use RMIT, Swinburne or Deakin as preferences, or a pathway such as a diploma with credit, and keep a transfer path to Monash **[Check pathway rules]** |
| You cannot keep high marks in first-year maths without Specialist | Take lighter loads and use bridging; do not enter a double degree at full load |
| You get into the Formula SAE team and find it takes more time than you expected | Stay in the single degree or the extended-load double |
| After a first-year finance breadth unit, you want a finance career | Then rank the double degree first |
| Interstate offer from UNSW or UQ with a scholarship | Reconsider if it pays for relocation costs |
| You prefer research or aerodynamics at a deeper level | Melbourne's 3+2 moves up |
| Monash rules do not allow later transfer into the double | Decide now, in your preference order |
