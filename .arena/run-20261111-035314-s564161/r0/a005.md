## 2. What motorsport employers and teams actually screen for

I worked backwards from the outcome. A hireable junior in motorsport or high-performance engineering usually shows:

1. Strong engineering fundamentals: dynamics, mechanics of materials, thermofluids, control, numerical methods. **[Judgement]**
2. Hands-on build experience, which for you means a Formula SAE team. Employers read a finished car as evidence of design-for-manufacture, testing, teamwork and deadlines.
3. Tool fluency: parametric CAD, basic FEA, data acquisition and analysis, and some programming (MATLAB or Python).
4. A decent academic record. It matters most for graduate programs and for any later move into finance.

Commerce appears nowhere in that list. It helps only if you pivot.

Be honest about the geography. **[Judgement]** F1 teams are concentrated in the UK. Australian pathways run more realistically through Supercars, GT, motorsport suppliers and Formula SAE alumni networks, and some graduates then move overseas. No Australian degree alone puts you in F1. Formula SAE results, a strong WAM and a willingness to relocate matter more than the degree label.

## 3. Single versus double, analysed

| Factor | Engineering only | Engineering + Commerce (full load) | Engineering + Commerce (reduced load) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Highest. Room for electives in vehicle dynamics, control, advanced materials and CAD-heavy design. | Lower. About a third of your credit points go to non-engineering study. | Same content as full load, spread over more years. |
| WAM | Easiest to keep high. | Hardest. More subjects per semester, and finance units are graded against a commerce cohort. | Better than full load, but you still carry more total assessed subjects. |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best. | Worst. Team deadlines (design freeze, build, competition) clash with exams. | Feasible, but you pay for it with extra years. |
| Duration | Four years **[Check]**. | Roughly five to six years **[Check]**. | Longer again. |
| Cost | One degree's HECS-HELP debt. Commonwealth-supported places apply, but confirm your course's current student contribution band **[Check]**. | More years and more units, so a larger debt. Study time you could be spending earning an engineering graduate salary is also a cost. | Largest total time cost. |
| Finance flexibility | Real but narrower. Engineers move into finance mainly through graduate programs, a later Master's (for example finance or MBA) or a quantitative role. Having no finance degree can close some doors in banking. | Strongest, and credible on a CV. | Same. |
| Employability in engineering | Good. | Not better than an engineering degree plus Formula SAE. | Same. |
| Opportunity cost | Lowest. | Highest. | High. |

My reading **[Judgement]**:

- **The main weakness in your plan is the assumption that more credentials mean more options.** For motorsport, the scarce resources are time in the workshop and WAM, and the double degree spends both.
- **The finance fallback is probably over-insured.** An engineering degree with a high WAM is already a strong base for graduate programs, consulting, and quantitative or technical finance roles. A finance minor or a few electives, or a later postgraduate degree, covers most of that fallback at a fraction of the cost.
- **The double degree makes sense if your real interest is finance with an engineering flavour.** If you would be genuinely happy in finance, it is a good degree. If you picture yourself at a race circuit and would treat finance only as an escape route, take the single degree.
- **Do not decide now.** If the university allows you to add Commerce or switch after first year (check this rule on Monash's pages **[Check]**), start in engineering alone. Year 1 of both programs overlaps heavily, so you lose little by waiting and you will know your WAM and how much time Formula SAE takes.

## 4. The options in detail

### Rank 1: Monash, Mechanical Engineering (single degree)

- **Why first:** Monash Motorsport is one of the better-known Formula SAE teams in Australia and has run for many years **[Stable, but check its current structure and recruitment]**. It is on campus, so team work and classes fit together. Monash is a large, well-regarded engineering school with strong industry links in Melbourne's automotive and manufacturing sector **[Judgement]**.
- **Entry:** VCE Mathematical Methods and Physics are the usual prerequisites for engineering **[Check the exact prerequisites and any Specialist Mathematics recommendation on the current course page]**. Selection rank and any adjustment schemes (such as equity or school-recommendation programs) change yearly. **I will not quote a figure. Look up the "clear" and "lowest selection rank" values on VTAC and Monash's course page.**
- **Structure:** a common first-year engineering core, then a mechanical specialisation. Typical first-year units include engineering mathematics, computing for engineers and a design project (codes like ENG1005, ENG1060 and ENG1011 are my recollection **[Check all of them]**).
- **Cost:** Commonwealth-supported place, with the contribution set by the government and indexed. HECS-HELP lets you defer it. Rules on indexation and debt reductions have changed recently, so read the current Study Assist (studyassist.gov.au) pages **[Check]**.

### Rank 2: Monash Engineering/Commerce (Finance)

- Use this only if you accept the costs in section 3. Plan a lighter load per semester to protect your WAM and team time, and map out how long it takes before you enrol **[Check duration and any maximum-load or overload rules]**.
- **Sequencing idea:** do engineering core and team work first, and defer heavy finance units to semesters when you are not in the build or competition crunch.

### Rank 3: Swinburne, Mechanical (industry-based learning)

- Swinburne's industry-based learning places students in paid work for a year during the degree **[Check that this is still offered and how places are allocated]**. For a student whose weak point could be a thin practical record, a year in industry is an alternative path to the experience Formula SAE gives, and it can lead to a graduate offer.
- Swinburne also has a Formula SAE team **[Check its name and recent results]**.
- It ranks below Monash mainly on my judgement of research depth, overall reputation and size of the motorsport network, which is arguable. If you value the guaranteed industry year more, it could move above Monash's double degree.

### Rank 4: University of Melbourne (BSc, then MEng Mechanical)

- Melbourne uses a three-year undergraduate degree followed by a two-year professional Master's **[Check the current course structure]**. That is five years to an accredited engineering qualification and costs correspondingly more time and money.
- Strengths: prestige, a strong finance and commerce ecosystem, and a Formula SAE team (Melbourne University Racing **[Check]**). Weak point for you: the structure delays deep specialised engineering, and a five-year commitment is a poor fit if you want to be working sooner.
- It becomes a leading option if your ATAR is very high and you want the strongest general academic brand, or if finance becomes your main pathway.

### Rank 5: RMIT, Mechanical Engineering

- Practical, industry-connected, and RMIT has a Formula SAE team **[Check name and activity]**. It is a sound option and a good fit for hands-on learners.
- It ranks below Swinburne here because I am less sure of its structured industry placement offering **[Check]**. A different reading of the facts could reverse this.

### Others (interstate and further options)

- **UNSW, University of Sydney, UQ, Adelaide, UWA, Newcastle and Wollongong:** all have mechanical engineering programs and Formula SAE or motorsport teams of varying strength **[Check each team's current activity]**. Moving interstate adds living costs and breaks your Melbourne industry network. I would consider them only if you receive a clearly better offer or want to leave Melbourne. UNSW is the strongest-brand candidate **[Judgement]**.
- **Deakin:** has a mechanical program and a motorsport team **[Check]**. It is a reasonable backup.
- **Overseas:** not justified for undergraduate study. International fees are very high and Formula SAE teams exist in Australia. A UK or European motorsport-focused Master's (for example at Cranfield, Oxford Brookes or Imperial) is worth considering later, after a strong Australian degree and team record **[Judgement]**. Check scholarships and fees then.

## 5. Subject-level advice (Monash)

I cannot verify subject codes, including **MMA3101**, which you mentioned. I do not recognise what it covers and I will not guess. **Open the Monash Handbook entry for MMA3101 and judge it by content, not by whether it sounds useful.** Use this checklist of topics and what each does for a motorsport pathway, then find the matching codes in the Handbook:

| Topic to look for | Why it matters for motorsport |
|---|---|
| Engineering mathematics and numerical methods | Underpins simulation, lap-time models and data analysis. |
| Dynamics and vibration | The base of suspension kinematics, ride and handling, and driveline behaviour. |
| Vehicle dynamics (if offered) | Directly develops what you want: tyre models, load transfer, steady-state and transient handling. |
| Control systems | Traction control, engine management, active aero and electric powertrain control. |
| Materials and solid mechanics | Choosing and sizing metals and composites, understanding failure, basic FEA. |
| Manufacturing and design for manufacture | Turns a CAD model into a part you can machine, lay up or weld. |
| CAD and engineering design projects | Parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing. |
| Thermofluids and aerodynamics (fluid mechanics, CFD if offered) | Aero and cooling. |
| Data analysis and programming (MATLAB or Python) | Data acquisition work and optimisation. |
| Optimisation and finance electives | Optimisation helps design trade-offs. Finance electives serve your fallback and add little to technical depth. |

**Direct engineering skill versus transferable intuition versus interest.** Coursework and Formula SAE build direct skill. Karting, workshop hobbies, Product Design and Technologies work and your VCE Physics build transferable practical intuition. Watching F1 is interest, which is fine but is not evidence.

## 6. Where the plan is weak

1. **Missing Specialist Mathematics.** Many engineering students have it. Methods plus Physics is normally enough for entry, but you may find first-year mathematics demanding. Plan to revise before starting and use university maths support **[Check what the course page says]**.
2. **Assuming a double degree is a safety net.** It adds cost and risk to your WAM and may leave you average at both.
3. **Treating Monash Formula SAE as automatic.** Teams select members and the workload is heavy. Look up the team's recruitment process and contact current members.
4. **Underestimating the overseas gap for F1.** Plan for a path that may include relocating.

## 7. What would change the ranking

- If your ATAR is very high and you want maximum brand and finance optionality, Melbourne and the Monash double move up.
- If you decide you care mostly about finance, the Monash double and Melbourne move above single engineering.

- If Swinburne's industry placement is confirmed and well paid, it can rise above the Monash double.
- If you want a different city, UNSW or UQ become serious candidates.
