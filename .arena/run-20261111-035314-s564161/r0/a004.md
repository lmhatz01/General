# Best-suited university options for Lukas (VTAC, 2027 entry)

## Bottom line

Do Monash Mechanical Engineering (Honours) as a single degree. Fill your electives with finance and economics-adjacent units and put your spare hours into Monash Motorsport. Treat Commerce (Finance) as an option you can buy later, not a premium you pay now.

Your own stated priority is F1/motorsport engineering. Motorsport employers hire on technical depth, project record and Formula SAE (FSAE) leadership. A Commerce degree adds almost nothing to those signals. It costs about a year and a lot of time and attention.

## How I framed the double-degree question

Finance is a fallback and a complement. That is the shape of a financial option. You pay a premium to keep a right you may never use. A double degree pays that premium in full now, in extra years, lower WAM and less time for FSAE. A finance minor or electives pays a small premium. A later postgraduate qualification (Graduate Certificate or Master of Finance or similar) pays it only if the fallback is needed.

The analogy breaks in three places, and each one changes the answer slightly:

1. **The option price is not fixed.** Later postgraduate entry may need a strong WAM and may cost more. That is a reason to protect your WAM now.
2. **The right may lapse.** Industry rarely checks for a Commerce degree if you have an engineering degree, a strong profile and the ability to show finance knowledge. It is usually enough for graduate programs in consulting or finance, but some finance roles filter by degree. **(check)** Test this by looking at actual graduate job ads.
3. **Time decay is real.** A 5 to 6 year double degree delays your first engineering job and your first full income.

## Ranked recommendation

### 1. Monash, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical specialisation, with finance-flavoured electives

- **Why first:** The Clayton campus is close, the course is established, and Monash Motorsport is one of the most visible FSAE teams in Australia. This is where the stated priorities (Formula SAE, CAD, manufacturing, vehicle dynamics) are most easily met without relocating. **(stable that the team exists; check its current recruitment and structure)**
- **Duration:** 4 years full time **(check)**.
- **Entry:** VCE Mathematical Methods and Physics are the usual prerequisites for engineering **(check, including whether Specialist Mathematics is required or recommended)**. Look at Monash adjustment schemes and any entry pathways on the VTAC and Monash pages.
- **Finance without Commerce:** Use free electives or any approved minor and take units such as introductory economics, accounting or finance only where they fit. **(check)** whether a minor can be taken alongside the engineering degree and which units count.
- **Skills fit:** Choose electives and projects that build mathematics, numerical methods, dynamics, control, materials, manufacturing, CAD and data analysis. Each should be justified by a specific motorsport use. For example, numerical methods and FEA support suspension and chassis design. Control and signals support data acquisition and traction control. Materials and manufacturing support composites and machining.
- **What to verify:** the subject list for the mechanical specialisation, final-year project options, industry placement options and the real time commitment of Monash Motorsport.

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance), done at full or near-full load

- **When it ranks above #1:** only if you decide finance is equally important, not a fallback.
- **Cost:** about 5 years full time **(check)**, or longer at 3 units a semester. That extends HECS-HELP debt, delays full-time work, and risks a lower WAM and less FSAE time.
- **Verdict:** Do not choose this as the default. If you start it, check whether you can drop the Commerce half after first year without penalty. **(check)** Many double degrees let you do this, so a late exit is cheap insurance.

### 3. University of Melbourne (Bachelor of Science then Master of Engineering, mechanical) **(check structure, entry and prerequisites)**

- **Strengths:** strong research and academic reputation, and a student FSAE team. **(check team name and current activity)**
- **Weaknesses:** the pathway is usually 3 years plus 2 years, so it is a longer commitment and has a different structure from a standard 4-year degree.
- **When it ranks higher:** if you want a research career or later postgraduate study in aerodynamics or CFD, or if the Melbourne brand matters to you.

### 4. RMIT, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), mechanical **(check)**

- **Strengths:** practical, industry-facing, and has an FSAE team and strong CAD and hands-on culture. **(check)**
- **Weaknesses:** lower academic prestige than Monash or Melbourne, though employers in motorsport care little about it.
- **Role:** a strong backup in the same city.

### 5. Swinburne, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours) with industry placement **(check)**

- **Strengths:** the industry-based learning model gives real work experience, and there is a motorsport team. **(check)**
- **Consider it if:** paid industry experience matters more than FSAE and you want a built-in placement year. Confirm that the placement is included, rather than assumed.

### 6. Deakin (Geelong) **(check)**

- A reasonable option, with some Formula SAE activity **(check)**. Less compelling than the options above unless location or a specific scholarship favours it.

### 7. Interstate: UQ, UNSW, Sydney, Adelaide **(check all)**

- UQ and UNSW have well-known FSAE teams, and UNSW and Sydney have strong engineering reputations. The cost and disruption of moving interstate, plus lost access to Victorian networks, mean none outranks Monash unless a specific team, scholarship or research group changes that.
- Adelaide's structure may have changed with its university merger, so confirm the current course and team before considering it.

### Overseas

Not justified for undergraduate entry. A UK or European motorsport-focused master's (for example at a university with a strong Formula Student and motorsport industry cluster) is realistic **after** your bachelor's, and it needs a strong WAM and a project record. Plan for it as a later step, not as a 2027 preference.

## Where the plan is weak or assumption-based

- **"Double degree gives flexibility."** It gives optional flexibility at a certain price. You have not shown that employers in your target field value it.
- **"Spread the double degree to free up time for FSAE."** It frees time per semester but adds years. FSAE has its busiest period around build and competition. A part-time load does not guarantee you can carry a major FSAE role.
- **"Motorsport interest" is not a skill.** Watching F1 is interest. Driving, karting or marshalling gives some transferable practical intuition. Design, build and test work in FSAE is direct engineering skill development. Rank your time accordingly.
- **F1 itself is extremely competitive and small.** Make sure the broader automotive, motorsport, defence and high-performance engineering industries are acceptable fallbacks, which they are, and which also keeps finance a less-needed fallback.
- **You have not stated a target ATAR.** Entry rank matters only if you miss the course you want. Engineering at Monash has historically been reachable with a strong but not extreme ATAR. **(check, do not rely on this)**
