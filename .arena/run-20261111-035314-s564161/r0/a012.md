# Best-suited university options for Lukas (VTAC 2027 entry)

## Bottom line

1. **Monash Mechanical Engineering (Honours), single degree, with finance/business electives and Formula SAE (Monash Motorsport).** This is the best fit for your primary goal.
2. **Monash Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance) double degree.** This is a close second. It becomes first only if you confirm you can handle the load and still make the Formula SAE team (conditions below).
3. **University of Melbourne: Bachelor of Science (Engineering Systems major) then Master of Engineering (Mechanical).** It is a strong, prestigious hedge with Melbourne University Racing, but it takes 5 years and is less hands-on.
4. **RMIT Mechanical Engineering (Honours).** It is very practical, with Redback Racing, and has the lowest entry risk of the main options.
5. **Swinburne Engineering (Honours) with its industry placement stream, then Deakin.** These are good, practical Victorian options for hands-on experience.
6. **Interstate (UQ, UNSW, Sydney, Adelaide/UWA) and overseas.** Only if you want to relocate. UQ Racing is a top Formula SAE programme. Overseas is a postgraduate decision, not an undergraduate one.

The double-degree question is answered directly in the section below. In short, it is not automatically better for you. Your own concerns (WAM, Formula SAE time, technical depth) are the right ones.

## The system you are optimising

Your outcome depends on five linked things, and the choice of degree affects all of them.

- **Technical profile.** F1 and motorsport employers hire on engineering fundamentals, CAD, hands-on build experience and evidence of project ownership.
- **Time.** You have roughly 40 to 50 productive hours a week. Every unit of commerce competes with study, with a Formula SAE role, and with sleep.
- **Grades (WAM).** These matter for honours, graduate programs and any overseas master's application. A heavy double-degree load tends to lower WAM.
- **Cost and duration.** An extra year is a year of delayed salary and extra living costs, on top of any extra HECS-HELP debt.
- **Optionality.** Engineering to finance is an easy and common move. Finance to engineering is almost impossible without redoing the degree.

The second-order consequence is that the choice that protects Formula SAE time and WAM early also keeps both futures open. The one that locks in extra years costs you most in the area you rank first.

## Honest weaknesses in the current plan

- **"F1 engineering" is a very small market.** There are about ten teams, mostly in the UK, and hiring from Australia is rare. A realistic path is Formula SAE leadership, then Supercars, other motorsport or high-performance engineering (aerospace, defence, automotive R&D). Often it also needs UK or European experience or a master's. Plan so that the fallback pathways are good, because the fallback is the likely outcome.
- **Interest in motorsport is not a skill.** What counts is the direct engineering skill you can show: a car you helped build, tested and documented.
- **"Finance as a fallback" is already covered by engineering.** Engineering graduates enter consulting, finance and quant roles regularly. A mechanical engineering degree with good grades plus a later finance qualification (graduate diploma or master's, or the CFA path) gets you there without spending years of undergraduate time on commerce.
- **You are weighing too many options.** The cheapest decision is the one that keeps all doors open at the lowest cost, and the next section shows which one that is.

## Double degree vs single degree

| Factor | Engineering alone (finance electives) | Engineering + Commerce (Finance) |
|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Highest. Full space for design, dynamics, control, manufacturing, composites and projects. | Lower. Fewer engineering electives, and capstone and project time competes with commerce. |
| WAM risk | Lower. One discipline. | Higher. Two sets of assessments, and engineering and finance WAMs may be viewed separately. |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best. You can take a real role (suspension, aero, powertrain, data). | Harder, unless you extend the duration. |
| Duration | 4 years full-time. | About 5 years full-time, longer if you slow down. |
| Cost/HECS | Lower. One fewer year, lower debt. | About a year more of fees (debt) and living costs. Check the 2027 student-contribution bands and any changes to HELP indexation. |
| Employability | Strong in engineering. Finance roles are possible via graduate pathways. | Broader signal for finance and consulting. A slight edge for banks and graduate programs. |
| Flexibility | Good. You can add finance study later. | Best at the start, but expensive to carry. |
| Opportunity cost | Lowest. | A year or more, plus less time for projects and internships. |

**Spreading the double over a longer period (about 3 units a semester).** This helps WAM and Formula SAE time, but you pay for it in extra years of cost and delay, and you may run into HELP lifetime limits and maximum course durations. Verify both with Monash. In practice, if you need to slow down to fit Formula SAE, the single degree plus finance electives is the cleaner version of the same trade.

**What I recommend:** do not commit to the double on day one. Ask Monash two questions, because the answers decide the structure of your preferences.

1. Can I move from the double degree to the single degree in the first year or two? I believe this is usually allowed, but confirm.
2. Can I add Commerce later after starting the single degree, and under what conditions (a WAM threshold, remaining units)? This is usually harder.

- If moving down from the double is easy, list the double first. You get the option to keep both, and you can reduce to the single without penalty if the load hurts.
- If moving between them is restrictive, make the single degree your first preference. This is the safer choice for your stated priorities.

**What would make the double the better choice:** you are comfortable with the load after a trial semester, your engineering WAM stays high, you hold a real Formula SAE role, and finance becomes the more attractive career. Otherwise the single degree wins.

## Ranking, with reasons

### 1. Monash Mechanical Engineering (Honours), single degree
- **Why it ranks first:** it has the strongest combination of a well-regarded mechanical program and **Monash Motorsport**, one of Australia's leading Formula SAE teams. That is the single best lever for your primary goal. It also sits at a university with a wide set of elective and minor options in finance, so your secondary goal is covered without a second degree.
- **Check:** current entry requirements, course structure, available minors and electives, and the Monash Motorsport recruitment process, which usually opens at the start of the first semester.

### 2. Monash Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance)
- **Why second:** it is the same technical core plus a credential for finance. The cost is the extra year, the load and the pressure on WAM and Formula SAE. It is the right choice only if finance is truly a co-equal goal.

### 3. University of Melbourne: BSc (Engineering Systems) then MEng (Mechanical)
- **Strengths:** high prestige, strong research, **Melbourne University Racing**, and breadth that suits a later move into finance. The Master's is the accredited qualification.
- **Weaknesses:** 5 years in total, higher selection ranks, and a more theoretical approach with less hands-on work early on.
- **Check:** the current pathway, prerequisites, and whether it needs Specialist Maths or a particular selection rank.

### 4. RMIT Mechanical Engineering (Honours)
- **Strengths:** a hands-on culture, strong CAD and workshop emphasis, and **Redback Racing**. It has good industry links across Melbourne, and entry is typically lower, which helps if your ATAR falls short.
- **Weaknesses:** lower research prestige than Monash or Melbourne.

### 5. Swinburne, then Deakin
- **Swinburne:** an industry-based placement stream (paid work-integrated learning) that gives real engineering experience. Check its motorsport team's current activity.
- **Deakin:** mechanical engineering with mandatory work placement and **Deakin Motorsport**. Check the current course structure.

### 6. Interstate and overseas
- **UQ:** a very strong Formula SAE team (UQ Racing). **UNSW** and **Sydney** are strong academically. **Adelaide/UWA** are viable. Worth it only if you are prepared to relocate and pay the extra living cost.
- **Overseas:** not justified at undergraduate level, given cost and visa constraints. A UK master's in motorsport or advanced mechanical engineering (for example Cranfield, Oxford Brookes or Imperial) after your Australian degree is the realistic route, and a strong WAM matters for admission.

## Subjects to prioritise (check codes in the Handbook)

I do not give specific unit codes, because I could not verify them. Search the Handbook for these topics, and for each one confirm it is offered and check its prerequisites.

| Topic | How it helps a motorsport pathway |
|---|---|
| Engineering mathematics, numerical methods, optimisation | Underpins simulation, lap-time modelling, suspension and aero optimisation. |
| Computational modelling (Python/MATLAB) | Data analysis from the car's sensors, quick models and automation. |
| Dynamics and mechanics of machines | Vehicle dynamics, suspension kinematics and drivetrain behaviour. |
| Control systems | Traction control, engine management, active systems. |
| Materials and solid mechanics | Chassis and composite design, FEA interpretation and failure analysis. |
| Fluid mechanics / aerodynamics | Wings, underbody, cooling and CFD. |
| Thermodynamics and powertrain | Engine and electric powertrain performance, heat management. |
| Manufacturing and design for manufacture | Machining, composites, tolerancing and cost-aware design, which Formula SAE teams value highly. |
| Engineering design and CAD (parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing, basic FEA) | Directly employable on day one in any team. |
| Data analysis and instrumentation | Data acquisition and telemetry analysis. |

Finance electives are worth taking (for example corporate finance and investments), but fill them only after the technical core and project units. If you take a minor, pick one that fits around your capstone and Formula SAE commitments.

## Skill source: what builds what

- **Direct engineering skill:** coursework in the topics above, plus Formula SAE build and test work (CAD, FEA, machining, composites, data acquisition, documentation, teamwork).
- **Transferable practical intuition:** workshop time, driving or karting data, working on cars.
- **Mere interest:** watching and following F1. It is fine but counts for nothing in selection.

## Entry, cost and what to check

- **Prerequisites:** VCE Mathematical Methods (Units 3/4) and Physics are required or strongly assumed. Specialist Maths helps but is rarely mandatory. Confirm each course page, because prerequisites occasionally change.
- **Selection rank:** ATAR plus adjustments (subject bonuses, equity or regional schemes where eligible). I do not have verified figures, so use each university's published figures and the VTAC Guide.
- **Cost:** most places are Commonwealth supported, and HECS-HELP lets you defer the student contribution. The amount depends on the course band and the year. Confirm 2027 amounts on the university's fees page and the StudyAssist website. Include living costs for the extra year when you compare the single and double degrees.
- **Course codes and structure:** copy from the current Handbook or course page, not from memory.
