# Best-suited university options for Lukas (VTAC 2027 entry)

## The short answer

1. **Monash, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical specialisation, as a single degree, with finance/business electives or a minor if your course allows it.**
2. **Monash, Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance) double degree**, taken at a normal load, as the close second.
3. **Swinburne, Engineering (Honours) with an industry placement year.**
4. **RMIT, Engineering (Honours), Mechanical.**
5. **University of Melbourne, Bachelor of Science, then Master of Engineering (Mechanical).**
6. **UNSW Sydney, Mechanical Engineering.**
7. **UQ, Mechanical Engineering.**
8. **Deakin, Mechanical Engineering.**

An overseas degree is not a realistic undergraduate choice. It is a possible postgraduate step later (see below).

The obvious answer is "do the Monash double, it keeps both doors open". I argued against it and most of that argument holds. Your own stated priority is motorsport. The scarce thing in that pathway is technical depth and time with a Formula SAE team, and a double degree costs both. Finance is the fallback, and you do not need a commerce degree to get into it.

## The assumptions behind this answer

- You want mechanical engineering to be your degree. F1 and motorsport is the goal, and finance is a backup you would like to keep available.
- "Strong results" means a high ATAR. I have not assumed a number. Several of these courses are competitive, so plan for the possibility that some will be out of reach.
- You are in Melbourne and moving interstate is possible but costly.
- Specialist Maths is not in your subject list, so I treat it as not held.

## Where your plan is weak

- **"Double degree = flexibility" is partly an illusion.** It keeps a finance door open that is already open to engineers. Graduate programs in banking, consulting, and quantitative and analyst roles routinely hire engineers. They value Methods-level maths, modelling and data skills. Many people add finance later through a short postgraduate course or the CFA exams.
- **F1 is extremely scarce.** Most teams are in the UK, and a handful of Australians get in each year. Treat F1 as an aspiration. Judge each option on whether it gives you a very good *engineering career* and a real shot at motorsport, not on F1 alone.
- **Formula SAE is a strong signal, not a guarantee.** Team leaders and people who built and tested real parts get noticed. Members who only turn up do not.
- **Slowing the double to three units a semester** (about six to seven years) is a poor trade. You delay full-time pay by a year or more and the HECS-HELP debt grows. It also does not fix the real problem, which is that you split your attention across two disciplines.
- **A very high WAM matters less than you think.** For motorsport, a strong portfolio of projects counts for more than a high WAM. For finance, WAM matters more, but only for a handful of competitive graduate programs.
- **Your Year 12 subjects fit engineering well.** Methods and Physics are the two that matter, and Economics gives you a head start on commerce. Product Design and Technologies gives you design process and some CAD, though not yet engineering-grade CAD.

## Ranking and reasoning

### 1. Monash, single Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, with finance electives or a minor

- **Why it is first.** Monash Motorsport (the Formula SAE team) is one of the strongest and best-resourced in Australia, and it is at the same campus as the degree. A single degree gives you about four years of full-time load with time left for the team.
- **Technical depth.** You complete the full mechanical sequence plus a final-year project, without sharing credit points with commerce.
- **Finance later.** Use electives or a minor if the course structure allows it (check). Otherwise, finish engineering, then do a graduate diploma or Master of Finance if you want it.
- **Duration and cost.** About four years, one year shorter than the double, so lower HECS-HELP debt and an earlier salary.
- **Main risk.** If the engineering degree does not allow enough finance study, you lose a little breadth. That is a small cost.

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance)

- **Why it is second.** It is the same engineering degree and team access as option 1, plus a finance major that gives you a recognised qualification if you leave motorsport.
- **The cost.** It is about five years, with commerce units competing with engineering for your time. For a student who wants Monash Motorsport, that competition is the real cost.
- **When it overtakes option 1.** If, by the end of first year, you find you are more interested in finance than in motorsport. Then the double is clearly better.
- **How to use it.** Take it at a normal load, not stretched out.

### 3. Swinburne, Engineering (Honours) with industry placement

- **Why it ranks here.** Swinburne's model (placement or industry-based learning) gives you months of paid engineering work. That is real employer contact, and it is the thing employers value alongside Formula SAE.
- **Trade-off.** The course is usually less competitive to enter, and its research profile and brand are weaker than Monash or Melbourne.
- **When it wins.** If you miss the Monash cut-off or want a practical, placement-heavy route. Check the current team and placement options.

### 4. RMIT, Engineering (Honours), Mechanical

- **Why.** It is practical and city-based, has an active Formula SAE team and good industry links. It is a sound alternative to Swinburne.
- **Trade-off.** It is similar to Swinburne in profile, with less research depth than Monash.

### 5. University of Melbourne, BSc then Master of Engineering (Mechanical)

- **Why.** Very strong academically, with a motorsport team of its own. The model is a three-year undergraduate degree followed by a two-year masters, which is five years in total.
- **Why it ranks below Monash.** It is longer, you pay for the masters years, and the undergraduate phase has less engineering. You also need to plan your subject choices so that you qualify for the masters.
- **When it wins.** If you want a research-heavy or postgraduate-oriented route, or you value its finance and breadth subjects.

### 6 to 8. UNSW, UQ, Deakin

- **UNSW and UQ** both have strong mechanical engineering and active, well-regarded Formula SAE teams. They rank below the Victorian options only because relocating adds cost and moves you away from your base. If you would genuinely like to live in Sydney or Brisbane, move them up.
- **Deakin** has a lower entry profile and a motorsport team. It is a sensible backup, not a first choice.
- **Others** worth a look: the University of Sydney, University of Adelaide (which merges with UniSA in 2026, so check the current name and course structure) and UWA. They fit only if you want to move interstate.

### Overseas

An undergraduate degree overseas is expensive, and you would lose Australian HECS-HELP access. The realistic overseas route is a **postgraduate** year in the UK motorsport cluster after your Australian degree. Check visa rules, funding and whether the specific course suits you.

## Skill development: what each part of the degree gives you

| Skill you want | Where it comes from | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Parametric CAD, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing, DFM/DFA | Engineering design units and **Formula SAE**, which is the main place you will use them on real parts | Direct skill |
| Basic FEA, kinematics | Mechanics and solid mechanics units, then the team's suspension and chassis work | Direct skill |
| Vehicle dynamics, suspension, aerodynamics, powertrain | Dynamics, fluids and thermodynamics units, then the team. Vehicle dynamics is mostly learned by doing | Direct, mostly via the team |
| Manufacturing, machining, composites | Workshop access, manufacturing units, the team's build season | Transferable practical intuition |
| Data acquisition, control, numerical methods, optimisation | Control, numerical methods and computational units, plus team telemetry work | Direct skill |
| Teamwork and documentation | Team projects, design reports | Direct skill |
| Watching F1, reading about it | Nothing by itself | Interest only, not evidence of skill |

Favour electives and units that build maths, computational modelling, dynamics, control, materials and manufacturing. Each of those feeds one of the rows above. A commerce unit feeds none of them directly, so each one you add should be a deliberate choice, not a default.

## What would change the ranking

- **You enjoy finance more than engineering after first year.** The double (2) moves to first.
- **You do not get into Monash.** Swinburne (3) or RMIT (4) become your best option. Use Formula SAE there to make up the difference.
- **Monash does not allow finance electives, a minor, or a transfer to the double.** Then decide between 1 and 2 at VTAC time, and lean to 2 only if you are sure you want both.
- **You want a research or postgraduate route.** Melbourne (5) moves up.
- **You want to live interstate.** UNSW or UQ move up.

## Next steps

**Now to December 2026**
1. Check the Monash course pages for the single and double degrees (see the checklist). Write down the exact course codes, prerequisites, duration and whether a minor or transfer is allowed.
2. Check VTAC for the current change-of-preference dates. Change windows after the exams are usual, but confirm.
3. Draft your preference list. A sensible starting order is: Monash single Engineering (1), Monash double (2), Swinburne (3), RMIT (4), Melbourne (5). Put courses in the order you actually prefer them, because VTAC gives you the highest preference you qualify for.
4. Look into adjustment and scholarship schemes at each university (for example, subject or regional adjustments, and equity or merit schemes). Check which apply to you.

**January to February 2027**
5. Accept your offer, then enrol in the first-year units and check the timetable.
6. Contact Monash Motorsport (and any backup team) about recruitment, onboarding and time commitment.

**First year**
7. Join the team, learn CAD properly and take on a small, real design task early.
8. Decide about the commerce add-on by the end of first year, using your actual results and interest, not today's guess.
9. Apply for a placement or internship each year.
