## 3. Double or single? Answered directly

**For a motorsport-first goal, the single degree is the better default. The double is justified only by a real finance goal.**

| Factor | Engineering alone | Engineering + Commerce (Finance) |
|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | More room for technical electives (dynamics, control, numerical methods, composites). Exact count: **[Unknown, check the course map]** | The accredited engineering core is kept, but the elective space is taken by Commerce |
| WAM/GPA | One assessment culture, and it is the one motorsport employers read | Two cultures. I have no evidence on which half is easier to score in, so I make no claim |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best. Team build seasons are heavy; time per week varies by role, so ask the team **[Unknown]** | Workable at about 3 units a semester, but only if he really uses the freed hours on the team |
| Duration | 4 years full-time **[Mem]** | Roughly 5 to 6 years full-time **[Mem, check]**, longer at reduced load |
| Cost | Fewer units | More units, and under the Job-ready Graduates fee bands commerce-type units sit in a higher student-contribution band than engineering **[Mem, check the current bands on Study Assist]**. So each Commerce unit likely costs more than an engineering one |
| Employability | Accredited engineering is the key credential for motorsport | Adds finance credentials. Adds little for race-engineering roles |
| Finance fallback | Real: engineers move into banking, consulting and quant roles, and a postgraduate finance qualification is available later | Direct and cleanest |
| Opportunity cost | Lowest | Extra years of delayed salary and reduced team time |

**Weak points in the plan, stated plainly**

1. "Flexibility" is one-directional. The double buys flexibility toward finance and costs flexibility toward motorsport, the stated primary goal.
2. F1 and top-level teams hire on demonstrated engineering work (a documented suspension or aero project, a data-acquisition tool, a working car), not on a second degree.
3. Economics at school does not show that he will like finance. Test it first.
4. Reducing load to about 3 units a semester only helps if the freed time goes to the team. If it goes to extra Commerce units, nothing is gained.

**The recommendation does not depend on an internal transfer.** Whether Monash lets a student move from the single into the double after first year, or from the double down to the single, is **[Unknown]** and he must ask Monash admissions. Plan for both outcomes:

- **If he does not want finance as a career:** list the single first. Add finance as electives or a minor if the rules allow, and a postgraduate qualification later.
- **If finance is a real possibility and his rank clears the double:** the double may be the safer start, **if** Monash allows a student to drop down to the single later. Giving up a half is usually easier than adding one, but that is **[Mem, not verified]**, so ask. If Monash allows neither direction, decide on your own judgement before preferences are final.
- **If his rank clears only the single:** the question is settled. Take the single.

**MMA3101.** He found this code himself, so I withdraw my earlier doubt: I cannot confirm what it is, and the fact I do not recognise it proves nothing. ## 5. Specialist Mathematics: how big is the gap?

- Specialist is usually recommended rather than required for engineering **[Mem]**. His Methods and Physics cover the formal prerequisites **[Mem]**.
- The content he missed is mainly: complex numbers, vectors in 3D and proof, further integration and differential equations, and some mechanics. First-year engineering mathematics at Monash covers calculus, linear algebra and differential equations, and it normally assumes only Methods **[Mem]**. So the gap is real but manageable, not a crisis.
- **Plan (December to February):** cover vectors and complex numbers from a free source (Khan Academy, university bridging notes), then do a first pass of single- and multivariable calculus and basic linear algebra. Check whether Monash publishes a bridging or preparation course for incoming engineers **[Unknown]**.
- **Where it bites:** only if first-year maths marks suffer, since a high WAM is a stated goal. That is one reason to avoid a full load of two degrees in first year.

## 6. Entry, prerequisites and cost

- **Selection ranks [Unknown]:** I give no numbers. My relative expectation **[Mem, low confidence]** is that Melbourne, UNSW and the Monash double are the most selective, the Monash single is somewhat lower, and RMIT, Swinburne and Deakin are lower again. Check the "lowest selection rank to receive an offer" on each course page and VTAC.
- **Prerequisites [Mem]:** Units 3/4 English, Methods, and usually Physics or Chemistry. He has them. Check each course page.
- **Adjustment schemes:** universities have subject bonuses, equity and regional schemes. Do not assume he qualifies. Check each.
- **Cost:** Commonwealth Supported Places with HECS-HELP, so no upfront payment. Each unit's cost depends on its band **[Mem]**, so a longer double means more units, probably at a higher band for the Commerce half. Check Study Assist for current bands, indexation and any debt-reduction changes.

**VTAC logic that matters.** VTAC makes the offer from the highest-ranked preference he qualifies for. Preferences should therefore follow what he *wants*, not what he expects to get. If the double has a higher cut-off than the single, listing the single above it means the double can never be reached: anyone who qualifies for the double also qualifies for the single. So:

- If he would rather have the double when he qualifies for it, list the double above the single.
- If he prefers the single, list the single first and do not list the double above it. Listing it below adds nothing, so it can be left out unless he would still take it over lower options.
