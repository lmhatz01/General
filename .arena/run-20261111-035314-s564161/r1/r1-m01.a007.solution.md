## Worked example: what each version of the Monash degree actually costs

Assume (Medium confidence) a full load is 4 units a semester, 8 a year, with about 32 units for single and 40 for the double.

| Option | Load | Years | Units of fees |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Single engineering | 4 a semester | 4 | about 32 |
| B. Single engineering, reduced load | 3 a semester | about 5.3 | about 32 |
| C. Double | 4 a semester | 5 | about 40 |
| D. Double, your idea | 3 a semester | about 6.7 | about 40 |

What this shows:

- **Fees and HECS-HELP are charged per unit, not per year.** Student contributions are set per unit by discipline band (Medium confidence; bands differ for engineering and commerce, so check the current amounts). So stretching a degree from 4 to 3 units a semester does **not** add fee years. The extra cost of the double over the single is the roughly 8 commerce units, about 8 units of contribution, plus delayed earnings from finishing later. HELP debt is also indexed each year, so a longer time with a balance carries indexation, and repayments only start once your income passes the repayment threshold (check the current threshold on the Australian Taxation Office or StudyAssist sites).
- **Time cost.** Option D finishes about 2.7 years after Option A, which is a large delay in graduate salary and in starting a career. That is the real price of D, not extra fee years.
- **What D buys.** A lighter semester, which is exactly the point of your idea: it frees time for Formula SAE. So D is not useless for Formula SAE capacity. The honest comparison is among B, C and D.
- **Compare B against D.** B (single degree at 3 units a semester) gives the same weekly relief as D, finishes about 1.4 years later than A, and keeps the full technical core. D gives the same weekly relief and adds a finance degree, but costs 1.4 years more than B plus about 8 more units of fees. If your reason to slow down is Formula SAE and WAM, B is the cheaper way to get it. D is justified only if you want the commerce credential.
- **Reality check on reduced load.** Check Monash's rules on minimum loads and on any impact for scholarships, and for any visa or Centrelink conditions. Also, Formula SAE time demand is concentrated before the competition season, usually around the end of the year, not spread evenly.

## Double versus single: the explicit answer

| Factor | Single Eng (Mech) | Eng + Commerce (Finance) |
|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Full core plus more technical electives | Core preserved (accreditation intact), fewer technical electives |
| WAM | Easier to protect | Harder; a second set of assessments, and commerce marking is a different skill |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best at a normal load | Worse at a normal load; recoverable only by extending the years |
| Duration | 4 years | 5 years, about 6.7 at 3 units a semester |
| Cost | Lowest | About 8 extra units of contribution, plus delayed earnings |
| Motorsport employability | Strongest signal: Formula SAE plus WAM plus projects | Commerce adds little for technical F1 hiring (my judgement, not a verified hiring statistic) |
| Finance employability | Possible, but some graduate programs screen by degree | Clear signal for finance and consulting |
| Flexibility | Can add a Master of Finance or MBA later | Both doors open from day one |

**Does engineering alone give a pathway into finance later?** Yes, but unevenly. Engineers are recruited into consulting, quantitative and some corporate finance roles, and a postgraduate finance degree or MBA is a normal later route. What you lose is the undergraduate finance credential for programs that screen by degree. That matters only if you want those roles. The reverse path (commerce graduate into F1 engineering) does not work. This is my judgement from general knowledge; confirm with graduate-employer pages if finance becomes a serious goal.

**Where your plan is weak:**
1. Finance is described as a fallback, but this fallback has a real price: about 8 more units of fees, a lower WAM risk, and a year or more of extra time, for a plan B you may never use.
2. Formula SAE and WAM both drive technical hiring, and a double degree puts pressure on both.
3. "Interest in motorsport" is not a skill. Teams hire on technical ability, so the best evidence is things you designed, built and tested.
4. F1 team roles are few and global. A strong mechanical degree with Formula SAE experience also leads to Supercars, Tier 1 automotive, aerospace, defence and high-performance engineering. That is your realistic safety net, and it needs no commerce degree.
5. The decision to double is reversible in one direction (adding commerce later or dropping it) only if the university allows it. That is unverified, so ask.

**Internships and graduate outcomes.** I could not verify Monash's industry placement options or graduate outcome statistics. Formula SAE itself is the strongest internship-equivalent for motorsport. Check the course page for any industry-based learning or internship unit, and check the Quality Indicators for Learning and Teaching (QILT) site for graduate employment and salary outcomes by university and field.

**What would make the double rank first:** finance interests you as much as motorsport; you accept a likely lower WAM; or you decide not to join Monash Motorsport.

## Skill development map (what builds what)

Three categories: **direct skill** (graded or built), **transferable intuition**, **mere interest**.

- **Direct skill, from the degree** (find the matching Monash Handbook units yourself; I will not quote codes I cannot confirm):
  - Calculus, differential equations and linear algebra (engineering maths): the base for every model below.
  - Numerical methods and computational modelling: simulation, lap-time and suspension models, optimisation.
  - Statics and dynamics / mechanics: loads, kinematics, vehicle dynamics and suspension geometry.
  - Control and instrumentation: data acquisition, sensors, stability.
  - Materials and solid mechanics: stress, fatigue, composites; the basis for FEA.
  - Thermofluids: aerodynamics, CFD basics, engine and cooling.
  - Manufacturing and design units: DFM/DFA, tolerancing, engineering drawing.
- **Direct skill, from Monash Motorsport (not graded):** parametric CAD, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing, basic FEA, composites, machining, data acquisition, documentation and teamwork. Course CAD classes are usually introductory, so this is where most practical CAD skill is built.
- **Transferable intuition:** karting, car setup and tuning, watching racing. Useful in conversations, not hiring evidence.
- **Mere interest:** following F1 counts for nothing on its own.

Elective rule: secure the technical electives first (numerical methods, control, vehicle dynamics, manufacturing, advanced materials, data analysis), then use any remaining slots for finance or business units.

**MMA3101:** I cannot identify it from memory and will not guess. Look it up in the Monash Handbook and test it against the list above: does it build maths, modelling, dynamics, control, materials, manufacturing or CAD? If not, it is a low priority.

## Next steps, tied to the calendar (typical dates; confirm every date on VTAC)

- **Now to the end of exams (November):** finish VCE strongly. Methods and Physics are your prerequisites and your main entry levers. Do not change your plan based on stress.
- **This month:** open the VTAC site and confirm your application status. Timely applications normally close around late September, so if you missed it, late applications with a fee normally remain open into the following months. Check your account and ensure your preferences are entered.

- **After first year:** decide on finance using real data on your WAM and team time, not before. This works only if the transfer rules permit it, which is why you must check them early.
