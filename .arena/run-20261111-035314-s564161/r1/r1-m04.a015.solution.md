# Best-suited university options for Lukas (2027 entry)

## 1. The short answer

**One ranked list, used throughout this document.** Ranked by how well each option serves a motorsport engineering career, with finance kept open as a fallback.

1. **Monash, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, single degree, with finance electives.** Join Monash Motorsport in first year.
2. **Monash, Engineering (Mechanical) + Commerce (Finance) double degree.** A close second, not a loser. It is the right choice only under the conditions in section 5.
3. **Swinburne, Mechanical Engineering (Honours), with its industry placement option.** The best Victorian alternative: a direct four-year engineering degree plus built-in industry experience.
4. **University of Melbourne, Bachelor of Science (engineering-leading major), then Master of Engineering (Mechanical).** Strong brand and a strong racing team, but five years, and the engineering qualification only arrives at the Master's.
5. **RMIT, Mechanical Engineering (Honours).** Local, practical, has a team. Solid fallback.
6. **Deakin, Mechanical Engineering (Honours).** Credible, lower priority for your goals.
7. **UNSW, 8. UQ, 9. Sydney, 10. Adelaide.** Only if you genuinely want to move interstate. They sit below the Victorian options because the move costs money and takes you away from Melbourne's motorsport and manufacturing base, not because the courses are weak. UNSW and UQ are strong courses with strong teams.

**Why the order falls where it does, pair by pair:**
- 1 over 2: the single degree protects marks and team time, which are what motorsport employers see.
- 2 over 3: Monash has the larger, better-integrated team and you can still add finance.
- 3 over 4: Swinburne gives you the engineering degree in four years with paid-placement potential (to verify). Melbourne takes five years and delays engineering depth. Melbourne would move above Swinburne if you value its brand, its research profile and its team more than a shorter route.
- 4 over 5: Melbourne's team and brand are stronger, but the gap is small. RMIT would move up if you want to avoid the five-year Melbourne route.
- 5 over 6: RMIT's metropolitan industry ties and location are a better fit for a Melbourne-based student. This is a judgement call that I have not verified with course data.
- 6 over 7 to 10: staying in Melbourne avoids relocation cost.

**The plan's weak point.** The double degree is not automatically better, and your own concerns are right. For F1 and motorsport, hiring teams look at what you built and how well you understand engineering. A Finance major adds little to that. A second degree and an extra year are also not what they screen for.

## 3. What matters most for the motorsport goal

Four kinds of value, which are not equal.

| Kind | What it is | Examples | Weight for F1 / motorsport |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct engineering skill | Things you can demonstrate and be tested on | Dynamics, materials, CAD, FEA, controls, manufacturing, Formula SAE design | Highest |
| Transferable practical intuition | Carries over, not motorsport-specific | Workshop time, data analysis, teamwork, documentation, project management | High |
| Business and finance knowledge | Real but narrow | Cost and budget control, business cases, sponsorship, commercial awareness, financial modelling | Low to moderate for engineering roles, higher for team management or commercial roles. Formula SAE itself includes a cost and business assessment. |
| Interest in motorsport | Enthusiasm | Watching F1, knowing the teams | Low. It earns the interview, not the job. |

Quantitative skills also transfer in both directions, but they come from the mathematics and modelling in the engineering degree, not from the finance major. So engineering study already builds most of what quantitative finance roles want.

## 5. The central question: double degree or single degree?

**My answer: finish in the single Mechanical degree with finance electives, and treat the double degree as the second choice. But when you fill in VTAC, the order may differ. See 5.3.**

### 5.1 Comparison

| Factor | Engineering only (with finance electives) | Engineering + Commerce |
|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Highest | Same core units, less spare capacity to go deeper |
| WAM / GPA | Easier to protect | Harder, workload split across two disciplines |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best | Tighter. Possible, but costs sleep or grades |
| Duration, full load | About 4 years | About 5 years |
| Duration, 3 units a semester | About 5.3 years | About 6.7 years |
| Cost / HECS-HELP | Lowest total | Roughly one extra year of fees and delayed earnings (see 5.2) |
| Finance flexibility | Good: electives, later Master's, graduate programmes, CFA | Best: formal major |
| Employability, motorsport | Strongest | Neutral. Commerce does not help here. |
| Employability, finance | Reasonable. Engineers with strong maths are hired into quantitative, consulting and some banking roles (verify current graduate intake rules). | Strongest |
| Opportunity cost | Low | A full extra year plus reduced team involvement |

**Reasoning.**
- Your primary goal is motorsport. Employers look at your Formula SAE role, your design and manufacturing evidence and your engineering results. A Finance major does not compete on any of those.
- Engineering alone does give a path into finance later, through quantitative roles, a later Master of Finance or the CFA. Verify current intake rules.
- The assumption to challenge: the double degree keeps both doors more open on paper, but time is finite, and in practice it narrows the motorsport door.

### 5.2 Duration, cost and HECS-HELP (arithmetic)

- **How units work (recalled, verify):** a full-time load at Monash is four units (24 credit points) a semester. A 4-year degree is about 8 semesters of 4 units. The double degree is about 10 semesters.
- **Three units a semester is 75% of full time.** Single degree: 8 divided by 0.75 gives about 10.7 semesters, or **about 5.3 years**. Double degree: 10 divided by 0.75 gives about 13.3 semesters, or **about 6.7 years**. These are estimates. Actual length depends on unit sequencing and prerequisites, so ask the faculty.
- **Fees are charged per unit, not per year (recalled).** So a lighter load does not raise total tuition. It spreads the same fees over more years. The real costs of going slower are delayed full-time income and a longer wait to start repaying the loan at a professional salary.
- **Extra units cost extra.** The double degree is more units than the single degree, so total fees are higher by roughly the extra year of units, and Commonwealth-supported rates differ by discipline. I have not verified the current student contribution amounts. Look up the current Monash fee tables for engineering and commerce.
- **HECS-HELP (recalled, verify at studyassist.gov.au):** for a Commonwealth-supported place, you pay a student contribution, which you can defer through a HELP loan and repay through the tax system once your income passes a threshold. The loan is indexed. I recall recent government changes to indexation and to debt amounts but have not verified them, so check the current rules.
- **Forgone salary:** the double adds about one year before a full-time engineering job. At 3 units a semester, the double delays you by about 2.7 years compared with a full-load single degree. A graduate engineer salary is plausibly in the range of the low-to-mid $70,000s to $80,000s a year (my rough estimate, unverified). So one extra year is on the order of $70,000 to $80,000 of forgone gross income, and 2.7 years is roughly $190,000 to $220,000, before the extra fees. Treat these as order-of-magnitude figures, not quotes.
- **Part-time work caveat:** a lighter load frees time but not necessarily money. Most of the gain is marks and team time.

### 5.3 VTAC mechanism and what to put first

**How VTAC offers work:** you list courses in order of preference. VTAC makes you an offer for the highest-ranked course on your list whose selection rank you meet. It will never offer you a lower preference than one you qualify for.

So the order is not the same as "which degree I want to finish in":
- If you list the **single degree first** and meet its rank, you get the single degree, and the double is never offered even if you qualified for it.
- If you list the **double first** and meet its rank, you get the double. If you do not meet its rank, you fall through to the single.

**What decides it is whether Monash lets you drop Commerce later.** I recall that moving from a double degree to the single degree is usually easier than adding a second degree later, but I could not verify this at Monash. **Get the answer in writing.**
- **If Monash lets you drop Commerce after first year (or later) without a penalty:** list the **double degree first and the single second**. You lose nothing, because you can drop to the single if your first-year marks or team workload say so. You keep the finance option you would otherwise lose permanently.
- **If Monash does not allow dropping, or only with a long delay:** list the **single first**, because you do not want to be locked into a double you may not want.
- **If you cannot get an answer before preferences close:** assume dropping is possible but treat the first year as a test, and go with the single-degree workload mindset.

This resolves the question rather than postponing it: the VTAC order depends on one fact, which is cheap to check, and the ranking of what you want to finish in does not change.

### 5.4 When the double degree is the right call (what would change my ranking)
- Finance is as likely a career as motorsport.
- You can keep a strong WAM and a real Formula SAE role at the same time. Test this in first year.
- Monash lets you drop Commerce without penalty, which makes the double a low-risk start.

**On three units per semester:** it stretches the degree (see 5.2) but protects marks and team time. If you use it, plan it: for example, a lighter load only in semesters when you lead a sub-team, not by default.

### 6.2 Electives and extras to look for
- **Manufacturing, materials and composites.** Machining, composites, welding and knowing what can actually be made. Search "manufacturing" and "materials" in the Handbook. This is not a stated core and may be an elective.
- **Numerical methods, FEA and computational modelling.** Search "finite element" and "numerical". Learn the limits of the software, not only the buttons.
- **Optimisation and data analysis.** Choosing trade-offs and reading data-acquisition logs.
- **Automotive or vehicle-specific units.** I do not know whether Monash offers one. Search "vehicle" and "automotive".

### 6.3 Finance electives that cost the least technical time
Recalled Monash Business prefixes (medium to low confidence): **ACC** (accounting, for example ACC1100 for financial accounting), **ECC / ETC** (economics and statistics, for example ECC1000 for microeconomics and ETC1000 for business statistics) and **BTF** (banking and finance). Take one or two introductory units, not an upper-level load. You already study Economics in VCE, so you start with an advantage. Check whether Monash lets engineering students take Commerce units as free electives or as a defined minor, and how many credit points that uses.

### 6.4 MMA3101
I do not recognise this code, and its prefix does not match the usual Monash mechanical engineering prefix (MEC), so I cannot say what it covers. Do not guess. Look it up in the Handbook and check four things: which faculty offers it, whether it is a core, elective or minor unit, what its prerequisites are, and which semester it runs. Then apply this test: **take it if it clearly maps to a row in the table in 6.1 or 6.2 (a technical skill) and does not cost a core unit's place. Skip it if it is a general-interest unit.**

## 7. Most important thing: Formula SAE

- Join the team in your **first year**, even in a small role. Early involvement turns into sub-team leadership.
- Pick a sub-team that builds your target skills: suspension, chassis, aerodynamics, powertrain or data acquisition.
- Document what you do: CAD files, drawings, test results and design reports. This is your portfolio.
- Check how many first-years each team accepts and how it selects.
