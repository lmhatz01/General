## 1. The direct answer

## 2. What motorsport employers and teams screen for

Worked backwards from the outcome (a hireable junior engineer in motorsport or high-performance engineering):

1. Strong fundamentals: dynamics, mechanics of materials, thermofluids, control, numerical methods **[Judgement]**.
2. Hands-on build experience, for you a Formula SAE team. A finished car shows design-for-manufacture, testing, teamwork and deadlines.
3. Tool fluency: parametric CAD, basic FEA, data acquisition and analysis, MATLAB or Python.
4. A solid academic record, which matters most for graduate programs and any later move into finance.

Commerce appears nowhere on that list. It matters only if you pivot.

Geography **[Judgement]**: F1 teams are concentrated in the UK. Australian routes run through Supercars, GT, suppliers and Formula SAE alumni, and some graduates then move overseas. No Australian degree alone gets you into F1. Formula SAE record, WAM and willingness to relocate matter more than the degree name.

## 3. Single versus double, analysed

Assume a full-load semester is four units and Monash engineering is about 32 units (four years) with the double about 40 units (five years) **[Check both; these are my recollection]**.

### 3a. Duration at different loads [Arithmetic, from those assumptions]

| Plan | Units | Units per semester | Approx. years (two semesters a year, no summer study) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single engineering, full load | 32 | 4 | 4 |
| Single engineering, 3 units/semester | 32 | 3 | about 5.3 |

### 3b. Comparison

| Factor | Engineering only | Double, full load | Double, 3 units/semester |

Whether commerce units mark harder or easier than engineering ones is unknown to me and not claimed | Lower pressure per semester; still more total assessed units |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best | Worst: team deadlines (design freeze, build, competition) collide with exams | Feasible, paid for with extra years |
| Duration | About 4 years **[Check]** | About 5 years **[Check]** | About 6.5 to 7 years |
| Cost | One degree of units. Contribution band depends on the discipline **[Check Study Assist]** | HELP debt scales with units taken, so more units means more debt. Slowing to 3 units per semester does not raise the debt per unit but stretches it over more years **[Check indexation rules]** | Same debt as full-load double; the extra cost is delayed earning |
| Opportunity cost | Lowest | One extra year before an engineering salary | About 2 to 3 years behind the single-degree peer |
| Finance flexibility | Real but narrower: graduate programs, quantitative roles, later Master's. Some banking doors may favour a finance degree | Strongest, credible on a CV | Same |

### 3c. Reading [Judgement]

- **The weak assumption is that more credentials mean more options.** For motorsport the scarce resources are workshop time and WAM, and the double spends both.
- **The finance fallback is probably over-insured.** A high-WAM engineering degree already opens graduate, consulting and quantitative roles. A few finance electives or a later postgraduate degree cover much of the rest.
- **If you would genuinely enjoy finance for its own sake, the double is good.** If finance is an escape route only, take the single.
- **Compare like with like:** the alternative to the 3-unit double is not only the full-load double. It is also the single degree plus a later Master's (about the same total years as a double, but you get engineering salary in between), or the single degree at a lighter load if Formula SAE is heavy. I cannot tell you which is cheapest without current fees.

### 3d. The "decide after year 1" idea: what must be true [Check]

I previously leaned on "year 1 overlaps heavily, so waiting costs little". That is **unverified**. It fails if the double is a separate VTAC course with its own selection rank and its own first-year commerce units, and if adding Commerce later needs an internal transfer with a WAM threshold or a limited number of places. In that case starting in single engineering does not keep the option open.

**Test it before you finalise preferences:** email Monash (course enquiries) and ask: (1) can a single-degree engineering student add Commerce later, by when, and with what WAM or places limit; (2) how much of year-1 credit counts in both courses; (3) is the double's entry rank higher than the single's. Decision rule:

- If adding Commerce later is easy and year-1 credit carries over: list the single first (rank 1 stands).
- If it is hard or capped and you want the option: list the double first; you can always drop Commerce later if that is permitted **[Check]**, whereas adding is the hard direction.
- If you do not care about Commerce: the single stands either way.

## 5. Subject-level advice (Monash)

**MMA3101:** I could not open the Handbook and I do not recognise the unit, so I will not describe its content. By convention a 3xxx code is a third-year unit **[Judgement, not verified]**. Judge it by content using the rubric below; do not decide on its name.

**How to find the right units:** in the Monash Handbook search the code prefixes for your mechanical specialisation (my recollection is that mechanical and aerospace units sit under prefixes such as MEC and MAE, and common core under ENG **[Unverified; check]**), then read the unit outcomes, assessment mix and prerequisites for each.

**Rubric (score each unit 0 to 2 on every row; prefer units that score on the rows you most need):**

| Capability | What to look for in the unit | How it contributes to motorsport |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics and numerical methods | ODEs, linear algebra, numerical solution, optimisation | Lap-time simulation, suspension models, data fitting, design trade-offs |
| Dynamics and vibration | Rigid-body dynamics, vibration, kinematics | Suspension kinematics, ride and handling, driveline |
| Vehicle dynamics | Tyre models, load transfer, handling | Directly the car's behaviour; closest thing to your stated goal |
| Control | Feedback, state-space, simulation | Traction control, engine and motor management, active aero |
| Materials and solid mechanics | Stress, failure, composites, FEA | Sizing wishbones and chassis, layup decisions |
| Manufacturing and design for manufacture | Machining, tolerancing, DFM and DFA | Parts that can actually be made, fit and assembled |
| CAD and design projects | Parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings | Day-to-day team skill |
| Thermofluids and aerodynamics | Fluid mechanics, heat transfer, CFD if offered | Aero and cooling |
| Programming and data analysis | MATLAB or Python, signal handling | Data acquisition and logging analysis |
| Finance electives | Corporate finance, markets | Serves the fallback; adds almost no technical depth |

**Direct skill versus transferable intuition versus interest:** coursework and Formula SAE build direct engineering skill. Karting, workshop hobbies, your Product Design and Technologies projects and VCE Physics build transferable practical intuition. Watching F1 is interest: fine, but not evidence.

## 6. Where the plan is weak

1. **No Specialist Mathematics.** Many engineering students have it. Methods plus Physics is normally enough for entry **[Check]**, but first-year mathematics may be demanding. Revise before starting; use support services.
2. **Assuming the double degree is a safety net.** It adds cost, time and WAM risk and may leave you average at both.
3. **Assuming Formula SAE is automatic.** Teams select members and the workload is heavy. Learn the recruitment process and talk to members.
4. **Underestimating the overseas gap for F1.** Plan for possible relocation.
5. **Planning around unverified facts, including the ones in this answer.** Section 9 shows the order to verify them.

## 7. What would change the ranking

- You decide you mainly want finance: the double and Melbourne go above single engineering.

## 8. Timeline and VTAC [Check all dates with VTAC; these follow typical past-year patterns, not the 2026 calendar]

Today is 11 November 2026.

- **Main application:** the timely deadline is normally at the end of September, so your application should already be lodged. Late applications with a fee are usually accepted until about late November or early December.
- **Preferences:** these can usually be changed until a deadline in December, **after ATAR results are released** (typically mid-December). Confirm the exact change-of-preference date this week.
- **VCE exams:** the written exams run into mid-November. Do not spend this period researching courses beyond one short email to Monash; protect your exam results first.
- **Offers:** the first round normally comes in January, with later rounds after.
- **What you can still do now:** write the Monash enquiry (3d), check the exact course codes, and finalise your order in December once you know your ATAR.
