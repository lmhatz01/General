## 2. The plan's weak points, said plainly

1. **"Double degree = better" is an assumption, not a fact.** For the primary goal (motorsport/F1 mechanical engineering), the Commerce half adds about a year and roughly a fifth to a quarter of your credit points in units that a Formula SAE scrutineer, a chassis lead or an F1 recruiter will never read. Its value is as a hedge, and hedges should be bought cheaply.
2. **Spreading the double over about three units a semester is the weakest idea in the plan.** Monash units are 6 credit points [recall]; a 5-year double is 240 credit points [recall]; at 3 units (18 cp) a semester that is 240 / 18 = 13.3 semesters, about 6.7 years. Fees per unit do not change (HECS-HELP is charged per unit), so the cost is not the fee itself: it is indexation on a debt that sits longer, about 1.7 extra years before graduate earnings, and graduating a cohort behind people who were running cars at the same age. It also does not solve your actual problem. The scarce resource is not calendar time, it is concentrated attention in exam weeks and the Formula SAE build crunch, and a lighter load only partly relieves that. Take the full load of one degree, or the full load of both, not a diluted middle.
3. **Formula SAE is the credential that gets looked at, not the degree title.** Cars that ran, subsystems you owned, drawings and test data you can show. Do not expect finance study to substitute for that, and do not expect a very high WAM to substitute either: both together matter, and the double puts them in tension.
4. **"Engineering alone cannot lead into finance" is false.** Engineers routinely enter finance through graduate programs, quantitative and analyst roles, consulting, a Master of Finance or MBA later, and the CFA program. A finance credential bought later costs less lost technical time than one bought concurrently, and by then you will know whether you want it.
5. **Interest in motorsport is not skill.** Following F1 is interest. Karting or driving gives transferable intuition only. Parametric CAD, FEA sanity-checking, machining, laminating and a documented design are direct skill. I rank courses on direct skill development.
6. **Be honest about the destination.** Australian passenger-car manufacturing ended in 2016-17 (Ford 2016, Holden and Toyota 2017). "F1 engineering" is realistically a move to the UK/Europe after a degree, via Formula SAE results, a strong transcript, placements and often a Master's or a graduate job abroad. Local motorsport employment (for example Supercars teams and suppliers in Victoria [low-confidence on named employers]) is the nearer rung. No Australian university choice guarantees an F1 pathway; the choice shifts probabilities, mainly through team access, WAM and placements.

## 3. The single-versus-double answer, with its dependencies

**Recommendation: Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, as a single degree at a full load, with finance added cheaply if the structure allows. Take the double only at full load and only if you can say plainly that finance is a co-equal outcome you would be content with.**

This conclusion does **not** depend on the unverified electives question. Both branches:

- **If the Monash single degree has room for a finance/commerce minor-sized sequence (check: 24 credit points = 4 units of free electives, and whether Engineering students may take a Commerce finance minor):** do that. It gives you a finance signal on the transcript at a fraction of the double's cost.
- **If it does not:** still do the single degree. You then add finance later: one or two first-year Commerce-style units as extra-curricular if allowed, CFA Level 1 reading, then a Master of Finance or graduate program if your interest holds. Nothing in this branch hurts the motorsport goal.

**What to ask Monash (email the Engineering student advisors, ask for the answers in writing):**
1. How many free elective units does the Mechanical specialisation of the single Engineering (Honours) course have, and can they be used for a finance minor?
2. Can a student transfer from the single degree into Engineering/Commerce after first year, under what conditions (results, places)? And can a double-degree student drop to the single degree?
3. The reverse-transfer point matters: at many universities it is easier to step down from a double to a single than up from a single to a double. **If Monash confirms that**, then the double has option value, and the right VTAC order could be double first, provided you are willing to drop Commerce after year 1 if the load hurts your WAM or team work. If Monash says the opposite, keep the single first.
4. How are Formula SAE/Monash Motorsport hours treated in workload advice for double-degree students?

Whether Lukas's WAM suffers in the double is a prediction, not a fact. The honest evidence is indirect: the double has about 25 percent more units to pass and two different assessment cultures, and it competes with the team for the same hours.

## 4. Entry requirements, adjustments and schemes (all [recall] unless stated)

- **Prerequisites.** Monash Engineering: Units 3 and 4 English (any), Mathematical Methods, and a science (Physics or Chemistry) [recall]. Specialist Mathematics is not a prerequisite [recall] but is recommended. You have Methods and Physics, so you meet the usual prerequisite pattern at Monash, RMIT, Swinburne and Deakin [recall]. Melbourne's engineering pathway is a Bachelor of Science (Engineering Systems major) into the Master of Engineering [recall] and has its own prerequisites; check. The Monash Commerce half needs Methods [recall].
- **Selection rank = ATAR + adjustment.** VTAC lists, per course, subject-based **increments** (extra points, small, for particular Units 3/4 subjects such as Mathematical Methods, Specialist Mathematics, Physics) and the maximum. Monash and Melbourne use these; the amounts differ by course and year. Open VTAC Course Search for each course, and read "Selection criteria" and "Increments". I will not quote amounts.
- **Equity and access schemes.** VTAC's Special Entry Access Scheme (SEAS) and each university's own schemes (Monash and others run access and equity rounds [recall]) can raise your selection rank if you meet disadvantage criteria. The SEAS application window for 2027 entry has likely already closed (usually around September-October [low-confidence]); check immediately if you have any reason to qualify.
- **Early offers and scholarships.** Several universities run Year 12 early-offer or scholarship programs (Monash, Melbourne, Swinburne, RMIT, UQ, UNSW, Sydney all have some [low-confidence]); most close before results. Check each university's "scholarships" page this week. Monash Motorsport sponsors and engineering-faculty scholarships are worth asking about once enrolled.
- **ATAR context (ordinal only, [low-confidence]).** Melbourne's engineering pathway generally has a higher selection rank than Monash, which is generally higher than RMIT, Swinburne and Deakin; UNSW, Sydney and UQ engineering are selective too. This is a rough ordering, not data. VTAC's published "lowest selection rank to receive an offer" for 2026 is the only figure to use, and it is a guide, not a guarantee.
- **VTAC offers you the highest preference you qualify for**, so the order below should reflect what you want, not your guess at cut-offs.

## 5. Cost and HECS-HELP (all [recall]; approximate)

Approximate 2026 student contributions per full-time-equivalent year (indexed annually; verify on studyassist.gov.au and each university's fees page):

- Engineering band: roughly **$9,000-$10,000** per year.
- Commerce/business band: roughly **$15,000-$17,000** per year.

Approximate totals, not quotes:

| Option | Duration (full load) | Rough student contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering (Honours) alone | 4 years | about $36,000-$40,000 |
| Engineering/Commerce double | 5 years | about $50,000-$65,000 (depends on how many units sit in each band) |
| Double at 3 units a semester | about 6.7 years | same fee per unit as the 5-year plan; plus indexation on the debt and about 1.7 extra years before graduate pay |

How HELP works (recall; legislation changed in 2025):
- You can defer the contribution as a HELP debt. It is indexed each 1 June (recently the lower of CPI or wage growth [recall]).
- Repayment is income-contingent and (recall) now marginal: nothing repaid below a threshold of about $67,000 and a percentage of income above it (15 cents in the dollar at the first step [low-confidence]). Check current thresholds.
- A one-off 20 percent cut was applied to existing HELP debts in 2025 [recall]; it will not apply to your future debt.
- A Commonwealth Supported place has no general time limit that I know of; what you must watch is the rule that failing too many units can lose your Commonwealth Supported eligibility (the 50 percent pass requirement under the Job-ready Graduates changes [recall]). That, not duration, is the real risk of overloading yourself.
- Opportunity cost is bigger than the fee: an extra year on a graduate engineering salary (very roughly $70,000-$80,000 [low-confidence]) is worth more than the fee gap between the double and the single.

## 6. Ranking, with reasons

Ranking is by fit to the primary goal (motorsport/mechanical engineering with strong technical depth and team access), with a separate safety note.

### 1. Monash, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, single degree at full load (4 years)

- **Team on your doorstep.** Monash Motorsport is a large, multi-sub-team organisation with sponsors and a track record at the Australasian Formula SAE-A event (Winton, in December [recall]). I cannot verify its recent results; look them up on the team's site and the Formula SAE-A results page, and compare with UQ Racing and UNSW Redback Racing.
- **Four years at full load protects WAM**, which feeds honours class, graduate programs, and any Master's abroad.
- **Fit to your stated skills list.** The Monash course teaches mathematics, mechanics, dynamics, materials, thermodynamics/fluids, computing and design [recall]; the team lets you apply them to suspension, aero, powertrain and data acquisition.
- **Finance stays open** by the branches in section 3.
- Honest weakness: cohort size is large, so access to team leadership is competitive; and the team's first-year recruitment window must be met.

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours) / Commerce, Finance major (5 years at full load)

Choose this only if finance is a co-equal outcome, or the step-down option in section 3 is confirmed and you will use year 1 as a trial. At full load only. Plan summer terms for engineering placement or industry-based learning (check availability), not for Commerce units. Cap your Formula SAE role honestly: sub-team member in years 1-2, lead only if WAM holds. It ranks above other universities because it keeps the Monash Motorsport advantage and the hedge; it ranks below the single degree because the extra year and cost buy a credential that can be purchased later.

### 3. RMIT, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical [check course name and code on RMIT and VTAC]

[recall] Practical, with industry-linked and work-integrated learning, a Formula SAE team (RMIT Racing [low-confidence on name]) and city-campus access to Victorian aerospace and automotive suppliers. Evidence quality for ranking: moderate on course style, low on team results. Choose it if you miss the Monash selection rank, or if you want hands-on labs more than research depth.

### 4. Swinburne, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, with Professional Placement (about 5 years including the industry year) [check]

[recall] Its built-in industry year is the draw: a paid year at an employer is concrete evidence. It has a Formula SAE team [low-confidence on name]. The cost is one more year and lower technical prestige than Monash. Better than RMIT if you value the placement above team strength.

### 5. University of Melbourne, Bachelor of Science (Engineering Systems major) then Master of Engineering (Mechanical), 3+2 = 5 years [recall; check]

Excellent mathematics and research reputation, and a good route if you might do research or a Master's abroad. Against it: five years, probably the highest selection rank of the Victorian options (ordinal, [low-confidence]), and I cannot verify that its Formula SAE team matches Monash's. Take it only if you value the Melbourne brand or a research track more than team access.

### 6. UQ (Brisbane), Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical [check]

UQ Racing is widely regarded as one of the strongest Formula SAE-A teams [recall; verify against the results]. It is the best out-of-state option if team strength is your main criterion. Relocation costs roughly $12,000-$20,000 a year more than living at home (rent in a shared house plus living costs [low-confidence estimate]); you lose the Monash team advantage for a roughly equal one. Rank 6, because that cost buys no clear technical gain over staying.

### 7. UNSW (Sydney), Mechanical Engineering (Honours) [check]

Redback Racing is the UNSW Formula SAE team [recall]. UNSW has a large industry and finance ecosystem in Sydney, which is relevant if you value the finance hedge; it also has a high rank and the highest living costs of the options here. Rank 7 for cost and distance.

### 8. Deakin, Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), Mechanical [check]

Deakin has a motorsport team [low-confidence on name] and Geelong-area advanced-materials and composites capability (Carbon Nexus [recall]). It is the **safety** that should still appear in your preference list, because a safety is judged by the chance of an offer, not by fit. It is ranked here for fit, which is why it sits below the two interstate options with stronger teams.

### 9. Sydney and Adelaide

Both are credible mechanical engineering programs with Formula SAE teams (Sydney: name unclear [unknown]; Adelaide: Adelaide University Motorsport [recall], and note Adelaide's merger with UniSA into a new institution from 2026 [recall], so course codes and structures need re-checking). Relocation with no differentiating advantage over UQ or UNSW puts them last.

### Overseas

Not justified for undergraduate entry: full international fees, visas, no HELP. The realistic overseas play is a **Master's or graduate job in the UK/Europe after an Australian honours degree** with Formula SAE and placement experience. Treat it as year 3-4 planning.

## 8. What would change the ranking

- Monash confirms that stepping down from the double to the single is easy and stepping up is hard: put the double first in VTAC, and treat year 1 as a trial.
- Monash confirms no room for finance electives and no later transfer: single degree still first; the finance route becomes graduate study.
