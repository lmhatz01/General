ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Cost and duration maths for the 3-units-per-semester option is wrong
Where: "That means seven years of study, a full year or two later into the workforce than the single degree, and two or three more years of fees and HECS-HELP accrual." (Worked example section)
Problem: The same answer calculates 40 units at 6 per year = about 6.7 years, versus 4 years for the single degree. That is about 2.7 years later, not "a full year or two". The cost claim is also wrong. Student contributions are charged per unit, so the same 40-unit double costs the same in fees at 3 units a semester as at 4. Spreading it adds no fee years, only calendar time and delayed earnings. The real extra fee against the single degree is the roughly 8 commerce units. The answer then dismisses the stretched option ("does not buy you more time for Formula SAE"). But a lighter per-semester load is exactly what the student said it would free up (task: "about three units per semester ... to free time for Monash Formula SAE"). The answer never compares a single degree at a reduced load, or the double at 4 units, against this. The central double-versus-single question is answered with a flawed worked example.
