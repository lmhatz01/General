## The system you are optimising

Five things interact, and the degree choice affects all of them.

- **Technical profile.** Motorsport employers hire on fundamentals, CAD, build experience and visible project ownership.
- **Time.** Commerce units compete directly with study, Formula SAE and sleep. I have not quantified the hours, because they depend on your load.
- **WAM.** It matters for honours, graduate programs and master's admission. A heavier load tends to lower it.
- **Cost and duration.** An extra year means delayed salary, extra living costs and more HECS-HELP debt.
- **Optionality.** Moving from engineering to finance is common, for example via a graduate diploma, a master's, or the CFA. Moving from finance to engineering usually means redoing a degree.

The second-order effect is that the choice that protects Formula SAE time and WAM early also keeps both futures open. The choice that locks in extra years costs most in the area you rank first.

## Honest weaknesses in the plan

- **"F1 engineering" is a tiny market.** There are about ten teams, mostly in the UK, and direct hiring from Australia is rare. Realistic paths are Formula SAE leadership, then Supercars, other motorsport, or high-performance engineering (aerospace, defence, automotive R&D), often with UK or European experience or a master's. The fallback is the likely outcome, so make sure it is good.
- **Interest in motorsport is not a skill.** What counts is a car you helped build, test and document.
- **Finance as a fallback is mostly already covered** by an engineering degree with good grades. This is a judgement, not a verified fact.
- **You are weighing too many options.** One preference order that keeps the doors open at the lowest cost is better than waiting for perfect information.

## Double degree vs single degree

| Factor | Engineering alone (finance electives) | Engineering + Commerce (Finance) |
|---|---|---|
| Technical depth | Highest. More room for design, dynamics, control, manufacturing, and projects. | Lower. Commerce units compete with engineering electives and capstone time. |
| WAM risk | Lower. One discipline. | Higher. Two sets of assessments. Check how each WAM is reported and used. |
| Formula SAE capacity | Best. You can take a real role. | Harder, unless you slow down. |
| Duration | 4 years full-time. | About 5 years full-time (check the exact course), longer if slowed. |
| Cost | Lower. | About a year more of fees (debt) and living costs. Check 2027 bands and HELP rules. |
| Employability | Strong in engineering. Finance is reachable through graduate pathways. | A broader signal for finance and consulting, and a possible edge for bank graduate programs. |
| Flexibility | Good. Finance can be added later by graduate study. | Best at the start, but expensive to carry. |
| Opportunity cost | Lowest. | A year or more, plus less time for projects and internships. |

**Slowing the double to about 3 units per semester.** It protects WAM and Formula SAE time, but it adds years of cost and delay and may hit HELP limits or maximum course durations. Verify both with Monash. If you need to slow down to fit Formula SAE, the single degree plus finance electives is the cleaner version of the same trade.

**My recommendation:** put the single degree first. If Monash confirms that stepping down from the double in the first year or two is easy, list the double first only if you accept that being offered it means taking on its load from day one. That is a tactic, not a better ranking. Two questions for Monash:

1. Can I move from the double to the single in the first year or two?
2. Can I add Commerce later, and under what conditions (a WAM threshold, remaining units)?

**What would make the double better:** you cope with the load after a trial semester, your engineering WAM stays high, you hold a real Formula SAE role, and finance becomes the more attractive career.

## Ranking, with reasons

### 1. Monash Mechanical Engineering (single)
- **Why first:** a well-regarded mechanical program plus Monash Motorsport, one of Australia's leading Formula SAE teams (my background knowledge, unverified). That is the biggest single lever for your primary goal. Finance electives, if allowed (see the key assumption), cover the secondary goal without a second degree.
- **Check:** current entry requirements, structure, minors and electives, and the Monash Motorsport recruitment process.

### 2. Monash Engineering + Commerce (Finance)
- **Why second:** the same technical core plus a finance credential. The costs are an extra year, the load, and pressure on WAM and Formula SAE time. Choose it only if finance is a co-equal goal.

### 3. RMIT Mechanical Engineering
- **Why above Melbourne:** a 4-year route with a hands-on culture, strong CAD and workshop emphasis, Redback Racing, and Melbourne industry links. Entry is typically lower, which reduces risk. It matches your stated skill list (CAD, manufacturing, build experience) better than a 5-year, more theoretical route.
- **Weakness:** lower research prestige than Monash or Melbourne.

### 4. University of Melbourne: BSc then MEng (Mechanical)
- **Strengths:** prestige, research, Melbourne University Racing, and a breadth model suited to a later move into finance.
- **Weaknesses:** 5 years in total, which is the same extra-year penalty applied to the double degree, plus a more theoretical start and likely higher selection ranks. Prestige counts for less than build experience in motorsport hiring.
- **Check:** the current pathway, prerequisites, and any Specialist Maths expectation.

### 5. Swinburne and 6. Deakin
- **Swinburne:** an industry-placement stream that gives paid, real engineering experience. Check the team's current activity.
- **Deakin:** mechanical engineering with work placement and Deakin Motorsport. Check the current structure.
- They rank below RMIT because I have less specific evidence of their motorsport and CAD profile. That could change on checking.

### 7. Interstate and overseas
- **UQ first:** UQ Racing is widely regarded as a strong Formula SAE programme (unverified). It ranks below the Victorian options only because relocation adds cost and distance, not because of quality. It rises if you are willing to leave Victoria.
- **UNSW, Sydney and Adelaide:** academically strong, with Formula SAE or motorsport teams that I have not verified. They are viable only if you relocate. I have not ranked UWA, because I have no basis to.
- **Overseas:** not justified at undergraduate level given cost and visa constraints. A UK master's (for example Cranfield, Oxford Brookes, Imperial) after your Australian degree is the realistic route, and WAM matters for admission.
