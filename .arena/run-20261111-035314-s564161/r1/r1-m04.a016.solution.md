**The double-degree question, answered directly.** The obvious answer is "do the Monash double, it keeps both doors open". I tested it and mostly reject it as your first choice. Your stated priority is motorsport, and what is scarce on that path is technical depth and time with a Formula SAE team. A double degree costs both. Finance is your fallback, and engineers already get into finance. So: single degree first, double second, and decide about the commerce add-on during first year using real results.

## Where your plan is weak

- **"Double degree = flexibility" is partly an illusion.** Banks, consultancies and analyst programs routinely hire engineers because of their maths and modelling. Finance can also be added later by a postgraduate course or the CFA exams. (Judgement, widely observed.)
- **F1 is extremely scarce.** Most teams are in the UK and few Australians get in. Judge each option as a route to a very good engineering career with a real shot at motorsport, not on F1 alone.
- **Formula SAE is a strong signal, not a guarantee.** Team leaders and people who built and tested real parts get noticed. Members who only turn up do not.
- **A very high WAM matters less than you think for motorsport.** A portfolio of real projects counts for more. WAM matters more for competitive finance graduate programs. (Judgement.)
- **Your Year 12 subjects fit.** Methods and Physics are the ones that matter. Economics helps with commerce. Product Design and Technologies gives you design process and basic CAD, not yet engineering-grade CAD.

## Ranking and reasoning

### 1. Monash, single Engineering (Honours), Mechanical

- **Why it is first.** It wins on the two things you said matter most, and that holds whether or not finance electives are possible. It gives you the full mechanical sequence and a final-year project without sharing credit points. It puts you at the same campus as **Monash Motorsport**, a long-running, well-regarded Formula SAE team (Recalled; confirm current recruitment and structure).
- **Duration.** About four years full time (Recalled, confirm).
- **Finance on the side.** Check the course map for free elective slots or a minor, and for whether business or commerce units are allowed (Uncertain). Engineering courses leave limited electives, so expect room for a few units, not a major. Even without them you have two fallbacks: apply for a graduate finance program with an engineering degree, or do a graduate diploma or Master of Finance later.
- **Real cost of choosing this.** You do not leave with a commerce degree. If you decide in third year that you want banking, you start with a short postgraduate course instead of already holding a major. That costs a year or less, and you will have the engineering degree and a motorsport record either way. That is a modest cost. It is not nothing, and it is the main reason the double stays second.
- **What would flip it.** If the course map shows no elective room at all and you want finance study in parallel, the double moves up.

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours) + Commerce (Finance)

- **Why second.** Same engineering and team access as option 1, plus a recognised finance qualification.
- **Cost.** About five years (Recalled, confirm). Commerce units compete with engineering for time and energy, and Formula SAE is the activity that gets squeezed.
- **Use it at a normal load.** Do not stretch it (see the HECS-HELP section).
- **When it overtakes option 1.** If, by the end of first year, you find you like finance units more than the engineering ones.

### 3. University of Melbourne, BSc then MEng (Mechanical)

- **Why third.** Very strong academically and well regarded by employers. It has its own Formula SAE team (Recalled; check the current name and status). Total length is about five years, three plus two (Recalled).
- **Why it is below Monash.** It takes about a year longer than a four-year single degree. The first three years contain less engineering, and you must pick the right subjects to qualify for the masters (Uncertain, confirm). Entry is also more competitive.
- **Why it is above Swinburne and RMIT.** Its brand, research depth and breadth of electives (including finance and economics subjects, which suit your Economics background) are stronger. The extra year is a real cost, which is why it is not higher.
- **When it wins.** If you want a research or postgraduate route, or you value breadth and a strong academic brand.

### 4. Swinburne, Engineering (Honours) with industry placement

- **Why.** Swinburne has a placement or industry-based learning model, which gives months of paid engineering work (Recalled; confirm what is offered for mechanical and whether it adds a year). Employers value that alongside Formula SAE.
- **Trade-off.** Lower entry tier and weaker research profile and brand than Monash or Melbourne (Judgement). Check the current team and workshop access.
- **When it wins.** If you miss the Monash cut-off or want a practical, placement-heavy route.

### 5. RMIT, Engineering (Honours), Mechanical

- **Why.** City-based, practical, with an active Formula SAE team and industry links (Recalled; confirm). A sound alternative to Swinburne.
- **Trade-off.** Similar profile to Swinburne, with less research depth than Monash.

### 6. Deakin, Mechanical Engineering

- **Why here.** It is Victorian and probably the easiest entry among these (Judgement). Its engineering is based at Geelong (Waurn Ponds), so unless you live near Geelong you are commuting or relocating (Recalled, confirm campus). Its motorsport profile and Formula SAE record are, as far as I recall, smaller than Monash and Melbourne (Uncertain).
- **Use.** A sensible safety option, not a first choice.

### 7 and 8. UNSW and UQ

- **Why they are below Deakin for you.** My ranking assumes you stay in Melbourne. Relocation adds rent and living costs and moves you away from your base. Academically UNSW and UQ are stronger than Deakin, and both have established Formula SAE teams (Recalled).
- **If you would happily move.** UNSW moves to around third, UQ to around fifth.
- **Others to know about.** University of Sydney, University of Adelaide (which merges with UniSA in 2026 per my recollection, so check the current name and structure) and UWA. They only matter if you want to move.

### Overseas

An undergraduate degree overseas is expensive and you would lose Commonwealth support. The realistic route is a postgraduate year in the UK motorsport cluster after your Australian degree. Check visa rules, funding and course content then.

## HECS-HELP, the three-units-a-semester option, and cost

How it works (Recalled; confirm on Study Assist, studyassist.gov.au):

- Most Australian-university places for citizens are Commonwealth supported, so you pay a student contribution per unit, and the amount depends on the field of study.
- You can defer it with HECS-HELP and repay through the tax system once your income passes a threshold.
- The debt is indexed each year. Recent rule changes cut debts and changed repayment, so read the current rules rather than rely on older advice.

What this means for slowing the double to three units a semester:

- **Correction to what a flat claim might suggest.** The contribution is charged per unit, so a slower load does not itself raise the total contribution. The real costs are: indexation on the balance for longer, a later start to full-time pay, and a year or more of lost earnings. That last one is the biggest. (Judgement.)
- **Its benefit.** It gives you real time for Formula SAE, which is your stated reason, and it protects WAM.
- **My view.** The reason is sound but the tool is wrong. The single degree gives you the same extra capacity without the extra years, and keeps your technical depth. If you want the double, taking a normal load and dropping a commerce unit or two is better than stretching to six or seven years. If you do stretch it, make it a deliberate choice to protect team time.
- **Check.** The exact units per semester allowed for a double at Monash, and the effect on scholarships and any visa or concession rules.

## Next steps (today is 11 November 2026)

The dates below are **typical and approximate. Confirm every one on vtac.edu.au.**

**Now (November)**
1. Contact Monash Motorsport and one backup team (for example Melbourne's or RMIT's). Ask about recruitment for 2027, first-year roles and time commitment. You do not need an offer to ask.
2. Open the Monash course pages for the single and double degrees. Write down the course codes, duration, prerequisites, elective room, any minor, and whether you can move from single to double after first year.

**Before ATAR release (usually mid-December)**
5. Find the VTAC change-of-preference closing date. It is normally in late December or early January, before the first offer round. Check it now so you do not miss it.

**When your ATAR arrives**
6. Order your preferences by what you most want, not by how likely you are to get in. VTAC offers the highest-ranked preference you qualify for, so ranking a course lower than you want does not help you. Suggested order: Monash single, then Monash double (or swap if you now lean to the double), then Melbourne BSc, then Swinburne, RMIT, Deakin. Put your most competitive wish first. Always keep at least one course you are sure to get at the end, so that you receive an offer.
7. If your ATAR is lower than expected, move Swinburne or RMIT up and rely on Formula SAE and placements.

**January to February 2027**
8. Accept your offer by the deadline, enrol and check the timetable. Confirm team recruitment dates.

**First year**
9. Join the team, learn CAD properly and take a small real design task early.
10. Decide about the commerce add-on by the end of first year, using your real results and interest.
11. Apply for a placement or internship every year.
