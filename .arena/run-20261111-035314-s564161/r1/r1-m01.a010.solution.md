# Best-suited university options for Lukas (VTAC 2027 entry)

Written 11 November 2026.

## Bottom line

1. **Monash B. Engineering (Honours), Mechanical, single degree, with finance via electives or a minor.** This is the default.
2. **Monash Engineering (Honours)/Commerce, at full load.** Choose it only if the single degree does not give you enough finance space, or after the check described below.
3. **University of Melbourne, B. Science (Engineering Systems major) into the Master of Engineering (Mechanical).**
4. **Swinburne, B. Engineering (Honours) Mechanical, with an industry placement.**
5. **RMIT, B. Engineering (Honours) Mechanical.**
6. **UNSW Mechanical (with or without Commerce).** Only if you are happy to move to Sydney.
7. **Deakin, UQ, Sydney, Adelaide.** Reasonable, no clear advantage for you.

Overseas is not recommended for undergraduate study. A UK Master's after your bachelor is the realistic overseas step.

**What gets given up by this choice:** ranking the single degree first gives up the formal commerce credential and its networks. I judge that worth giving up because your stated primary goal is motorsport, where the technical record decides outcomes.

Your plan is weakest in two places:
- **Stretching the double degree to about 3 units per semester.** If a full double is about 5 years, 3 units a semester is roughly 6.5 to 7 years (about 13 semesters at 40 to 48 units total; unit counts unverified). That is about 2.7 years longer than the 4-year single degree, and about 1.7 years longer than the normal double. It delays your graduate salary and your first engineering role, and a build-cycle team like Formula SAE suits full involvement for a few years better than thin involvement over seven.
- **Assuming the double degree is the safe choice.** For motorsport, technical depth and what you built matter most. Finance adds the least there.

## First principles: what the decision depends on

1. **Motorsport employers hire on capability, evidence and grades.** A strong WAM, plus a record of designing and making things (Formula SAE is the standard example), is the signal. Finance study on that CV is neutral at best.
2. **Formula SAE is the best undergraduate motorsport signal.** It builds CAD, manufacturing, testing, data acquisition and teamwork. Winning a subsystem lead role takes steady effort across years.
3. **Time is fixed.** The double degree adds roughly 25 percent more study (about 5 years against 4, unverified). That load comes out of WAM, the team, or sleep.
4. **A degree is not the only door into finance.** Engineers move into finance, consulting and management through graduate programs, a postgraduate degree, or professional exams such as the CFA. Some banking graduate programs prefer commerce or finance degrees, and some accept any degree. I have not verified specific employers' screens, so ask the ones you care about.
5. **F1 is a narrow target.** Most teams are in the UK, roles are few, and entry commonly involves UK experience or study. I have no source for exactly how most Australians get there, so treat this as a plausible pattern, not a statistic. The wider realistic target is motorsport, automotive, aerospace, defence and advanced manufacturing, which all value the same technical profile.

## Interests versus skills

- **Direct skill building:** CAD, FEA, machining, composites layup, data analysis, design reviews. Built by coursework and Formula SAE.
- **Transferable practical intuition:** workshop time, fixing things, karting, understanding why parts fail. It backs up the first but is not a credential.
- **Mere interest in motorsport:** watching and following. It gets you into the room and does not get you hired.

## Course comparison

### 1. Monash, B. Engineering (Honours), Mechanical (single degree)

- **Duration:** 4 years full time (stable).
- **Why first:**
  - Monash Motorsport is a large, long-running Formula SAE team (recalled, unverified). Check the current class (combustion, electric or driverless), recruitment dates and weekly hours on the team site.
  - A broad mechanical core: mathematics, mechanics, dynamics, thermofluids, materials, design and a final-year project.
  - Space for finance study via electives, or a minor, without a second degree. **Whether this exists in the current course structure is the most important fact to check.**
  - The lowest workload of the Monash options, which protects WAM and team time.
- **Weaknesses:** competitive team, so show up early. Less flexible if you later need a commerce degree for a specific employer.

**Units to look for (recalled, unverified; check codes and availability in the Handbook).** I recall Monash mechanical units use the prefix **MEC** (for example, dynamics and design units at second year) with first-year units under **ENG**, and aerospace under **AER**. Use these as search terms only, and confirm each in the Handbook.

| Unit type to search | How it helps motorsport |
|---|---|
| First-year engineering mathematics and engineering computing (ENG prefix) | Numerical methods and programming are the base for lap-time simulation and data analysis. |
| Engineering design project units (ENG first year, MEC later) | A taste of CAD, design reviews and documentation, mirroring a Formula SAE subsystem cycle. |
| Dynamics / mechanics of machines (MEC) | Suspension kinematics, load transfer, vibrations. Vehicle dynamics starts here. |
| Solid mechanics, materials and FEA | Sizing structures; why parts fail; composites basics. |
| Thermofluids and aerodynamics | Aero packages, cooling, engine and powertrain thermal limits. |
| Control systems | Traction control, engine management, active systems. |
| Manufacturing and mechanical design (MEC) | Design for manufacture and assembly, tolerancing, machining. |
| Optimisation / data analysis electives (maths, data science or engineering) | Setup and design trade-offs; sensor data. |
| Final-year project (MEC) | Pick a vehicle, aero or powertrain-linked topic if offered. This is the best coursework substitute for a team project. |

For finance on the single degree, look for a **commerce or finance minor, or free elective slots** (finance, accounting, economics units). Whether engineering students can enrol in these is a policy question for Monash: ask the faculty or check the course map.

### 2. Monash, Engineering (Honours)/Commerce (Finance) double degree

- **Duration:** about 5 years full time (recalled, unverified).
- **Entry:** I recall it as usually requiring a higher rank than single engineering. Unverified.
- **Pros:** formal finance credential, a commerce cohort, and the strongest signal for finance-screened graduate roles.
- **Cons:** more load, more pressure on WAM, and less time for the team.
- **Stretched version:** about 6.5 to 7 years, 2.7 years longer than the single degree. Not recommended (see above).

**Decision logic that works at VTAC time.** The preference order is chosen before you know your first-year WAM, so I give a rule you can apply now:

- **Default:** list the single degree above the double. If Monash lets students move from the single degree into the double after first year (this is common at universities, but **I have not verified it for Monash**; ask the faculty in writing and note the credit and any WAM or timing conditions), then the single degree is the low-regret choice: you can add commerce later with evidence about your WAM and team involvement.
- **If transfer into the double is not possible:** then the choice is real at VTAC time. List the double first only if you already know you want a finance career that needs a commerce degree and you accept a longer or heavier load. Otherwise list the single degree first.
- **When you do move up (after first year), use these tests:** (1) your full-load WAM is where you want it, (2) you have a real role in the team or an equivalent project, (3) you can name a type of finance work you want and have checked, by asking 2 to 3 employers or alumni, whether it needs commerce or accepts engineering plus a minor or postgraduate study. A type of work, not a specific named role, is a fair bar for a Year 12 student.

### Overseas

I would not go overseas for an undergraduate degree. International fees are much higher and there is no HECS-HELP. A UK Master's in motorsport or advanced mechanical engineering after your bachelor is the realistic step for F1, planned around your third year. Exchange semesters through your Australian university are a cheaper test. Check Engineers Australia accreditation for any Australian course.

## Cost and HECS-HELP

I have no verified dollar figures. What I can say with reasonable confidence (stable):
- As a domestic student in a Commonwealth Supported Place you pay a student contribution set by discipline band, and you can defer it with a HECS-HELP loan.
- The loan is indexed each year, so waiting longer to finish raises the real cost of the same degree while delaying income.
- A double degree pays for more units, and a stretched double pays for them over more years.
- Check current bands and indexation on the Study Assist site and each university's fees page. Check whether any commerce units are in a different band from engineering units.

## Where finance fits

- On a single degree, use electives or a minor for economics, accounting and finance units. Your VCE Economics helps.
- Engineering alone is not a closed door to finance. It is a respected route into consulting, management and some graduate programs. A later postgraduate degree or the CFA can add the credential.
- The double degree is mainly useful if you already know you want roles that screen for commerce.

## Stress test

1. **Monash single degree has no room for finance units, and no transfer into the double.** Then the double moves to first, if finance matters to you at all. This is the fact that could flip ranks 1 and 2.
2. **You do not get into Formula SAE.** The team is competitive. Have a second project: another team, a society, a personal build, a karting or motorsport club. Do not rest the plan on one selection.
3. **Your ATAR is lower than you hoped.** Monash single degree, Swinburne and RMIT carry the plan. Do not take the double degree.
4. **You prefer finance after first year.** The single degree keeps this open, subject to credit and transfer rules, which you should check now.
5. **You want F1 specifically.** Even the single degree is not enough alone. Plan for strong WAM, a leading team role, probably a UK Master's or UK experience, and several years of work. The double degree does not help here and may hurt.
6. **You find the maths harder without Specialist.** Use first-year support, practise early, and treat WAM in first-year maths as a warning signal, not a verdict.

## What would change the ranking

- Monash offers no workable finance route inside the single degree: the double moves up.
- A finance role you want clearly needs commerce: the double moves up.
- Your ATAR clears Melbourne and you want a Master's: Melbourne moves up.
- Monash Motorsport is weaker or less accessible than I recall: Swinburne or RMIT moves up.
- A strong preference for another city: UNSW or others move up.
