## 3. Where the plan is weak, stated plainly

1. **"F1 engineering" is a very low-probability outcome for anyone.** F1 teams employ roughly a thousand or more staff each (approximate), with hundreds in engineering, mostly based in the UK and hiring worldwide. Realistic destinations from Australia are Supercars and other race teams, motorsport suppliers, high-performance automotive, aerospace, defence and heavy equipment. Some people then move to Europe, usually after a master's or standout Formula SAE results. Treat F1 as upside, and pick the degree that is also good if F1 never happens.
2. **Interest in motorsport is not skill.** Watching racing gives motivation and vocabulary. Employers screen for demonstrated work: a car you helped build, CAD models with proper drawings and tolerances, data you analysed, parts you machined.
3. **A finance degree adds nothing to a mechanical race-engineering application.** It only pays off if you pivot. It is a hedge, and a hedge should be cheap.
4. **"Engineering alone gives a pathway into finance later" is true but slower and less certain.** Engineers enter banking, consulting and quant roles through graduate programs, a master's, or strong maths and programming skills. It is harder than going through a finance major, but it is a real route. (Medium confidence.)
5. **The Specialist Maths gap is a real risk.** Methods plus Physics is the usual minimum, but first-year engineering maths moves quickly. You can reduce this risk before February (section 9).
6. **The double degree is not automatically superior, and neither is the single degree.** The answer below is conditional on a fact I could not verify (section 5).

**Answer: engineering alone, with finance study added where the course allows it, is the better fit for your stated primary goal.** My confidence is moderate, about 65%, and that figure is a judgement, not a calculation.

Reasoning:

- The mistakes are asymmetric. If you choose engineering alone and later want finance, you can study postgraduate finance later, paid for from income. If you choose the double and never use the commerce half, you have already paid in time and WAM.

**The decision depends on one fact I could not verify: how easy it is at Monash to move between the single and the double.** Ask a Monash course adviser this exact question: "If I start in Engineering (Honours) / Commerce, can I drop to Engineering alone after first year, and if I start in Engineering alone, can I add Commerce later? What are the rules and deadlines?" Then apply this rule:

- **If dropping the commerce half is easy (no penalty, credit kept) and adding later is hard:** start in the double and decide at the end of first year. The extra first-year commerce units cost little and keep the option open.
- **If adding commerce later is also easy, or dropping is hard:** start in engineering alone.
- **If you cannot get a clear answer before preferences close:** default to engineering alone first, because it protects the thing you said matters most.

**What would flip the answer for the whole plan:** you realise finance is your real main goal; you are comfortable with a 6 year plan; or you find in first year that you enjoy commerce units more than engineering ones. These are tests to run on yourself in first year, not numeric thresholds.

## 6. Subject-level guidance

I recall the Monash engineering structure as below. **All codes here are [B] and must be checked in the Monash Handbook for the 2027 year.** Codes and names change, and I may have swapped or misremembered some.

**First-year common core (as I recall it):**
- ENG1005 Engineering mathematics: calculus, linear algebra, differential equations. *Motorsport use:* every lap-time simulation, vehicle model and data analysis rests on this. This is where missing Specialist Maths bites.
- ENG1014 Mechanics: statics and dynamics of particles and rigid bodies. *Use:* load transfer, suspension geometry, the base of vehicle dynamics.
- ENG1060 Computing for engineers: programming for engineering problems (MATLAB or Python). *Use:* data logging analysis, parameter sweeps, optimisation.
- ENG1013 Engineering modelling and design, and ENG1011 an introductory design project unit. *Use:* team design process, documentation, and prototyping habits.
- ENG1090 Foundation mathematics: a bridging unit for students without Specialist Mathematics. If it exists and applies to you, it is the most relevant fix for your gap. Check whether you are eligible and whether it counts toward your degree.

**Mechanical stream, second to fourth year (names and codes to verify):** the MEC-prefixed units cover mechanical dynamics, solid mechanics, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, mechanical design, materials and manufacturing, and control. Map each to its motorsport use:
- **Dynamics and vibration:** ride and handling, damper tuning, kinematics.
- **Solid mechanics and FEA:** sizing wishbones, uprights, chassis; checking stress and deflection.
- **Materials and manufacturing:** composites, machining, joining; design for manufacture.
- **Fluid mechanics and thermodynamics:** aero packages, cooling, intake and exhaust.
- **Control and numerical methods or optimisation:** traction control, simulation, lap-time optimisation.
- **Instrumentation, signals and data analysis:** data acquisition, a core race-engineer skill.
- **Final-year design or research project:** choose a vehicle-related or Formula SAE-linked project if the course permits.

**Selecting electives (rule):** score each candidate unit against the list above. Prefer a unit that builds dynamics, vehicle, control, numerical, data or manufacturing capability. Only then use leftover slots for economics, finance or business units. A unit that builds none of the technical capabilities is a credit-filler.

**MMA3101:** I cannot confirm its content, level, faculty or availability. Open its Handbook entry and run three checks: (1) is it open to engineering students and does it count as an elective or minor unit; (2) which of the capabilities above does it build, from its learning outcomes; (3) what are its prerequisites and teaching period. If it fails check 2, treat it as optional.

**Three kinds of benefit, kept separate:**
- *Direct engineering skill:* CAD (parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing), FEA, dynamics, manufacturing, data analysis, documented design.
- *Transferable practical intuition:* teamwork to a deadline, debugging a car, reading a data trace. These come mostly from Formula SAE and workshop time, not lectures.
- *Mere interest:* motivation and vocabulary. Useful, but not a credential.
