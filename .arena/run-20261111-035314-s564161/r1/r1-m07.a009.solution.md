## Double degree versus single degree, answered directly

**Verdict: with motorsport as the primary goal, the single degree plus finance electives is the better default. The double degree wins only if you can name a finance career you want as much as the engineering one.** That is a judgement, not a measured threshold.

**1. Technical depth.** The engineering core is the same in both [Assumption]. The double costs time and energy, not missing engineering subjects.

**2. Duration and workload, with the arithmetic shown.** Standard full-time load is 4 units a semester [Recall]. Years scale with 4 divided by units per semester.

| Plan | Units/semester | Approx. years |
|---|---|---|
| Single, standard | 4 | ~4 [Recall] |
| Single, spread | 3 | ~5.3 (4 x 4/3) |
| Double, standard | 4 | ~5 [Recall] |
| Double, spread | 3 | ~6.7 (5 x 4/3) |

Sensitivity: if the double is really 4.5 years, spread gives 6 years; if 5.5, it gives about 7.3. The spread adds roughly 1.5-2 years under any plausible length, so the verdict does not depend on the exact figure.

**3. FSAE capacity (correcting my earlier claim).** A single degree at 4 units a semester does *not* leave as much weekly time as a double at 3 units. The fair comparison is weekly load. Roughly (assumption: about 10-12 study hours per unit per week including class time):

- Double at 4 units: highest weekly load, the worst case for FSAE and WAM.
- Double at 3 units: about 25% lighter, but 6.7 years.
- Single at 4 units: the same weekly load as the standard double, 1 year shorter.
- Single at 3 units: lightest per semester, ~5.3 years.

So the real choice is how many hours a week you want to give FSAE, and then which degree length you accept. Your option of a double at 3 units buys FSAE time at the price of about 1.5 extra years. A single degree at 4 units is the cheapest in years, and FSAE can still be done, but expect build and competition crunches to collide with exams [Assumption]. Ask Monash Motorsport directly how many hours a week members really commit.

**4. WAM.** A heavier concurrent load usually lowers marks, and some graduate programs filter on grades. For motorsport, FSAE experience and a portfolio often count for more than a small WAM difference [Assumption].

**5. Cost and HECS-HELP (correcting my earlier claim).**
- In a Commonwealth-supported place, the student contribution is charged per unit, and HECS-HELP lets you defer it per unit enrolled [Recall]. Spreading the same units over more semesters therefore does **not** add a year of tuition. Only the Commerce half's extra units add tuition. Check the current per-unit band amounts and indexation in the Monash fee pages.
- The real cost of spreading is a later start to full-time graduate pay and more years of living costs, and your HELP debt is indexed while it stays unpaid [Recall]. Recent changes to indexation and repayment thresholds have been announced [Recall]; check the current rules at studyassist.gov.au.
- Load matters for other things, not HECS-HELP itself: Centrelink payments, visa conditions and some scholarships can require full-time load [Recall]. Check these if they apply to you.
- HECS-HELP does not apply to overseas study, and overseas fees would be paid up front or by private loan [Recall].

**6. Finance later.** Engineering alone does not close the door. Finance graduate programs and a postgraduate qualification (for example a Master of Finance) accept engineers [Assumption]. Check with Monash Careers. A finance minor or electives give you vocabulary and a way to test whether you like finance.

**7. Switching later.** Starting with the single degree and adding Commerce later is possible only if Monash allows a transfer or course change [Assumption]. Check the process and the deadlines. If it is easy, the single degree keeps the option open at low cost.

## Skills map: how each subject area feeds a motorsport task

I cannot confirm Monash subject codes without the Handbook, so below I give topic areas with what they do for a race team, plus search terms. First-year codes I recall (check them): **ENG1005** Engineering mathematics, **ENG1060** Computing for engineers (MATLAB-style programming), **ENG1090** Foundation mathematics for engineering (a bridging unit for students without Specialist) [Recall, moderate confidence].

| Topic area (search the Handbook for these titles) | How it contributes to motorsport | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering mathematics (calculus, linear algebra, differential equations) | Differential equations describe suspension and damper motion. Linear algebra underlies kinematics and multi-body models. | Direct |
| Numerical methods and computing | Turn equations into code: lap-time simulation, tyre-model fitting, step-by-step solvers for vehicle motion. | Direct |
| Statics and mechanics of materials | Load paths and stress in wishbones, uprights and chassis tubes, so you can size parts and avoid failure. | Direct |
| Dynamics and vibrations | Weight transfer, ride and roll behaviour, damper tuning, and natural frequencies. The core of vehicle dynamics. | Direct |
| Engineering design and CAD (parametric modelling, assemblies, drawings, tolerancing, DFM/DFA) | Drawings and tolerances are how a part is made and fits. Parametric models let you iterate a suspension geometry quickly. | Direct |
| FEA / computational mechanics | Check stiffness and stress before manufacturing, and trade weight against strength. | Direct |
| Fluid mechanics and thermodynamics | Aerodynamic force and drag, cooling, and engine or powertrain efficiency. | Direct |
| Control and signals | Traction and stability control, sensor filtering, and damping logic. | Direct |
| Manufacturing, machining and composites | Making what you design. FSAE workshop time matters most here; units help with process knowledge. | Direct, mostly via FSAE |
| Optimisation and statistics / data analysis | Choose geometry or gear ratios against lap time, and interpret data-acquisition logs. | Direct |
| Finance electives (corporate finance, financial markets) | Costing and budgeting, and the fallback career. Not a motorsport skill. | Transferable |
| Watching F1 | No engineering skill. | Interest only |

When you read a unit guide, ask one question: does this give me a tool I would use on a suspension, a chassis, a data log or a lap-time model?

## Entry requirements and adjustment (all [Recall], must be checked)

- **Prerequisites.** Engineering courses at Monash and the other Victorian universities generally prescribe VCE Mathematical Methods. Physics is commonly listed as prescribed or strongly recommended, and Specialist Mathematics is commonly recommended but not prescribed [Recall]. You have Methods and Physics, so you probably meet the prerequisites. Confirm in the VTAC course guide for each course.
- **Specialist gap.** You are not doing Specialist. Its content (complex numbers, vectors, more calculus) is taught again in first-year engineering mathematics, and Monash offers a bridging unit (see ENG1090 above) [Recall]. Treat this as a real extra effort in first year, not a barrier.
- **Adjustment factors.** Many Victorian universities add study-score increments for named subjects in named courses, and run access or equity schemes (for example the VTAC Special Entry Access Scheme, and each university's own schemes) [Recall]. I do not know whether Methods, Physics or Economics earn an increment in your target courses. Product Design and Technologies is unlikely to [Assumption]. Look up "adjustment factors" or "increments" for each course on the university site, and check the equity schemes you may qualify for.
- **Competitiveness (qualitative, [Recall]).** Monash Engineering and Melbourne are the most selective of the Victorian options. Swinburne, RMIT and Deakin generally have lower selection ranks. Interstate courses vary. I give no numbers. Compare each course with the latest published selection rank on the VTAC site once your ATAR is released.

2. **F1 realism.** Few F1 roles exist in Australia. Most are in the UK, with visa and right-to-work limits. Realistic pathways include Australian motorsport, automotive, aerospace and defence. Plan for those too [Assumption].
3. **The finance fallback is untested.** You have not tried finance study yet.
